The year was 1992. Arataki Kotaro was twenty when the Boryokudan Countermeasures Act, as it was commonly known, was passed.

Until then, the Arataki Group had waved the banner of honor and chivalry and preached that they were a necessary evil, all while raking in money behind that respectable front. Now they were plunged into a dark age.

The Boryokudan Countermeasures Act was an unreasonable law, the kind that had no place in a nation under the rule of law.

Its contents were insane, practically announcing, Get on the police's bad side and we can crush yakuza like you whenever we want, got it? A light threat, a little kidnapping, or some petty drug dealing was enough to get a yakuza thrown in prison.

Kotaro's father, Arataki Iwao, the first boss of the Arataki Group, had shot dead two executives of an enemy group in a nightlife district as payback after another group killed his young men. Just for that, he got real prison time. And that was even though he had mercifully taken care not to harm civilians and made sure only gangsters died.

With his father behind bars, Arataki Kotaro took over as the second-generation boss of the Arataki Group, rode the tide of the times skillfully, and branched out into all kinds of rackets.

They ran host clubs and sex businesses. Phone scams. They sweet-talked stupid kids into working as runners for robberies, and they put middlemen between themselves and their drug sales so police investigations would never reach the Arataki Group.

The Arataki Group had once had its roots sunk deep in big local companies and lived the easy life, but thanks to the Boryokudan Countermeasures Act, those days were over. Even so, they got by well enough by working every petty racket they could.

Now and then they still made a big score, but the organization shrank year after year. Rival groups, too, faded away on their own or announced they were disbanding and vanished.

For all Kotaro's efforts, the Arataki Group kept shrinking, as if it were slowly being strangled with silk floss.

Even when the era name changed to Reiwa and the public got excited about a new age, the Arataki Group was still stuck in its dark age.

Whenever he thought of his father's bitter regret, anger welled up in him. The first boss had died in prison without ever achieving his dream of unifying the whole country under the Arataki Group.

Then, in the middle of all that, the Gremlin Disaster happened and turned the world upside down.

Incredibly, Kotaro's younger brother, Arataki Kojiro, and Kojiro's mistress, Kiwada Yoko, had the tremendous luck to awaken as a mage and a witch.

This was it.

Kotaro was certain the time had come to vent the frustration he had bottled up for so many years.

His father's dream, his own dream, the Arataki Group's dream. The time had come to set out to conquer the whole country, the dream every yakuza in history had dreamed and no one had ever achieved.

The time had come for the Arataki Group, scorned as society's garbage for more than thirty long years, to show the world just how terrifying they were.

The time had come to show those well-behaved, stuck-up goody-goodies who had looked down on the Arataki Group every chance they got what real power was!!

Kotaro immediately named his younger brother Kojiro the third-generation boss of the Arataki Group and took the post of adviser for himself.

When Kojiro became a mage, his already strong body grew even tougher, and he began to give off something like charisma. Kojiro had come along late in their father's life, so he was in his early forties, still young compared to Kotaro. He wasn't slow on the uptake, either. As the third-generation boss to lead the group, he left nothing to be desired.

If there was one problem, it was that becoming a mage had taught him a useless sense of mercy.

Their father had raised Kojiro to be a ruthless, merciless fighter, but the mutation had awakened him to an incomprehensible, honor-code-like way of thinking. It was as if he truly believed in honor instead of just using it as a front. It even had something in common with chivalry.

As the Arataki Group's third-generation boss, he laid down a policy of treating those who surrendered with mercy.

No matter who the enemy was, if they surrendered quietly and swore loyalty to the Arataki Group, they would never be killed or bound with contract magic.

Kotaro was irritated by principles that lukewarm, but he somehow choked them down, telling himself he could just help his brother out as adviser.

If it had been up to Kotaro, he would have cast contract magic on every enemy and ally without exception and bound them all so none of them could ever defy him. After all, betrayal and defection were everyday occurrences for yakuza.

Not that the new gang boss had gone completely soft from peace, either. He declared that anyone who betrayed them after being shown mercy would pay with their life, so, well, Kotaro could just barely give him credit on that point.

Kotaro drew up the blueprints as adviser, and Kojiro, as gang boss, turned them into reality.

Working together, the brothers brought Fukuoka to heel in the blink of an eye and, in the middle of the great disaster, built a huge survivor community with a population of some four hundred thousand.

Of course, the gang boss and his mistress had beaten witches and mages into submission with perfectly coordinated violence and brought them under the Arataki Group's umbrella, but not all of them had been loyal to the gang boss from the start.

Many only obeyed because they had been forced into contracts, and wore looks that said, Just you wait.

Kotaro, as adviser, took his time working on people like that until they came around.

He tortured them and played good cop, bad cop to shake them up.

He took hostages, or had them commit petty wrongs they wouldn't balk at much and dragged them in deeper and deeper. Sharing meals with them worked too, and so did winning over their families first and letting the families talk the real target around.

Once someone had settled in, the rest was easy.

The raid and takeover of the Lake Biwa Pact in particular, with its fierce fighting, left the Arataki Group more united and loyal.

It cost them one member, but in exchange, a witch who had been one of the Lake Biwa Pact's hawks and had helped the Arataki Group get in joined them. It came out even, and the Arataki Group was on a roll.

When things go right, everything goes right. The Arataki Group secured both actual stock of the magic drug the Lake Biwa Pact's doves had been hiding and the method for making it, and grew stronger still.

The magic drug was made by refining the sap of a creepy monster, a tree with a face (called a <ruby>Treant<rt>tree elder</rt></ruby>). Simply drinking it could restore and boost someone's magic power beyond their normal capacity.

Thanks to it, the Arataki Group's underlings could get enough magic power to cast spells and became real fighting strength. Well, even as fighters, they didn't amount to much next to witches and mages.

The magic drug had its drawbacks too, so it was decided that the Arataki Group's leadership would not use it themselves. Instead, they would pass it down to the underlings or use it as a card to play when winning people over.

The magic drug made it easy to increase your magic power; all you had to do was drink it. But the effect was temporary, and the extra magic power disappeared once it was used. On top of that, every dose lowered the user's maximum magic power. Some idiot underlings overdid the stuff and turned to dust and vanished.

Just as tobacco and alcohol were more addictive the younger the user was, this magic drug's addictiveness followed a set rule: the less magic-power capacity someone had, the stronger the sense of omnipotence when they drank it, and the more addictive and habit-forming it became.

Witches and mages didn't get hooked at all. Magic-power doping that hit ordinary people hard barely gave Transcendents a boost. Since it also lowered their maximum magic power, you could even call it a net loss.

But for small fry who didn't even have enough magic power to cast fertility magic, it was as good as a narcotic, a powerful drug they couldn't let go of after using it once.

With this new weapon in hand, the Arataki Group used contract magic and the magic drug to take control of the Lake Biwa Pact's core, and once the core couldn't defy them, they rode the momentum straight up to Tokyo.

Thanks to the magic stones they had taken from the Lake Biwa Pact, every member of the Arataki Group now had a magic stone. It was like giving wings to a tiger. There was no reason to stop the invasion.

The gang boss started saying something spineless about how they owed Tokyo for fertility magic, but when Kotaro said those people had brought in mushroom disease, so they owed them nothing, he accepted that.

The Tokyo Witches' Council, which ruled Tokyo, the capital of the old era, was the largest survivor community in Japan.

Its total population was two to four million, with fifteen to thirty witches and mages. The information they had squeezed out of the Dragon Witch was pretty unreliable, but there was no doubt that, along with that Dragon Witch, the Foresight Mage and the Blue Witch were dangerous. If they took down those three, the Tokyo Witches' Council would fall.

The Witches' Council had little unity. If they crushed its head and either defeated or absorbed the standout fighters who could turn the tables in one stroke, all that would be left was picking off the rest one by one.

The Arataki Group had ten witches and mages combined. Even at a high estimate, the Tokyo Witches' Council had thirty.

But the ten were united as one, while the thirty would be a disorderly rabble once a few people at their core were crushed.

They could win.

That was how Kotaro read it.

Kotaro had drawn up the plan that brought down the Lake Biwa Pact with few losses, but the Foresight Mage, just as the name suggested, was a tough nut to crack.

He tried to approach the Setagaya Witch, who looked like the easiest to turn, to make her an informant, but every attempt fell flat. These weren't chance failures. They came from obvious interference.

He tried to rot them from within with the magic drug, but that didn't work either.

Tokyo had an underworld too, with black marketeers who dealt in contraband. But they were thoroughly scared of the Foresight Mage. They did accept the actual magic drug through middlemen, but then they just pocketed it, and none of it ever made it out onto the market.

He gave up on making an informant and spreading the magic drug and had the witch best at stealth try to steal Cyanos, the Blue Witch's famed strongest magic wand. Even that failed.

There shouldn't have been the slightest sign of a theft coming, yet the Blue Witch suddenly went openly on guard, and they couldn't lay a hand on the wand.

However united the Arataki Group was, with their plans read this far ahead and the enemy's greatest fighter on her guard, they had no way to act.

But there was an opening.

Kotaro, who had been pitting schemes against the Foresight Mage behind the scenes, noticed that the Foresight Mage had limits.

Their operations got disrupted, and he also had the sense that the Foresight Mage wanted to drive the Arataki Group away. But the Foresight Mage apparently didn't know how many the Arataki Group numbered in total, and not every scheme they set in motion was being painstakingly crushed.

The Foresight Mage's ability to respond had its limits.

So Kotaro went to his brother, the gang boss, and proposed a multifront attack to overload the Foresight Mage's ability to respond.

Given the enemy's nature, spreading their numbers out to some degree would work better than having all ten attack as one.

Kotaro's plan hit the mark.

About half of it seemed to have been read, but a red smoke signal rose from the Bunkyo Ward Office to announce a witch's defeat, and red smoke rose from Magic University, signaling its takeover and the securing of hostages. A red smoke signal was visible over Chofu as well.

The Arataki Group's invasion of Tokyo had gotten off to the best start they could realistically have hoped for.

But after that, things went badly.

The black smoke signal confirming Foresight's death just wouldn't go up.

No smoke went up to announce that the Blue Witch and Dragon Witch had been neutralized, either, and evening came on, with night drawing near.

Kotaro was the Arataki Group's adviser, and not much of a fighter.

He could at least fire the “<ruby>![gaiji-0002.png](images/gaiji-0002.png)-<rt>Fire</rt></ruby>” spell thirty times, enough to steamroll ordinary people, but if he got caught up in a battle between Transcendents, he would die instantly.

That was why Kotaro had secretly taken up position with his underlings on the top floor of a high-rise apartment building in safe Koto Ward, watching the whole battle unfold through a telescope.

Just as the sun was about to set, the battle, which had been deadlocked since the opening strike, finally shifted.

And it shifted against the Arataki Group.

A dragon came in from the mountains and landed somewhere around Bunkyo Ward. No sooner had it taken off again than it flew to the Flower Witch's territory, where a gigantic white vortex suddenly appeared in the sky, fell to the ground, and turned the streets white.

That wasn't all. The dragon slipped between the buildings at high speed and made the same gigantic white vortex appear several wards away as well, freezing buildings solid with its swirling cold.

Kotaro clenched his teeth hard enough to crack them, tossed the telescope aside, and began hurrying down from the high-rise apartment building with his underlings.

The Arataki Group had lost.

If the dragon was flying free, then Kiwada, one of their greatest fighters and the best match against the Dragon Witch, had been killed. That told him one was down.

That powerful cold magic was the Blue Witch's. No smoke signal had gone up to warn of the danger before the Blue Witch started rampaging, which meant Kurihara, her watcher, had been killed. That made two.

The hostages clearly weren't working, either, so the gang boss, the Young Boss, and the Junior Boss, who should have taken over Magic University, had been killed. That made five.

Given the time, the three who had taken over the ward office should already have joined up with the others at Magic University. But the dragon that landed in Bunkyo Ward hadn't been intercepted at all. The Blue Witch had been firing powerful magic, a perfect target for magic-rampage spells, yet nothing had happened. That meant the three witches assigned to the ward office had almost certainly all been killed too. That made eight.

Ikaruga had been attacking the Flower Witch's administrative district. If magic like a natural disaster had rained down on it from above, Ikaruga, an attrition-type fighter who had probably worn their magic power thin, would not have been able to block it and had likely died. That made nine.

Shishido had successfully defeated the Chofu Witch and was hauling her off. Judging by how things had played out, it was safe to assume Shishido had been killed on the way by the Blue Witch's last spell.

That made ten.

Every one of the Arataki Group's witches and mages had been killed.

Arataki Kotaro ran.

He ran for his life.

He ran and ran, afraid that at any moment the dragon would come flying overhead and a white vortex of despair would pour down on him.

He ran north. He couldn't run toward their home base in the south.

If the gang boss was dead, everyone who had been forced into contracts was free now.

Even if he somehow made it there, the people who hated them to the bone would mob him and beat him to death.

If he was going to run, north was the only way.

After running all through the night, Kotaro put his underlings on watch and fell into a shallow sleep in the back room of a shop at a parking area along the national highway.

When he woke up, he was alone.

Every one of his underlings had run off.

Kotaro was dumbfounded.

He staggered out of the shop and stood all alone on the ruined national highway.

Tree branches poked out between the abandoned vehicles lined up there, their bodies rusted from exposure to wind and rain.

Small birds were chirping, and at the foot of the mountains along the highway, a deer nibbling young shoots was staring right at Kotaro.

Anger surged up in him for no good reason, and Kotaro fired shooting magic at the deer.

But the magic missed. The deer was a little startled and backed away, but after a moment, it went back to leisurely eating young shoots. It wasn't particularly scared.

“You look down on me! You look down on me, on the Arataki Group! You look down on us, you look down on us, aaahhh!! <ruby>![gaiji-0002.png](images/gaiji-0002.png)—! ![gaiji-0002.png](images/gaiji-0002.png)![gaiji-0002.png](images/gaiji-0002.png)![gaiji-0002.png](images/gaiji-0002.png)![gaiji-0002.png](images/gaiji-0002.png)![gaiji-0002.png](images/gaiji-0002.png)![gaiji-0002.png](images/gaiji-0002.png)—!!<rt>Fire! Fiiiiiire!!</rt></ruby>”

Half out of his mind, Kotaro sprayed magic wildly.

When he came back to his senses, the deer was gone.

Panting, a vein bulging on his forehead, Kotaro took a deep breath and calmed himself down.

Then he thought, because thinking was his weapon. A first-rate weapon that had given even a mage who could see the future a hard time.

After mulling it over for a while, Kotaro decided to take over the Tohoku Hunting Association.

It would probably mean lying low again. But if he could worm his way into the good graces of the Tohoku Hunting Association's witches and mages, rebuilding the Arataki Group wasn't just a dream.

The Arataki Group had endured a long dark age.

They would endure this time too. As long as Arataki Kotaro was alive, the Arataki Group would never end.

Kotaro was headed for Sendai, where the Tohoku Hunting Association was rumored to be, walking in silence and gnashing his teeth as he swore revenge on the Tokyo Witches' Council, when he met a group coming the other way, pulling a cart.

There were about twenty of them, all with hunting rifles slung on their backs.

When the distance between them had closed to about thirty paces, the group stopped and called out to him warily.

“Just to make sure, you're not a monster, right!? Sorry, but could you say something for us!”

“Yeah! I'm not a monster!”

Kotaro plastered on a friendly expression and answered with both hands raised, and relief spread through the group.

Their guard dropped all at once. They halted the horse pulling their fully loaded cart and came up to Kotaro. A good-natured-looking bearded man who seemed to be their representative asked him with open curiosity.

“What happened to you, out here all alone? Even a wizard's in danger by himself. You're not a mage... are you?”

“No, I'm not. So it really is unusual, walking around a place like this alone?”

“Sure is. Tokyo's witches and our hunters do regular culling, but monsters still aren't rare. My outfit's got a good wizard guarding us, but if a Class A shows up, even running's tough. And then there's you, walking along all by yourself, so I thought you were a humanoid monster.”

The bearded man introduced himself as a trader and asked why Kotaro had been walking alone.

Kotaro thought fast, then answered.

“Truth is, I screwed up big with a business in Tokyo. You know the new currency they issued, right? I made a huge blunder over that. My wife and kids walked out on me, I'm flat broke, and Tokyo's gotten hard to stick around in. So I figured maybe I could start over on the Tohoku Hunting Association's home turf. You're right, walking alone was careless of me. I guess the shock of my family dumping me messed with my head.”

The lie rolled right off his tongue, and the merchant looked sympathetic.

“That's rough. Sorry, but we've got to haul this load to Tokyo. We can't take you along to Sendai, but...”

“No, no, that's fine. Put your job first. I'd feel bad making you spend time on a middle-aged guy like me. But you'll head back to Sendai after you unload in Tokyo, right? Mind if I tag along on your way back?”

“Oh, that'd be no problem at all. What's your name?”

“Kiwada.”

The fake name that popped out on the spot belonged to his younger brother's mistress. It galled him to hide the Arataki name, but this was no time to be picky.

“Kiwada-san, want to come back to Tokyo with us for now?”

“Ah, no. It'd be awkward if I ran into someone I know. I'll hide out at a parking area nearby and wait for you to come back. I've made up my mind that the next time I go back to Tokyo, it'll be as an official supplier to the Tohoku Hunting Association.”

“Ha ha, that's big talk!”

The bearded man laughed heartily, and Kotaro played along with a bright laugh of his own.

It looked like they were about to part ways for now, but then a man in a hood raised his hand and joined the conversation.

“Sorry, but I'm a little worried about leaving Kiwada-san on his own like this. Whatever his reasons, walking a trade route alone is way too dangerous. I'd like to at least teach him the simple things he needs to protect himself.”

“Oh, you're right. We should do that. Kiwada-san, he's our caravan's guard. He's a skilled wizard, so I think you'll find what he has to say useful.”

“Would that be all right? I'd like to teach you some simple magic for self-defense and the right frame of mind.”

“Oh, I couldn't ask for anything better. Would you?”

“Then let's go over there. It would be dangerous if I fired magic here and hit someone.”

The hooded wizard led Kotaro into a grove beside the national highway, a short way from the caravan.

Kotaro smirked to himself. It never hurt to know plenty of spells that even ordinary humans could use. His greatest ability was planning operations, but he had a pretty large amount of magic power too. Adding another card to his hand in a place like this meant his luck was turning.

Just like they say, once you hit rock bottom, the only way left is up, huh?

The hooded wizard, who had been warily scanning the grove, nodded once and spoke to Kotaro in a flat voice.

“You smell like a vicious beast. Sorry, but I can't let you get near <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby>.”

“<ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby>? What are you—”

Before he could finish, two hands seized Kotaro's neck and twisted it 360 degrees.

The hooded wizard looked down at the fallen corpse with cold eyes, then kicked it over and shoved it into a thicket of shrubs.

After that, the hooded wizard leaned against a tree trunk to kill time, waiting long enough not to arouse suspicion before he went back to the caravan.

“Welcome back, Murakumo-san. What about him?”

“Ah, it seems Kiwada-san decided to go on alone after all instead of waiting for us. He was gone before I could stop him.”

“Huh. Jeez, I'd think that's dangerous. Well, he did seem a little off, so maybe he's got something urgent going on.”

“I suppose so. He must have had his own reasons. But we have our own reasons too. Come on, let's get going. We have to deliver the trade goods to Tokyo before the day's out.”

“Right. Break's over! Let's hurry into the Witches' Council's territory and catch our breath!”

And the caravan set off for Tokyo without incident.
