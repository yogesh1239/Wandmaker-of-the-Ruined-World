It was 1992. When Arataki Kotaro was twenty, the Boryokudan Countermeasures Act was passed. It was commonly called the Boryokudan Countermeasures Act.

Until then, the Arataki Group had held up honor and chivalry, preached that they were a necessary evil, and made huge money behind that public front. But they entered a dark age.

The Boryokudan Countermeasures Act was an unreasonable law that had no place in a nation ruled by law.

Its outrageous contents all but said, Piss off the police and they can crush yakuza whenever they want. Even for a little threat, a small kidnapping, or minor drug sales, yakuza got thrown in prison.

Arataki Iwao, Kotaro's father and the first boss of the Arataki Group, shot and killed two executives from an enemy group in a nightlife district to get back at them for killing his men. But just for that, he got a prison sentence. Even though, mercifully, he had taken care not to harm civilians and kept the dead limited to other gangsters.

After taking over from his arrested father and becoming the second-generation boss of the Arataki Group, Arataki Kotaro rode the tide of the times and expanded into all kinds of rackets.

They ran host clubs and sex businesses. Phone scams. They talked stupid kids into being runners for robberies, and put intermediaries between themselves and drug sales so the police investigation would not reach the Arataki Group.

The Arataki Group had once put down roots in big local companies and had it easy, but the Boryokudan Countermeasures Act meant they could not do that anymore. Even so, they scraped by with every petty racket they could come up with.

Sometimes they could still make a killing, but the organization shrank year by year. Rival groups, too, faded away on their own or disappeared after announcing they were disbanding.

Despite Kotaro's efforts, the Arataki Group kept getting smaller, as if it were slowly being strangled with silk floss.

The era name changed to Reiwa, and people celebrated a new age, but the Arataki Group was still in its dark age.

When he thought of his father, the first boss, dying in prison with the bitter regret of never fulfilling his dream of unifying the country under the Arataki Group, anger welled up in him.

Then the Gremlin Disaster happened, changing the world completely.

His younger brother Arataki Kojiro and Kojiro's mistress, Kiwada Yoko, were blessed with the incredible luck of awakening as a mage and a witch.

This was it.

Kotaro was sure the time had come to vent the frustrations he had carried for so long.

His father's dream, his dream, the Arataki Group's dream. The time had come to set out for nationwide domination, the dream every yakuza had dreamed but no one had ever achieved.

The time had come for the Arataki Group, despised as society's trash for more than thirty long years, to show the world how scary they were.

The time had come to show those well-behaved, stuck-up goodie-goodies who had looked down their noses at the Arataki Group what real power was!!

Kotaro immediately named his brother Kojiro the third-generation boss of the Arataki Group and took the position of adviser himself.

When Kojiro became a mage, his already strong body became even tougher, and he started giving off something like charisma. Kojiro had been born when their father was old, so he was in his early forties and still much younger than Kotaro. His mind was not that bad, either. He was more than fit to lead the group as its third-generation boss.

If there was one problem, it was that becoming a mage had made him develop unnecessary compassion.

Their father had taught his younger brother to be a ruthless fighter, but the mutation had awakened in him an incomprehensible, code-of-honor-like ideology. It was as if he really believed in honor, rather than just using it as a front. It even somewhat resembled chivalry.

The third-generation boss set a policy of treating anyone who surrendered with mercy.

No matter what kind of enemy they were, if they surrendered honestly and swore loyalty to the Arataki Group, they would never be killed or bound with contract magic.

Kotaro was irritated by rules that soft, but he somehow swallowed it by telling himself he could help Kojiro as his adviser.

If it were Kotaro, he would cast contract magic on every last enemy and ally, binding them so none could ever defy him. Betrayal and defection were everyday things for yakuza.

The new gang boss was not completely soft, either. He declared that anyone who betrayed them despite being shown mercy would be punished with death, so Kotaro could barely give him credit on that point.

As the adviser, Kotaro drew up the plans, and as gang boss, Kojiro made those plans real.

The brothers worked together and brought Fukuoka under their control in the blink of an eye. In the middle of the great disaster, they built a huge survivor community with a population of four hundred thousand.

Of course, not every witch and mage who had been forced into submission by the coordinated violence of the gang boss and his mistress and made part of the Arataki Group had been loyal to the gang boss from the start.

Many were only unable to defy them because they had been forced into contracts, and wore expressions that said, Just you wait.

Kotaro, the adviser, carefully worked on winning people like that over.

He tortured them and shook them up with good-cop, bad-cop tactics.

Taking hostages. Getting them to commit small crimes they wouldn't balk at, then dragging them in deeper. Sharing meals with them. Winning over their families first and using them to win over the real target. Those worked too.

Once someone got used to it, the rest was easy.

The assault and takeover of the Lake Biwa Pact in particular increased the Arataki Group's unity and loyalty after fierce fighting.

They had one casualty, but in exchange, one of the Lake Biwa Pact's hawks, a witch who had guided the Arataki Group, joined them. It evened out, and the Arataki Group gained momentum.

When things were going well, everything went well. They secured both the actual magic drug the Lake Biwa Pact's doves had hidden and the method to make it, and the Arataki Group grew even stronger.

The magic drug was made by refining the sap of a creepy tree monster with a face, called a Treant. Just drinking it restored and boosted magic power beyond the user's magic-power capacity.

That gave the Arataki Group's underlings enough magic power to cast spells and turned them into fighting strength. Well, even then, they were nothing compared to witches and mages.

Magic drug had drawbacks too, so the Arataki Group leadership decided not to use it. They would pass it down to underlings or use it as a tool for winning people over.

Magic drug made it easy to increase magic power just by drinking it. But that was temporary, and the increased magic power disappeared when used. On top of that, each dose reduced the user's maximum magic power. Some idiot underlings overdid the drugs and turned to dust and vanished.

Just as tobacco and alcohol were more addictive the younger someone was, the magic drug's addictiveness followed a set rule. The less magic-power capacity someone had, the stronger the feeling of omnipotence when they drank it, and the more addictive and habit-forming it became.

Witches and mages did not get hooked at all. What was intense magic-power doping to normal people was not much of a boost to Transcendents. You could even call it a bad bargain, since it reduced their maximum magic power.

But to trash who did not even have enough magic power to cast fertility magic, it was equal to narcotics. It was a powerful drug they could not give up after using it once.

With their new weapon in hand, the Arataki Group took control of the core of the Lake Biwa Pact using contract magic and magic drug. After making them unable to defy them, they rode that momentum straight to Tokyo.

With the magic stones they had taken from the Lake Biwa Pact, every member of the Arataki Group now had a magic stone. It was like giving wings to a tiger. There was no reason to stop the invasion.

The gang boss started saying something soft about how they owed Tokyo for fertility magic, but when Kotaro said those people had brought in mushroom disease, so they did not owe them anything, he accepted that.

The Tokyo Witches' Council, which ruled Tokyo, the capital of the old era, was the largest survivor community in Japan.

Its total population was two to four million. There were fifteen to thirty witches and mages. The information they had pulled from the Dragon Witch was not very reliable, but there was no doubt that, along with that Dragon Witch, the Foresight Mage and Blue Witch were dangerous. If they took down those three, the Tokyo Witches' Council would fall.

The Witches' Council did not have much unity. If they crushed its leaders and defeated or brought in the key fighters who could turn things around in one shot, they could pick off the rest one by one.

The Arataki Group had ten witches and mages combined. Even at a high estimate, the Tokyo Witches' Council had thirty.

But the side with ten was united, while the side with thirty would be a disorganized mob once they crushed a few people at its core.

They could win.

Kotaro figured so.

Kotaro had drawn up a plan that brought down the Lake Biwa Pact with few casualties, but the Foresight Mage was not easy to deal with, just as the name suggested.

He tried to contact the Setagaya Witch, who seemed like the easiest person to win over, to make an informant, but nothing worked. This was not mere bad luck. Someone was clearly interfering.

Even when he tried to rot them from within using magic drug, that failed too.

Tokyo had an underworld too, with black marketeers who dealt in contraband. But they were completely scared of the Foresight Mage. They accepted the actual magic drug through middlemen, but just pocketed it, and none of it reached the public.

He gave up on making an informant and spreading magic drug, then tried to make the witch best at stealth steal Cyanos, the Blue Witch's renowned strongest magic wand. Even that failed.

She should not have shown any signs that she was about to steal it, but the Blue Witch suddenly went openly on guard, and they could not make a move.

No matter how united the Arataki Group was, if their plans were being read this far ahead and the enemy's strongest fighter was on guard, they could not act.

But there was an opening.

As Kotaro traded covert schemes with the Foresight Mage, he noticed the Foresight Mage's limits.

Kotaro's plans kept getting disrupted. He also got the sense that the Foresight Mage wanted to drive the Arataki Group away. But apparently, the Foresight Mage did not know the Arataki Group's total numbers, and not every plot they put in motion was being carefully crushed.

The Foresight Mage's ability to respond had limits.

So Kotaro advised his brother, the gang boss, and proposed a multifront attack that would saturate the Foresight Mage's ability to respond.

Rather than having all ten attack as one, the enemy's nature suggested it would work better to spread their numbers out to some extent.

Kotaro's plan worked.

It seemed about half their plans had been read, but a red signal flare rose from Bunkyo Ward Office to announce a witch's defeat, and red smoke rose from Magic University to announce its takeover and the securing of hostages. A red signal flare could be seen from Chofu as well.

The Arataki Group's invasion of Tokyo had gotten the best start they could realistically hope for.

But after that, things did not go well.

The black signal flare announcing that Foresight was confirmed dead still would not go up.

No smoke went up to announce that the Blue Witch and Dragon Witch had been neutralized, either. Evening came, and night drew close.

Kotaro was the Arataki Group's adviser, with low combat ability.

He could at least fire thirty shots of the “<ruby>![gaiji-0002.png](images/gaiji-0002.png)-<rt>Fire</rt></ruby>” spell, which would let him steamroll normal people, but if he got caught up in a battle between Transcendents, he would die instantly.

That was why Kotaro had secretly set up with his underlings on the top floor of a safe high-rise apartment building in Koto Ward, watching the battle unfold through binoculars.

Just when the sun was about to set, the battle, which had been deadlocked since the first strike, changed.

And it changed for the worse for the Arataki Group.

A dragon coming from the mountains landed around Bunkyo Ward. It seemed to take off again right away, then flew to the Flower Witch's territory. A huge white vortex suddenly appeared overhead, fell to the ground, and stained the streets white.

That was not all. The dragon flew at high speed through the buildings, and in places several wards away, huge white vortices appeared the same way, freezing buildings in their swirls of cold air.

Kotaro clenched his teeth hard enough to crush them, tossed his binoculars aside, and hurried down from the high-rise apartment building with his underlings.

The Arataki Group had lost.

If the dragon was flying freely, Kiwada, one of their strongest fighters and the best match for the Dragon Witch, had been killed. That told him one was down.

The powerful cold magic belonged to the Blue Witch. No signal flare had gone up to warn them before the Blue Witch started rampaging, which meant Kurihara, who was watching her, had been killed. That made two.

The hostages were clearly not working either, so the gang boss, Young Boss, and Junior Boss, who had supposedly taken over Magic University, had been killed. That made five.

By this point, the three who had taken over the ward office should already have joined up at Magic University. But no one had intercepted the dragon that landed in Bunkyo Ward. The Blue Witch had been firing powerful magic, a perfect target for magic-rampage spells, but there was nothing. That meant the three witches assigned to the ward office had almost certainly all been killed too. That made eight.

Ikaruga had attacked the Flower Witch's administrative district. If magic like a natural disaster poured down from the sky there, Ikaruga, an attrition-fighting type who had probably worn down their magic power, would not have been able to stop it and had likely died. That made nine.

Shishido had successfully defeated the Chofu Witch and was taking her away. Given the course of events, there was no doubt Shishido too had been killed by the Blue Witch's last spell along the way.

That made ten.

Every Arataki Group witch and mage had been killed.

Arataki Kotaro ran.

He ran desperately.

Fearing that a dragon would fly overhead at any moment and a white vortex of despair would pour down on him, he kept running and running.

He ran north. He could not run toward the southern headquarters.

If the gang boss was dead, everyone who had been forced into contracts was free.

Even if he somehow made it there, the people who hated the Arataki Group to the bone would beat him to death in a mob.

If he was going to run, north was his only choice.

After running through the night without stopping to escape, Kotaro put his underlings on watch and fell into a shallow sleep in the storeroom of a parking-area shop along the national highway.

When he woke up, he was alone.

Every underling had run away.

Kotaro stood stunned.

He staggered out of the shop and stood all alone on the ruined national highway.

Tree branches poked out from between a line of abandoned vehicles, rusted after being exposed to wind and rain.

He heard small birds chirping. At the edge of the mountains along the highway, a deer eating young shoots stared at Kotaro.

Anger welled up in him for no reason, and Kotaro fired shooting magic at the deer.

But the magic missed. The deer was startled and stepped back, but after a short pause, it went back to calmly eating young shoots. It was not very scared.

“You look down on me! You look down on me, on the Arataki Group! Half mad, Kotaro wildly fired magic in every direction.

When he came back to himself, the deer was gone.

With a vein bulging in his forehead and his breath coming hard, Kotaro took a deep breath and calmed himself.

Then he thought. Thinking was his weapon. A first-rate weapon that had even troubled the Foresight Mage.

After thinking it over for a while, Kotaro decided to take over the Tohoku Hunting Association.

The time to lie low would come again. But if he could worm his way into the good graces of the Tohoku Hunting Association's witches and mages, rebuilding the Arataki Group would not be a dream.

The Arataki Group had endured a long dark age.

This time too, they would endure it. As long as Arataki Kotaro was alive, the Arataki Group would not end.

Heading for the Tohoku Hunting Association rumored to be in Sendai, Kotaro silently walked along with his teeth clenched, swearing revenge against the Tokyo Witches' Council, when he met a group pulling a cart toward him.

There were around twenty people in the group, and all of them carried hunting rifles on their backs.

When they were about thirty steps apart, the group stopped and called out to him warily.

“Just to make sure, you aren't a monster, right!? Sorry, could you say something for me!”

“Yeah! I'm not a monster!”

Kotaro plastered on a friendly expression and answered with both hands raised. A relieved mood spread through the group.

The group relaxed all at once. They stopped the horse pulling their cargo-filled cart and came closer to Kotaro. A good-natured-looking bearded man who seemed to be their representative asked him with open curiosity.

“What're you doing out here alone? Even a wizard's in danger by himself. You're not a mage... are you?”

“No, I'm not. Is it really that unusual to be walking around a place like this alone?”

“Sure is. Tokyo's witches and our hunters do regular culling, but monsters aren't exactly rare. My team has a good wizard escorting us, but if a Class A shows up, it'll be hard even to run. Then here you are, walking alone, so I thought you were a humanoid monster.”

The bearded man introduced himself as a trading merchant and asked why Kotaro had been walking alone.

Kotaro thought fast and answered.

“Truth is, I screwed up big in business in Tokyo. You know the new currency they issued, right? I made a huge blunder over that. My wife and kids ran off, I'm broke, and Tokyo's hard to stay in. So I was thinking I might be able to start over in the Tohoku Hunting Association's home turf. Walking alone really was careless of me. I guess the shock of my family abandoning me had messed with my head.”

The lie flowed out smoothly, and the merchant looked sympathetic.

“That's rough. Sorry, but we've got to carry our load to Tokyo now. We can't take you with us to Sendai, but...”

“No, no, that's fine. Put your work first. I hate to make you spend time on an old man like me. But you'll return to Sendai after you unload in Tokyo, right? Could you let me travel with you on the way back?”

“Oh, that'd be no problem at all. What's your name?”

“Kiwada.”

The false name he blurted out belonged to his younger brother's mistress. It chafed to hide the Arataki name, but he could not say that now.

“Kiwada-san, would you like to go back to Tokyo with us for now?”

“No, no. It'd be awkward if I ran into someone I knew. I'll hide at a nearby parking area and wait for you to come back. I've decided the next time I go back to Tokyo will be after I become an official merchant for the Tohoku Hunting Association.”

“Ha ha, aiming high!”

The bearded man laughed cheerfully, and Kotaro joined in with a bright laugh of his own.

It seemed they were about to part ways, but a hooded man raised his hand and joined the conversation.

“Excuse me, but I'm a little worried about leaving Kiwada-san alone like this. Even if he has his reasons, walking a trade route alone is too dangerous. I'd like to at least teach him a few simple things he needs to protect himself.”

“Yeah, you're right. That'd be better. Kiwada-san, he's one of our caravan guards. He's a skilled wizard, so I think what he says will be helpful.”

“Would that be all right? I'd like to teach you some simple magic and basic precautions for defending yourself.”

“Oh, that would be more than welcome. Could you?”

“Then over there. It would be dangerous if I fired magic here and hit someone.”

Led by the hooded wizard, Kotaro entered a grove beside the national highway, a short distance away from the caravan.

Kotaro smirked to himself. It was good to learn as many spells as he could that even ordinary people could use. Kotaro's greatest ability was making plans, but he had a pretty large amount of magic power too. Getting another card in his hand in a place like this meant his luck was turning around.

Sure enough, once you hit rock bottom, the only way left is up.

Then the hooded wizard leaned against a tree trunk and passed the time. After enough time had passed not to make anyone suspicious, he returned to the caravan.

“Welcome back, Murakumo-san. What happened to him?”

“Yes. It seems Kiwada-san decided to go on alone rather than wait for us after all. He left before I could stop him.”
