The nice stoat who understood my tastes hadn't just sent the magic-power training method paper itself to Okutama, but also solid supplementary information on how it had been discovered.

I read through it gratefully, and it turned out to be pretty interesting.

Tokyo Magic University's Department of Mutation Studies was the department that researched mutations in monsters and humans.

Right after the Gremlin Disaster, people with electrically charged constitutions became witches and mages, and some animals started turning into monsters. If they could figure out the principles behind those mutations, they might be able to create witches and mages artificially, or stop animals from becoming monsters. Maybe.

Going the other way, they might even be able to turn people like the Spider Witch, who was suffering because she had become a witch, back to normal. The possibilities were endless.

But for all its promise, the Department of Mutation Studies had kept a low profile until now.

The Department of Gremlin Engineering had remarkable results to show, from making general-purpose magic wands to improving backlash-prevention mechanisms.

The Department of Magic Linguistics had been doing great work too. Its bypass incantations of every kind went without saying, and the Nameless Epic was another huge, important research topic.

The Department of Combat Studies ran military training built on real combat data from security forces in various areas, refining wizard combat and turning out dozens of skilled wizards every year.

Besides devising the Class A, B, and C monster classifications, the Department of Monster Studies had pushed forward research into monster ecology and dug into the magic-beast handling brought over by the Hokkaido Magic Beast Farm. It was a very important department.

The Department of Magic Medicine was super important. It mostly researched mushroom disease, but it also wrote response manuals for any outbreak of an unknown magical disease. I'd felt the results of its research into medical uses of magic and monster products firsthand when I had appendicitis.

The Department of Mutation Studies hadn't put out any especially eye-catching results, but only because its work hadn't paid off yet. It had been steadily building up data the whole time.

The discovery of the magic-power training method and its practical use were the culmination of all that steady data collection.

The magic-power training method had been derived from three phenomena that reduced magic-power capacity.

First up was the capacity reduction from magic-power-depletion fainting.

The Department of Mutation Studies professor was a pretty eccentric person. A little weird.

In lectures, he was normal. But any student who said they wanted to join his laboratory got a trial: serve as a test subject once.

“Anyone without the resolve to become a test subject themselves has no right to experiment on other living things!” he said.

It was kind of like bushido.[^1] Not something a researcher should say, but he was saying it, so what could you do?

If you only took its lectures, the Department of Mutation Studies was normal, but getting into its laboratory meant clearing a high bar.

The Department of Mutation Studies laboratory researched the principles of magic-power-depletion fainting using student volunteers who wanted to join it.

When you used up all your magic power, your magic-power capacity decreased.

So what exactly was happening at that moment?

Witches and mages had gained enormous amounts of magic power when they mutated. In that case, the reverse, a loss of magic power, had to be some kind of mutation too.

Investigating in every way they could think of, they found that when magic power decreased through magic-power-depletion fainting, the magnetic field changed along with it. A faint magnetic force radiated outward, centered on the head.

Magic power and Gremlins were closely related to electricity.

And electricity was closely related to magnetism.

It was entirely plausible that changes in magic power would come with magnetism.

Witches and mages could sense and control magic power, but they couldn't perceive magnetism. Without the Department of Mutation Studies' human experiments, nobody would probably have discovered the magnetic-field changes that came with a drop in magic-power capacity.

The Department of Mutation Studies laboratory tried playing those magnetic-field changes in reverse.

The thinking went like this: if they took the magnetic-field changes that occurred when magic power decreased and applied them in reverse, centered on the head, wouldn't magic power increase?

They promptly built a dedicated experiment room, gathered magnets, generated a magnetic field in the room, and ran an experiment exposing one of the students to reverse-playback magnetic-field changes.

It went horribly for that student.

They got hit with the full set of brain damage: severe headaches, nausea, dizziness, hallucinations, reduced attention, aphasia, agnosia, memory impairment, and so on.

Partly thanks to how short the experiment was, it didn't kill them, but the student was left suffering from aftereffects.

It was a negative result, but they had definitely observed a change.

The Department of Mutation Studies wanted more data!

But no matter how you looked at it, this was too dangerous. The student surviving the first experiment had been pure luck. The Department of Mutation Studies professor ran a follow-up experiment on himself and was now paralyzed down one side from the aftereffects.

Professor Ohinata, the university president, frowned on this too and handed down an order not to run overly dangerous experiments. Apparently she didn't forbid them too strictly, though, since the Department of Magic Linguistics had also racked up a staggering death toll from failed experiments in its early days. Not like she can criticize anyone else.

The second magic-power-capacity reduction phenomenon the Department of Mutation Studies dealt with was Gremlin implantation.

Implanting a monster's Gremlin in your body reduced your magic-power capacity by an amount that depended on the Gremlin.

Unlike with magic-power-depletion fainting, this decrease progressed slowly over many days, which made it easier to observe what was going on.

Just like with magic-power-depletion fainting, magnetic-field changes were observed during Gremlin implantation too.

But the way the field changed was different from what happened during magic-power-depletion fainting.

After a tug-of-war with the Department of Magic Medicine, the Department of Mutation Studies obtained a death-row prisoner and used them as a living human specimen in a magnetic-field reverse-playback experiment. The specimen showed numbness on one side of the body, visual impairment, fainting, brain hemorrhaging, and more, and died.

Living human specimens were hard to come by, and dangerous experiments were impossible without them, so the Department of Mutation Studies, at a loss, spent a while analyzing the data it had collected, building theories, and testing hypotheses.

In the Department of Mutation Studies' view, there was no doubt that something in the head was deeply involved in magic power going up and down.

Its evidence was “magnetic-field changes in the head,” “magnetoreception,” and “mushroom disease.”

First, both magic-power-depletion fainting and Gremlin implantation showed magnetic-field changes centered on the head.

Next, humans had a sense in their heads that could detect that field: “magnetoreception.”

According to a paper published in 2019 by a joint research team from the University of Tokyo and the California Institute of Technology, all humans had the ability to sense magnetism.

Magnetoreception, the sense of the Earth's magnetic field, was something many animals in the habit of traveling long distances had, such as migratory birds and salmon.

Humans had magnetoreception too, though it was extremely weak. According to the paper, an experiment that magnetically stimulated the heads of 34 men and women found that their brain waves reacted differently depending on the direction of the magnetic field.

This reaction happened completely unconsciously and was so slight that you couldn't even tell you were sensing magnetism.

But humans definitely had a sixth sense in their heads, separate from taste, touch, hearing, sight, and smell: magnetoreception.

Finally, mushroom disease.

The mushrooms in the mushroom pandemic had always grown on people's heads.

Without a single exception, on their heads.

That was no coincidence. During incantations, too, magic power always came out of the mouth. In other words, out of the head.

There had to be something in the head, something invisible but important to magic power.

The Department of Mutation Studies carefully checked its data, worked hard at building theories, and came up with several hypotheses, but there were just too few living human specimens! Magnetic-field reverse-playback experiments were too dangerous to run very often.

Because of that, the Department of Mutation Studies' research crawled along at a turtle's pace.

Then the magic drug and five hundred rank-and-file Arataki Group prisoners were dumped in its lap.

The Department of Mutation Studies went wild.

A third magic-power-capacity reduction phenomenon!

Tons of living human specimens they could use up without a single pang of guilt!

With this insanely exciting new research material in hand, the Department of Mutation Studies pushed ahead all at once with dozens of experiments it had been sitting on as pure theory for ages.

It burned through a huge number of living human specimens comparing the three magic-power-capacity reduction phenomena and working out the rules behind brain damage during magnetic-field reverse playback.

That was how it established a magic-power training method that applied the right magnetic-field changes to the head and raised magic-power capacity without causing brain damage.

According to the rules the Department of Mutation Studies discovered after tireless effort, the magic-power training method needed two things: magnetic-field changes and meditation.

The magnetic-field changes came from magnets. A person went inside a device made from an upright coffin with a huge number of magnets stuck to it, and the coffin rotated slowly at a constant speed, applying the right magnetic-field changes to the head.

But magnetic-field changes alone weren't enough. They did increase magic power, but they also brought on horrific brain damage.

That was where meditation came in.

Roughly put, if you thought about anything unnecessary while the magnetic field was changing, you went crazy and died.

There were various neuroscience explanations for this, but apparently it also came with unsolved phenomena that neuroscience couldn't explain, so the Department of Mutation Studies had only pinned down the part that went, “We don't know why, but if you do it this way, it's okay.”

That part was meditation.

You couldn't move while the magnetic field was changing (the point the magnetic field radiated from would shift).

And you couldn't think about anything.

You had to empty your mind, reaching the state people called things like “clear mirror, still water” or “the state of no self,” where you thought about nothing and your mind was calm.

If your mind wavered even a little, or you thought about something, it showed up in your brain and caused brain damage.

It sounded like a pretty strict requirement, but anyone could learn this meditation through training, and some people could do it with no training.

A high-ranking monk the university brought in as a meditation instructor slipped into a meditative state on his first try and increased his magic power without a shred of brain damage, cool as a cucumber. The Department of Mutation Studies professor also nailed it on his first try.

It varied from student to student in the Department of Mutation Studies laboratory, but they learned to enter a meditative state within roughly 3 to 20 days, and every one of them increased their magic power without taking any damage to the brain.

Even if your meditative state broke partway through a magnetic-field reverse playback, your headache would gradually get worse as a warning sign of brain damage, so as long as you stopped as soon as you felt the headache, no harm was done.

However, meditation didn't let you avoid the damage completely, so the medical view was that after one session of magic-power training, you should ideally leave 24 hours before the next. Back-to-back sessions raised the risk of brain damage.

So magic-power training was a once-a-day thing.

Magic-power training required setting up and running an appropriate magnetic field, and meditation skills were a must.

It wasn't something anyone could do easily.

But with the equipment and some meditation skill, anyone could do it, hard as it was.

A single magnetic-field reverse playback in magic-power training could increase magic-power capacity by 0.2 K.

That number didn't vary from person to person. Whether witches and mages could increase their magic power too was still unknown, because nobody at the Tokyo Witches' Council could meditate (though some had already jumped into training).

They also didn't know yet whether there was a limit to how far magic power could increase; in other words, whether there was a ceiling where you could grow up to a certain point but no further.

For now, all the Department of Mutation Studies students had finished five meditation sessions without anyone's growth stopping yet, so it seemed safe to assume anyone could gain at least 1 K.

As a special case, beast handlers were known to be unable to raise their magic-power capacity with this magic-power training method. Instead of their magic-power capacity going up, the Gremlin implanted in them apparently swelled just a tiny bit. Interesting phenomenon.

But by that logic, I couldn't do magic-power training. If I wanted to, I'd have to go through a tedious procedure.

I had a <ruby>lizard<rt>fire salamander</rt></ruby> Gremlin implanted in the back of my left hand, in the best spot I could find, carefully chosen so it wouldn't get in the way of my fingers. If it swelled and changed size, my dexterity would suffer.

In theory, I could have the implanted Gremlin removed, increase my magic power through magic-power training, then implant the Gremlin again and still come out with more magic power. In fact, one of the Department of Mutation Studies students, a beast handler (a pouch sparrow keeper), had apparently increased their magic power that way.

But while the Gremlin was pulled out, my bond with the fire salamanders would be cut off.

I had lost around 45 K of magic power through the fire-salamander Gremlin implantation. At 0.2 K a day of magic-power training, just getting back what I'd lost would take more than half a year.

There's no way I can go half a year without spending time with the fire salamanders.

Who's going to feed them in the meantime? Who's going to polish their scales? I won't be able to squish their bellies or go for walks with them.

I can't take it. No way.

I'd still like to learn meditation, at least.

Using the materials Professor Ohinata sent me as a reference, I'll take the money I have lying around from making the seven wands, buy up magnets, and try building a meditation room at home too. The lines of magnetic force with magical meaning that complex magnetic fields create look like they'd make a good reference for making wands, anyway.

But I probably wouldn't be increasing my own magic power through magic-power training.

Humans had little magic power. There were plenty of spells they could pronounce but couldn't use because they didn't have enough magic power.

The magic-power training method was an unbelievably important discovery that broke down that wall of limited magic power.

Now that this research had been published, production of magnetic-field reverse-playback mechanisms would probably ramp up, and more and more people would learn meditation.

For now, safety had only been confirmed and data was only being collected at the laboratory level.

If Tokyo's more than two million residents started magic-power training, the data would grow by leaps and bounds, and they would surely learn new things.

Even if they learned nothing, it was almost certain that anyone could increase their magic power by at least 1 K.

In other words, people who until now hadn't even been able to use the shooting-magic core spell, “<ruby>![gaiji-0002.png](images/gaiji-0002.png)-<rt>Fire</rt></ruby>,” would be able to use magic.

As long as they trained, every last human being could now use magic.

The dawn of a new age.

If people keep at magic-power training, some former ordinary person might even end up with insane magic power of around 10,000 K! I'm getting fired up.

Since magic-power training was effectively off the table for me, I couldn't ride this big wave.

But it looked like I could do something for the people riding that big wave.

Let me think about whether I can apply magic-power training theory to make some new kind of magic wand...
