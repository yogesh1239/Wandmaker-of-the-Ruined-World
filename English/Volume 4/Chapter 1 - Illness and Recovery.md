Why were monsters only now starting to gain new abilities? And the same troublesome ability at that?

Apparently, the research budget for the university's Department of Monster Studies had been increased this academic year to investigate that mystery.

The Department of Combat Studies had increased its recruitment too, and the Department of Gremlin Engineering was apparently researching a Gremlin grenade that used sharp Gremlins against Class A monsters.

Okutama was a peaceful region where even the strongest monsters were only Class C-1, and the Lost Mist was always spread over it. It was one of Tokyo's safest areas, but hearing all this made it hard to let my guard down. Monsters getting stronger had a hundred harms and not one benefit.

Now then.

I had been recuperating while reading all kinds of suspicious news in letters, but May and rice-planting time were approaching. The period when I had to rest had passed, so I took the fire salamanders with me and went for a walk to get my body used to moving again.

Rice planting was hard work. I had to get back in shape before then.

The fire salamanders understood the pack hierarchy well, so when we went for a walk, they never walked in front of me. They toddled along behind me.

Tsubaki, who had the biggest attitude, chased grasshoppers and caught them with its front paws. Curious Mokutan was busy chasing butterflies and trying to breathe fire on dandelion fluff. Laid-back Sekitan tended to fall behind, so Tsubaki often poked Sekitan's tail to hurry it along.

The Lost Mist spread across all of Okutama, so I couldn't enjoy the distant view of the spring mountains turning green. Still, walking familiar roads was a nice change of pace.

I felt good too. I even tried jogging a little, but my stomach did not hurt. Looks like I'm fully recovered.

I'm back! Ori Kenshi is back!

After walking to the edge of Okutama, I turned on my heel and was about to head home feeling good, but I thought I heard a voice from beyond the mist and stopped.

Thinking I might have misheard, I listened carefully, and definitely heard a voice.

“...-cle... ...ncle...”

It was a human voice.

And it belonged to a young girl.

I was confused. People aren't supposed to come near here, are they?

No, wait. Right.

Compulsory education had restarted, and the letters said children walking to and from school for the first time in their lives were often getting lost.

Ah-ha. I got it.

Some kid must have come all the way out here along the railway.

It could happen.

After all, when I was in elementary school, I once wandered all the way into the next city on foot. I felt like if I just walked straight ahead, I'd get somewhere I recognized. Back then, the police who came looking for me after my parents reported me missing helped me out.

Even if it's a lost child, they're still a person. I don't exactly want to go meet them.

But the Lost Mist is magical. Anyone who steps into it could end up wandering around until they starve to death.

“Uncle! Uncle! Unclllllle! ...Where are you? Uncle!”

The girl was anxiously calling for her uncle.

U-Um. I wanted to pretend I hadn't heard her and go home.

But if I ignored her here, she'd probably keep wandering until she died.

Even I can't do that.

I thought about calling Hiyori and having her handle it, but I couldn't call her over something this stupid. This is on a completely different level from a dragon showing up or getting sick.

I took several full minutes to work up the courage to meet a stranger. Just in case, I picked up Mokutan and Sekitan, one in each hand, to use as flamethrowers and headed toward the voice.

All I had to do was guide the lost child to the Ome Line tracks and tell her which way led toward central Tokyo. I didn't care whether she could make it home. I couldn't take responsibility that far.

What emerged from the mist was a young girl, just as the voice had suggested.

She was about as tall as a young elementary schooler. She had a cute, childish face, long green hair, and an elegant red flower ornament in her hair. A soft floral fragrance that somehow smelled both familiar and unfamiliar came from the girl as she looked around anxiously through the mist.

As I moved closer to speak to her, the girl turned around and noticed me.

And I noticed too.

What this girl was wearing on her lower half wasn't a skirt.

It was leaves and petals.

This girl's upper body was human, but her lower body was a plant!

“Ah! It's Uncle! I finally found you!”

The girl's anxious expression completely changed. Her face lit up, and she happily ran over to me.

I was so surprised my legs almost gave out.

Whaaaaaaaaaaaaaat!?

You're the Flower Witch's daughter plant!?

Why are you here!?
