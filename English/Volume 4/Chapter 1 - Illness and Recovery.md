I was really glad I was part of the generation that had gotten through all its schooling in peaceful times.

If it sounded this rough just hearing about it as an outsider, the people actually dealing with it had to be in total chaos. My condolences~.

Education wasn't the only issue, either. Monsters had been another headache for the Witches' Council over the past few months.

The Daidarabocchi hunt around the end of last year was still fresh in memory.

Mysterious black Gremlins like the one Daidarabocchi had carried were now turning up here and there in Class A monsters and some Class B monsters.

Around the time of the Daidarabocchi hunt, powerful monsters had started becoming even more powerful.

Mainly, Class A monsters had started using time-acceleration magic.

The corpses of monsters that used time-acceleration magic always yielded a mysterious black Gremlin, one that crumbled to dust and vanished over time.

Class A monsters were powerful enough as it was; only witches or mages could defeat them.

For those same Class A monsters to start gaining a troublesome new ability was extremely serious.

At present, Class A monsters with black Gremlins made up about one-fifth of the total, and their time acceleration only lasted a few seconds; the witches could still handle them.

But monsters with black Gremlins were gradually showing up more often.

There had also been several incidents where battle-hardened witches were caught off guard and injured by a monster that suddenly accelerated.

If more monsters with black Gremlins kept appearing and awakening to new abilities, there was no telling what would happen. In the worst case, the witches and wizards might be pushed past what they could handle, and even Tokyo being trampled by a horde of powerful monsters couldn't be ruled out.

Five years after the Gremlin Disaster.

Why were monsters only now starting to gain new abilities? And the same troublesome ability, at that?

Apparently, the university's Department of Monster Studies had been given a bigger research budget this academic year to get to the bottom of that mystery.

The Department of Combat Studies was taking in more recruits too, and the Department of Gremlin Engineering was apparently researching an anti-Class-A-monster Gremlin grenade that used sharp Gremlins.

Okutama was a peaceful area where the monsters were Class C-1 at most, and the Lost Mist always hung over it. It was one of the safest places in Tokyo, but from what I was hearing, I couldn't afford to let my guard down. Stronger monsters were all harm and no good.

Now then.

So I'd been recuperating and reading all sorts of ominous news in the letters, but May was getting close, and rice planting with it. The period when I had to rest was over too, so I took the fire salamanders along and went out for a walk to get my body used to moving again.

Rice planting was hard work, and I needed to get my rusty body back in shape before then.

The fire salamanders had a firm grasp of the pack hierarchy, so on our walks they never went ahead of me, just toddled along behind.

Tsubaki, the one with the biggest attitude, was chasing grasshoppers and catching them with its front paws, while curious Mokutan kept busy chasing butterflies and trying out its fire breath on dandelion fluff. Laid-back Sekitan tended to fall behind, so Tsubaki kept poking Sekitan's tail to hurry it along.

The Lost Mist covered all of Okutama, so I couldn't enjoy a distant view of the spring mountains bursting into green, but a walk along familiar roads still made a nice change of pace.

My body felt good too. I even tried a light jog, and my stomach didn't hurt. It seemed safe to say I'd fully recovered.

I'm back! Ori Kenshi is back!

I'd walked to the edge of Okutama and turned on my heel to head home in a good mood when I thought I heard a voice from beyond the mist, and stopped.

Wondering if I'd misheard, I listened closely, and there was definitely a voice.

“...-cle... ...ncle...”

It was a human voice.

A young girl's, at that.

I was confused. People aren't supposed to come near here, are they?

No, wait. Right.

One of the letters had mentioned that, now that compulsory education had restarted, kids walking to and from school for the first time in their lives kept getting lost.

Aha. I've got it.

Some kid must have followed the tracks all the way out here.

Totally possible.

After all, back in elementary school, I once wandered on foot all the way into the next city. I just had this feeling that if I kept walking straight, I'd end up somewhere I recognized. That time, I ended up in the care of the police, who came looking for me after my parents called them.

Lost child or not, it was still a person. I wasn't about to go meet one by choice.

But the Lost Mist was magic mist. Anyone who stepped into it could, in the worst case, be kept lost until they starved to death.

“Uncle! Uncle! Unclllllle! ...Where are you? Uncle!”

The girl sounded forlorn as she called for her uncle.

Uhh, hmm. I want to pretend I didn't hear anything and just go home.

But if I play dumb here, she'll probably keep wandering until she dies.

Yeah, that'd be going too far.

I thought about calling Hiyori to deal with it, but there was no way I could call her over something this stupid. This was on a whole different level from a dragon showing up or getting sick.

It took me several full minutes to work up the courage to meet a stranger. Then, just in case, I picked up Mokutan and Sekitan, one in each hand, as stand-in flamethrowers and headed toward the voice.

Guiding the lost kid to the Ome Line tracks and pointing her toward central Tokyo would be plenty. Whether she made it home from there was none of my business. I couldn't take responsibility that far.

The figure that emerged from the mist was, just as the voice had suggested, a little girl.

She was about the height of a kid in the lower grades of elementary school, with a cute, childlike face and long green hair decorated with an elegant red flower ornament. As she looked anxiously around in the mist, a gentle floral fragrance drifted from her, one that seemed both familiar and not.

As I approached to call out to her, the girl turned around and noticed me.

And I noticed too.

What she was wearing on her lower half wasn't a skirt.

It was leaves and petals.

This girl's upper body was human, but her lower body was a plant!

“Ah! It's Uncle! I finally found you!”

Her anxious look changed in a flash; her face lit up, and she came running over to me, delighted.

I was so shocked my legs nearly gave out.

Whaaaaaaaaaaaaaat!?

You're the Flower Witch's daughter plant!?

Why are you here!?
