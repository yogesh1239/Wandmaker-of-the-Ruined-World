It was one of those days when the clear blue sky just felt good.

Sekitan had grabbed my sock in its teeth and tugged on it to make its wishes known, so I let it lead me out on a walk to work off breakfast.

The dandelions blooming in the abandoned fields, which nobody tended anymore, had turned into white puffballs in the little while since I'd last looked, and the spring breeze sent the seeds drifting lightly away. Sekitan got distracted by the fluff floating through the air and nearly tumbled into the gutter at the side of the road.

“Mii...”

“You okay?”

“Mi.”

Sekitan carefully backed away from the gutter, which was clogged with mud and dead leaves, gave a short cry, and took the lead again.

It was unusual for laid-back Sekitan, who always seemed to be spacing out, to invite just me along instead of teaming up with the other two. Behind that placid face, Sekitan was far more thoughtful and clever than it looked. This walk had to have some purpose too.

Sekitan kept getting sidetracked and wobbling off after dandelion fluff, grasshoppers, and the sparkle of crystal-rain Gremlins sunk in puddles, but in the end it led me to the waterwheel hut I'd set up on the Tama River floodplain.

The waterwheel, which I'd built from floorboards pried out of empty houses, broke down a lot. Sometimes it couldn't handle the swollen river in the rainy season and got washed away, hut and all. Other times, mutant water plants that had multiplied out of control tangled around it and stopped it dead. Once, a gross monster that looked like a cross between a fish and a bird had even turned it into its nest.

I couldn't mill the rice I harvested without the waterwheel, and I also used it to grind big batches of Gremlins into powder, pound mochi, and press oil.

I used it all the time, so its habit of breaking down was a real headache.

The waterwheel wasn't turning today either. As I scratched my head, figuring it needed fixing again, Sekitan tugged at my shoelace with its mouth and showed me the gap between the waterwheel's buckets and its axle.

Wedged in there was the charred corpse of a four-armed bear. It had to weigh a good 400 kg, and it was caught on the waterwheel, half-submerged in the river.

“Whoa...! I-It's huge. Class C-1?”

“Miimi. Mimi.”

Only weak Class C monsters showed up in Okutama, but this misshapen bear looked like one of the stronger ones even among the weak. The Gremlin at its throat was on the big side too.

As I poked at the scorched-smelling fur to check it over, Sekitan clambered onto the corpse and kept breathing fire and wagging its tail right in front of me, showing off.

“Hm? Did Sekitan take it down?”

“Mi!”

“...All by yourself?”

“Mi.”

“Nice one. You're growing and getting stronger too. Sekitan's strong. Good job!”

“Mimimii!”

I squished my cute pet between both hands and petted it all over for showing me its kill, and it let out a proud meep-meep.

To think something small enough to sit in one hand could take down a monster this huge. Talk about giant-killing.

A misshapen bear this muscular, with four arms to boot, couldn't be killed by <ruby>![gaiji-0002.png](images/gaiji-0002.png)-<rt>shooting magic</rt></ruby>. You needed <ruby>Do Vaa-ra<rt>ice-spear magic</rt></ruby>.

Even before I drastically cut my magic power in exchange for implanting a fire salamander Gremlin, I wasn't sure I could have beaten it. And Sekitan had taken it down without a scratch, which was seriously reassuring.

After I'd showered Sekitan with praise and spoiled it rotten, I pried the monster corpse off the waterwheel and wrestled it up onto the riverbank.

When I stomped hard on the riverbank gravel a few times, Fuyo's roots felt the vibrations and sprang out of the ground. Their tips roved around as if searching for something, and when I let them touch the burnt corpse, it was dragged underground in no time.

Having secured its new nutrients, the root made one last graceful bend into a heart shape for me, then burrowed back into the ground and disappeared.

In the month since she'd taken root, Fuyo had spread her roots a long way. Only recently, they'd finally reached my house from the mountain out back, and now the riverbank was already under her control. It wouldn't be long before Fuyo's roots ran all through Okutama.

The one exception was the area around the reverberatory furnace, where she hadn't put down any roots so she wouldn't provoke the fire salamanders. That was the truce between the witches' children.

Fuyo didn't seem to have any complaints about my mediation as the boss of my pack, and she hit me with the kind of inhuman affection you'd expect from a monster, saying things like, “When Uncle dies, I'll suck up every bit of you♡.”

Given the possibility that resurrection magic existed, part of me wanted her to wait a bit after I died and see what happened, but eh, she could do what she liked. I wasn't interested in what happened after I was dead.

Compared with Fuyo's, the fire salamanders' affection was easy to understand. They showed off their kills like this and begged me to praise them. It was hard to believe creatures this adorable had been born from the sex-obsessed Flame Witch.

While I petted Sekitan and thought about this and that, it went all limp and fell asleep. I gently laid my pet, a snot bubble swelling from its nose, on the ashes in the waterwheel hut's brazier. Since I was here anyway, I decided to run a little experiment.

The waterwheel hut had a homemade Graham condenser that ran on water drawn up by the waterwheel. With the water mains dead, the waterwheel came in really handy for semi-automating experiments like this too.

According to a paper by Professor Handa Sakunosuke of Tokyo Magic University's Department of Gremlin Engineering, natural Gremlins always contained impurities.

Crystal-rain Gremlins formed in cumulonimbus clouds and fell to the ground with the rain, taking in dust and moisture from the air.

The Gremlins inside monsters had blood components mixed in (which was why they came in all sorts of colors).

The trace impurities in these Gremlins dragged down the quality of the melt-recast Gremlin used in the backlash-prevention mechanism, leaving it neither uniform nor top-grade.

So Professor Handa came up with a method for refining pure melt-recast Gremlin with no impurities at all.

Gremlin melted at a high temperature of 1,200°C. If you pushed it further, to 2,500°C, it reached its boiling point and evaporated. Cooling that Gremlin vapor back into a solid gave you pure Gremlin.

Putting the theory into practice wasn't as easy as it sounded. If you were after the highest quality, you had to work in a space filled with noble gases, keeping the Gremlin away from nitrogen, oxygen, and the rest of the air, so that it wouldn't take in anything but Gremlin as it re-solidified after sublimating—and there was plenty more besides.

This part was seriously convoluted, and the paper went on and on about the technical barriers to Gremlin refining and how to overcome them, plus proposals for scaling the process up from lab experiments to industrial production. I had to reread it about three times before I fully understood it.

Professor Handa can't come up with a single idea for a new invention, but give him an existing one and he'll come up with endless ways to improve it... He has zero ability to turn 0 into 1, and a ridiculously high ability to turn 1 into 100.

He's upgraded both the backlash-prevention mechanism I invented and the amulet in no time flat, too. Then again, he dutifully credits Wand Maker 0933 as a collaborator at the end of every single paper, so I can't say I mind.

Still, even if it's just one field, getting outdone as a Wand Maker is frustrat...

...No, I'm not frustrated, okay?

Besides, Hiyori often praises me too, saying Ori's the best Wand Maker there is. If the strongest witch herself gives me her seal of approval, there's no way it's wrong.

While I kept the Graham condenser's test run going, I amused myself by poking Sekitan's snot bubble with a finger to pop it. Down the line, I planned to use this thing to refine the blood components needed for personal-color Gremlins, mix them with Professor Handa's refined melt-recast Gremlin, and make top-quality, made-to-order magic-school-customized wands. You could never run too many function tests beforehand.

Fortunately, the homemade Graham condenser was working well. It outperformed the Liebig condenser I'd swiped from the Okutama Middle School science room, steadily distilling my homemade unfiltered sake and collecting more and more high-proof alcohol.

I timed it with a wind-up pocket watch, measured the processing efficiency per unit of time, and jotted the numbers down in my notebook. Hmm, decent efficiency, and no glitches either.

“Mi...?”

As I checked that the condenser's thermometer was fitted properly, Sekitan's nose twitched, and it woke up. It wriggled out of its brazier bed, scampered up the leg of the hut's workbench, and stared, drooling, at the flask of distilled alcohol.

Crap. So the smell of alcohol had caught the fire salamander's attention.

“Wait, Sekitan. That's not food. I'm in the middle of an experiment. Wait.”

“Mii...”

Sekitan looked up at me longingly, but when I shook my head, its tail drooped, and it gave up.

What a good listener. Mokutan or Tsubaki would've been way more persistent. Good job holding back!

Using fire really did draw the fire salamanders' attention. When I ran experiments like this, it might be safer to have Hiyori keep watch so they didn't get into mischief. And if I was going to ask Hiyori for help and tighten up safety, I'd better put the experiments on hold for a while.

Hiyori had been on edge lately and hadn't been coming to Okutama.

The Foresight Mage had warned her, “Cyanos will be stolen,” so she was holed up at home on high alert.

Apparently, she'd left Okutama's security to Fuyo and was even staying away from Professor Ohinata so that nobody would get dragged in if something happened.

Thanks to the warning and all that vigilance, the day Cyanos was originally supposed to be stolen came and went safely, without incident.

But the thief apparently hadn't given up, and orders had come down to stay on guard, so Hiyori couldn't make any careless moves.

She had told me over and over, to the point of nagging, never to come to Ome because it was dangerous. So here I was in safe Okutama, goofing off with my pets and the neighbors and working away at experiments and wandmaking at a leisurely pace.

And it really did look like a pretty dangerous situation.

After all, without Foresight's warning, Cyanos probably would have been stolen.

Just the fact that they pulled off stealing a magic wand from the Blue Witch, of all people (well, would have), told me the culprit was no ordinary crook.

I'd heard the Flame Heir Witch had also had a wand stolen, but she was a human at base who had inherited the title of witch under special circumstances. She wasn't in the same league as the Blue Witch, the best of the best even among witches.

Who could the culprit be? It was unsettling.

Personally, though it was in poor taste, the talk of Cyanos maybe getting stolen had me kind of excited. It was in really poor taste, so I kept that to myself.

Sometimes, artworks shot up in value by being stolen.

Someone wanting a piece badly enough to resort to theft, plus the intriguing history of having been stolen, added value. Picasso's paintings got stolen left and right, for one.

“If it gets stolen once and then recovered right away, think that'd give it a nice little backstory? Right, Sekitan?”

“Mii?”

“That'd be outrage marketing, I guess. Staging stuff isn't cool. Hiyori seems like she'd hate that kind of thing too.”

“Mii.”

I was tickling Sekitan under the chin, and it was looking completely clueless, when the eyeball familiar in my pocket suddenly buzzed.

Speak of the devil: an incoming call from Hiyori. Was she missing the sound of a human voice again?

When I pulled it out of my pocket, the eyeball familiar floated up to my eye level, snapped its pupil wide open, and blasted me with an ear-splitting yell.

“Ori! Are you safe!? Is everything all right!?”

“Whoa!? Wh-What's with you all of a sudden? I was perfectly safe and sound until you started yelling your head off.”

I clamped a hand over my ear, whose eardrum had nearly been blown out, and complained.

Look at Sekitan. Poor thing's so startled it's frozen stiff, eyes rolled back, totally zoned out.

Something was off about Hiyori, and she paid my complaint no mind at all.

“The Foresight Mage has issued an emergency declaration. He's unconscious. Tokyo is in danger!”

“Huh...!?”

She'd put it in plain, simple words, but they just weren't reaching my brain.

While I was still at a loss for words at the sudden news, Hiyori rushed on.

“Until I have a grasp of what's happening—no, until I tell you the situation has settled, do not leave Okutama under any circumstances. Have Fuyo protect you! Got it!?”

Hiyori fired it all off in an unusually desperate voice.

I was speechless.

The last time the Foresight Mage had lost consciousness, the mushroom pandemic had nearly wiped out Tokyo.

Now something on that scale was about to happen. Or it already had.

This is no time to be lazing around the waterwheel hut playing with my pet.

Tokyo is in serious trouble!!
