Nanase Nanami, associate professor in Tokyo Magic University's Department of Magic Linguistics, adored and looked up to Ohinata Kei.

Hardly any of the students who had learned under Ohinata failed to look up to her. Even as Nanase moved up from student to research student to associate professor, that admiration only grew stronger instead of fading.

The university president was smart, kind, helpful, cheerful, and cute, so even the crankiest people ended up liking her. Nanase was no exception. After the stalker incident, she had secretly organized a President Fan Club and set about keeping suspicious people away from her beloved Ohinata and keeping the fans in line.

When the emergency declaration went out early that morning, Nanase was in the hallway outside the document room with Ohinata.

“Emergency declaration! An enemy force will attack Tokyo in two hours! Remain where you are and wait for rescue!

Emergency declaration! An enemy force will attack Tokyo in two hours! Remain where you are and wait for rescue!

Emergency declaration—”

An eyeball familiar sent from the Bunkyo Ward Office flew all over campus, repeating the warning until it reached every corner.

Only seconds earlier, Ohinata had been chatting with a gentle smile; in an instant, her expression hardened.

“...We have a problem. Nanase-san, we are closing the university and fortifying it, starting now. Please help me. The enemy is probably a group of people—most likely the Arataki Group. If Class A monsters were attacking, they would not use the words ‘enemy force.’ We will build anti-personnel barricades at the entrances and set traps. Go to the Department of Combat Studies and tell them this. That is all. Do you have any questions?”

Ohinata grasped the situation at superhuman speed, worked out what was happening, and laid out countermeasures without a moment's hesitation.

Nanase had been on the verge of panic, but watching a girl so much younger than her stay perfectly composed calmed her down too. She took a deep breath and asked just one question.

“What will you do, Professor Ohinata?”

“I will go to Professor Sendo. We need the Department of Monster Studies to bring out the magic beasts it has on hand and place them at key points around the university. You have no other questions, correct?”

“Y-Yes.”

“Come on, hurry. You are allowed to run in the halls today, you know?”

Ohinata gave her one last mischievous wink, then dashed off down the hallway like the wind.

“Be careful, Professor Ohinata!”

Left behind, Nanase called after that small but dependable figure, then turned on her heel and ran the other way down the hallway.

Her heart was pounding. This was different from the cold fear that had crept up and squeezed her during the mushroom pandemic. This time, it wasn't a disease but a clearly hostile force coming to attack them.

Memories of the coup the Iruma Mage had staged flashed back, and she felt sick. Fights between Transcendents were terrifying, and there was no knowing what might happen; one unknown spell could easily turn the whole battle around.

The Tokyo Witches' Council had lost more than ten members since its peak. Even with the Blue Witch, could it really hold Tokyo against the Arataki Group...?

When Nanase burst into the Department of Combat Studies' lab, Professor Onigawara had already given the students their orders and started bringing out magic wands and Monster Traps. Key points on a campus map scrawled across the whiteboard were marked in red; they were clearly in the middle of drawing up a plan.

“Professor Onigawara! A message from the President. The enemy is believed to be the Arataki Group, a group of Transcendents. She said to put up anti-personnel barricades at the entrances and set traps.”

“We have already started.”

Nanase had said it while still catching her breath, and Onigawara answered curtly, his already stern face twisted into an even more frightening scowl.

“The plan is to put magic snipers on the roof and fire down on any enemies caught in the Monster Traps. But we need something to serve as a wall. We have sent people to the Department of Monster Studies to borrow magic beasts.”

“Professor Ohinata is at the Department of Monster Studies right now... No, wait, don't tell me you intend to take them on?”

Something about that bothered Nanase. When she asked, Onigawara nodded as though it were obvious.

“If we are attacked, we fight back.”

“Please wait. The instruction that came with the emergency declaration was ‘Remain where you are and wait for rescue’! The President only ordered us to fortify the university. She did not order a counterattack. Besides, half-baked attack magic will not work on Transcendents.”

“You cannot say that for certain. Even a mosquito can make you bleed to death if it stings you a million times.”

“Th-That's such a meathead way of thinking...”

Onigawara pulled a stab-resistant vest from a locker, shoved it at the speechless Nanase, and hurried off with the armed students.

Responding quickly to an emergency was admirable. But President Ohinata and the professor were already out of step with each other.

Nanase put her head in her hands.

There was no way they could beat Transcendents. And yet Professor Onigawara was raring to fight.

Does he think they have a chance of winning? Or is he just hot-blooded? The former would be fine, but the latter would be the worst possible case. If they attack the enemy and only manage to make them angry, there's no knowing what the angry Transcendents might do to them.

Besides, in an emergency like this, the Foresight Mage normally sent far more detailed instructions. That was how it had gone during the mushroom pandemic.

There should have been concrete instructions that sounded as if he had seen the future—or rather, because he really had. Instructions like “Take that and go here,” “Tell that person this,” or “Get this done by nightfall.”

This time, though, the instructions had no specifics at all.

Without specifics, the people on the ground were left to make more of their own calls, and the chain of command was coming apart.

Nanase feared that the Foresight Mage was already in no condition to give specific instructions.

At the same time, she was irritated that Onigawara had acted on his own judgment.

Fine, forget his defiance of the Foresight Mage's instructions for now. But why won't he follow the President's? Why is he doing something unnecessary? Ohinata has turned the Foresight Mage's vague instructions into something concrete, and that should be enough. Does he seriously think his judgment is better than Ohinata Kei's, of all people?

They needed a single chain of command.

Rather than everyone sending messengers back and forth, all the key people at the university needed to gather in one place and agree on a response.

Her thoughts in order, Nanase ran back out into the hallways, where panicked people were rushing every which way, to bring everyone together.

---

Because the emergency declaration had come two hours before the attack, the university had a decent head start, if not a fully adequate one. The school gates were barricaded, pots of turret balsam were set out, and barbed wire was hidden in the hedges.

Against Transcendents, it all made for pretty flimsy defenses. A single big spell could easily blow every bit of it away.

But the Foresight Mage had told them to wait, not flee. If he had seen a future where the enemy slaughtered every student at Magic University, he would have told them to flee. And if the enemy wasn't out to kill them all—if it was thinking about holding back—then showing a will to resist meant something.

In the large lecture hall where the noncombatants had gathered, Professor Ohinata paced back and forth, her tail drooping anxiously and her ears twitching nonstop. She held her beloved wand Aleister in her arms, and her fingers would not stop fidgeting with it.

“Maybe just negotiate... No, hit them once first... Make it a duel... Give us a common enemy... Spread false information... But then it won't be credible... Ugh...”

The anxious students followed Ohinata with their eyes as she muttered to herself. Nanase wanted to smack down the all-out resistance faction, who had brushed off Ohinata's attempts to stop them.

The emergency faculty meeting had left Tokyo Magic University split in two: the all-out resistance faction and the bloodless surrender faction.

The all-out resistance faction, made up of the hot-blooded Department of Combat Studies and Department of Monster Studies, argued for all-out resistance against the Arataki Group.

Even if they couldn't kill a Transcendent, badly wounding one might open up a chance of forcing a temporary retreat. And even if they were swatted aside without effort, as long as they made the enemy think, “These guys bite,” it would handle them more carefully.

The bloodless surrender faction feared resistance would backfire and argued for submitting to the Arataki Group without a fight.

Badly wound a Transcendent, and it might fly into a rage, stop holding back, and blow the university away. Make the enemy think, “These guys bite,” and it would decide that anything that bit was better off dead.

All-out resistance and bloodless surrender each had one good point.

And since Ohinata had said the best course was for her to negotiate surrender terms across the barricade, her plan had a hundred.

By a hundred to one, the President's policy should obviously have carried the day, but the all-out resistance faction refused to budge, and volunteers ended up resisting anyway. It was an unforgivable outrage.

Driven by anxiety, Nanase quietly slipped over to Ohinata, who wore a troubled look, and whispered in her ear.

“Professor Ohinata. Please, at least save yourself. It isn't too late. I will cut you a way out.”

“I cannot. If the head of an organization runs away after things have come this far, we will lose trust and morale will collapse. Whether we surrender or resist, I have to be here.”

Ohinata whispered back with a fragile smile, and Nanase's chest tightened.

Why did she have to bear such a heavy responsibility?

It was always like this. Ever since the Gremlin Disaster, it was always the people Nanase wanted to see take it easy who suffered, and the people she wanted to live who died.

Nanase grabbed Ohinata's sleeve and tugged it hard.

“If you give the word, Professor, there is nothing we cannot do. If those people catch you, there is no knowing what they will do to you.”

Through a gap in the boards nailed over the windows, she could see the Arataki Group loitering outside the blocked school gates. Every last one of them had hair dyed loud colors or buzzed short, and wore a suit sloppily over a flashy patterned shirt. Their ears and lips were full of tacky piercings, and they cackled like idiots as they hurled vulgar abuse and waved swords and nail-studded bats around.

The Arataki Group members surrounding the university were painfully obvious <ruby>yakuza<rt>outlaws</rt></ruby>.[^1]

Of course, piercings didn't necessarily make someone a bad person, and Tokyo's security force armed itself with close-combat weapons too. If a rumpled suit made someone the enemy, every salaryman coming off a night shift would qualify.

But ugliness gushed from every part of the Arataki Group, enough to kill any urge to look for their good points and defend them.

Standing beside Nanase, Ohinata glanced out the window and sighed.

“They don't look like the kind of people who would go home if we asked, do they?”

“How can you be so relaxed about this...!?”

“Foresight-san said to wait. If it were better to run, he would be telling us to run. And besides...”

Ohinata paused, then broke into a bright smile.

“Ao-san, the Blue Witch, is in Tokyo. If we hang in there, she will beat up all the bad guys! The whole Arataki Group will end up like this. Like this!”

As she spoke, Ohinata wagged her tail furiously, put on a determined face, and shadowboxed.

Nanase gave a vague nod.

She knew well how close the Blue Witch and Ohinata Kei were. Tokyo's strongest witch made no secret of it, always declaring, “I will protect Kei-chan. I will not go easy on anyone who lays a hand on her.”

That declaration was probably sincere.

But the fact was, the Blue Witch wasn't here right now.

Where Ohinata Kei was, the Blue Witch was not.

Being strong and being dependable were two different things.

During the Iruma coup, the Blue Witch had attacked without a second thought for the allies caught up in it, and every resident of Ome, her own home turf, was dead. However much she bragged that she would protect Ohinata Kei no matter what, Nanase couldn't bring herself to fully believe it.

Even now, the Arataki Group that had descended on Tokyo and surrounded the university wore thoroughly flippant, vulgar grins, and there was no sign whatsoever of the Blue Witch's hammer coming down.

“If the Arataki Group uses me as a hostage, that will make things hard for Ao-san too, so that is my only worry. But I have countermeasures ready, so please rest easy. It will be fine!”

Right after Ohinata pumped her fist and gave that firm assurance, there was movement in front of the gates.

The crowd of punks split apart, and two men appeared.

One was a hard-faced, blue-eyed man who looked to be in his fifties, and trailing after him was a bald man in sunglasses with tattooed arms. Both were clutching raw magic stones.

“We're the Arataki Group! Starting today, Tokyo's ours! Got that? Then bow your heads!!”

At the bald man's bellow, Professor Onigawara stepped forward from Magic University's battle line and shouted back.

“We refuse!”

“I can't hear anything but yes! Want me to twist that mouth of yours till yes is all it can say?”

“…………”

Professor Onigawara answered by sticking up his middle finger, a silent taunt.

With a vein bulging on his forehead, the bald man cleared the barricade in one easy, inhuman leap and landed inside.

The instant his foot touched the ground, a ring of light appeared beneath it, only to be sucked into the magic stone in his hand and vanish.

Professor Onigawara gasped, and Nanase and Ohinata, watching from inside, were stunned too.

“No way. The Monster Trap didn't go off!?”

“!! I see, it's a ground! The magic stone acted as a ground! Now that I think about it, we have never caught anyone carrying a magic stone in a Monster Trap...!”

Ohinata leaned forward as she spoke, her eyes glued to the scene through the gap in the window.

A Monster Trap was a time-stasis magic tool made by linking together crushed magic stones of several different kinds. It could slow time to an incredible crawl for anyone who stepped on it.

But apparently, when a separate magic stone got inside the trap, it warped the magic power flowing through the Monster Trap and threw it out of whack.

Nothing had happened, but the bald man seemed to realize that something had almost been done to him.

“Wh-What was that? Pulling some weird—”

Before the enemy's confusion could turn into fighting spirit, Professor Onigawara raised his right hand, wand and all, as a signal.

At the signal to open the battle, the seven wizards waiting behind him quickly readied their magic tools. Fanned out in formation, they matched their notes through the Seven Wind-Instrument Ritual Implements and performed a ritual-magic incantation, loud and clear.

“<ruby>Naetaku Ji-shio Ontaruku Ea××× Mabuinai××<rt>The warrior held fast by chains relied on his master</rt></ruby>”

The Seven Wind-Instrument Ritual Implements were harmonica-shaped magic tools that Wand Maker 0933 had originally made for research into unpronounceable sounds.

Their amplification ratio was negative; they had never been made for combat in the first place.

But ritual magic, in which several people joined forces to cast magic no ordinary person could, proved more than effective on the battlefield.

The ritual-magic implements shattered under the strain of the forced casting, and at the same moment, chain magic—the specialty of the late Hachioji Witch—activated.

Dull-gray chains shot out of thin air and lunged at the enemy like living things. They coiled around the arm he swung to shake them off, then wound around his whole body.

“You... bastard!”

“Well, well. Walked right into that one, huh? Need help?”

The blue-eyed mage, apparently his superior, laughed in amusement.

The bald man seemed to find that humiliating; his face went bright red, and he growled.

“No, sir! Boss, just watch from there! This... This kind of magic ain't even got proper magic power in it...!”

His defiant boast was cut short by a chain that wound around his mouth like a gag.

With the Monster Trap a dud, they had been forced to burn their prized chain-magic ritual incantation right at the start.

And even those magic chains were being yanked with brute strength; there was no telling when they would snap.

The witch's magic, recreated by humans pooling their wisdom and magic power, was a heavily downgraded version of what the witch herself could do.

Even so, it had stopped him in his tracks.

Nanase had opposed resisting, but even she held her breath as she watched. Would the fangs of a human wizard unit actually reach a Transcendent?

Backstepping as he took aim with his wand, Professor Onigawara fired ice-spear magic at the mage bound in magic chains, and with that, the massed magic barrage began.

Ordinary people had only a limited repertoire of magic. The wizards Magic University was so proud of aimed their wands at the enemy in perfectly matched stances and poured out the strongest spell each of them could cast, holding nothing back.

Ice spears flew every which way.

Light bullets and light beams pounded the enemy.

A swarm of bats made of fresh blood swooped in.

Anyone who ran out of magic power fell back at once, and the next wizard took their place. In under ten seconds, the spells fired in an unbroken stream probably numbered a hundred.

The storm of magic kicked up a cloud of dust where it landed, and with visibility gone, they could no longer aim.

“Stop! Spread out!”

The moment the dust spread and aiming became impossible, Professor Onigawara quickly gave the order, turned his back on the enemy, and ran. The wizard unit scattered neatly in every direction as well.

“Gaaaah!! I'll fucking kill you!!”

The bald man ripped the chains apart and burst out of the dust cloud, his suit in tatters and his sunglasses broken.

But what about the injuries that mattered—

“He's bleeding! It's working, Professor Ohinata!”

“Not yet. A mage would naturally heal that much in a few hours. It does not even count as an injury.”

Ohinata answered the delighted Nanase with a calm assessment.

As the two of them watched, the battle shifted again.

The bald man tried to use some kind of magic on the wizard unit scattering in all directions, but a rain of magic from diagonally above slammed him into the ground. A group of wizards stationed on the roof had started sniping.

Every time the man, now eating dirt, tried to get up, the ice spears and light bullets raining down beat him back to one knee.

But that was as far as their advantage went.

For just an instant, the mage who had been taking a one-sided beating fully regained his stance.

The next, he took off running at an unbelievable speed.

The bald mage reached top speed in no time, and the magic stopped hitting him entirely. Every spell fired down at him landed a good three or four steps behind.

It was simple: a moving target was hard to hit, and a fast one all the more so.

With a long enough run-up, the mage raced straight up the outer wall of the south building to the roof, without using any magic.

He easily dodged every one of the murderous seed bullets fired by the turret balsam lined up there, then, carrying that momentum, sent every wizard on the roof flying with his fists.

Nanase gasped and covered Ohinata's eyes with her hand to keep her from seeing the carnage.

Blood sprayed, and human bodies were torn apart and flung through the air.

Even the lucky wizards who escaped instant death after a Transcendent's blow knocked them into the air fell from the roof and left grotesque flowers of blood blooming on the ground below.

Without even using magic. The enemy mage had crippled half of the university's wizard unit with ease.

Nanase went pale. She wanted to slap herself for thinking, even for a split second, that they might pull this off. After everything they had thrown at him, all he had to show for it were scrapes and bruises all over his body.

As living creatures, humans and Transcendents were simply too far apart in fundamental strength.

Transcendents were Transcendents precisely because they had transcended the human realm.

They had never been something humans could beat, after all.

“...The outcome is decided. Nanase-san, thank you for covering my eyes. I am all right now.”

Ohinata said it in a trembling voice that did not sound all right at all, and gently moved Nanase's hand away.

The victorious mage's booming laughter rang across the whole university, and the students sheltering in the large lecture hall trembled and shrank in on themselves. Cold fear slowly filled the room.

Ohinata had to be terrified, and she surely wanted to cry, but she bravely kept her composure and gave Nanase firm instructions.

“I will request a ceasefire from the Arataki Group. Somehow, I will negotiate for everyone's lives—”

“I understand. Leave it to me. Even if it costs me my life, I will stop this fighting from going any further.”

Nanase started to dash out of the lecture hall in her place, but Ohinata tugged her sleeve and stopped her.

“This is my job as the president of Magic University.”

“No, this is an adult's job! Professor Ohinata, you are still a child!”

Nanase indignantly shook her off and tried to leave, but the adorable girl with small beast ears would not let her go.

Ohinata took a deep breath, then spoke in a calm, gentle voice, like a professor counseling a student about their future.

“Nanase-san. You have always treated me as an adult. You have done so much for me behind the scenes, respecting what I do and supporting me. That look of respect in your eyes, seeing me as a researcher in my own right—it made me very happy. So I do not want to be treated like a child only now, at a time like this.”

Tokyo Magic University's first president, Ohinata Kei, gently took the speechless Nanase's hand in both of hers and gave her an order, her voice kind.

“Nanase-san. Please stay hidden somewhere. If everyone surrenders and the Arataki Group decides to execute us all, we will be wiped out. We need someone to tell whoever comes to rescue us what happened here.

No. I will not hear any objections. So that we do not all go down together, so that you can help me later, endure for now. No matter what happens. All right?”

Ohinata's eyes were set; nothing would make her yield.

No matter how she tried, Nanase could not get a sound out of her throat. She only nodded.

The Foresight Mage had said, “Wait for rescue.”

All she could do was believe those words.

Surely—definitely—rescue would come.

In agony, Nanase turned her back on the professor she adored and looked up to, then vanished into the darkness of the school building to carry out her professor's instructions.
