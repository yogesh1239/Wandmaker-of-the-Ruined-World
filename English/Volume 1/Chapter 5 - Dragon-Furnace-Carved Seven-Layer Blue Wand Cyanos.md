I was washing my face with well water that morning when I spotted an ice statue of a freaking huge kaiju, its face poking up over the mountains.

"Huh..."

I dropped my foam-covered toothbrush.

What is that?

The Tokyo Snow Festival, in the middle of summer?

It was too huge to tell what it was... It was too huge to tell what it was.

What? What is that thing?

Is it a monster? Something somebody made? The real thing? An illusion?

I was gaping at the kaiju ice statue when I felt someone watching me, and I nearly jumped out of my skin when I saw the masked Blue Witch peeking out from behind my front gate.

That scared the hell out of me! If you're here, say so! Why are you staring at me without a sound or a hint of presence!?

"At least say something! My heart stopped!"

"I mean, yeah, I'm the one who asked you not to talk to me, but..."

Well, yeah, but still?

Maybe it was because the mask hid half her face, but seeing the Blue Witch had stopped being so scary.

At first I'd thought of her as something like a delivery lady, but these days she felt more like the neighborhood Fairy-san. I'd dropped the polite speech without even thinking about it and just talked to her casually.

I hated meeting people, but I didn't hate meeting Fairy-san.

Hmm.

"Can you try saying something?"

"?"

"Yeah. Sorry, I know I told you not to talk and now I'm asking you to. Just a little, for now."

"Uh, good morning...?"

The Blue Witch sounded bewildered, like she couldn't work out what I was after, but hearing her voice didn't wring my stomach with stress at all. Yep, with her face hidden I couldn't read her expression, and that made this way easier.

"Okay, you can talk normally from now on. Writing notes was a pain, right? But no yelling. The mask stays on at all times. And no touching."

"You're a demanding man. Well, all right."

"Sorry, I appreciate it. So, I wanted to ask—do you know what that thing is?"

I pointed at the kaiju ice statue in the distance, and the Blue Witch answered as she tossed me a paper bag of supplies.

"It's part of why I came today. That's the monster that attacked Tokyo yesterday."

According to her, the kaiju had come out of Tokyo Bay yesterday and gone on a rampage, wrecking the city.

So the Blue Witch had frozen it solid and taken it down. Strong.

The trouble was, the aftereffects had frozen the city too, and the ice wouldn't melt with ordinary fire. Apparently the cold pouring off it had made central Tokyo so chilly you'd never guess it was summer.

Someone called the Flame Witch had apparently started thawing the city with magical fire this morning, but the frozen zone was the size of all of Hamura City. Who knew when that would be done.

"Cities sure are scary. Good thing I live way out in the mountains."

"Don't act like this doesn't concern you, Ori. They found out I have a magic wand."

"Huh? But isn't that a good thing?"

She said it like something bad had happened, and I tilted my head.

The whole deal was supposed to be that I made the magic wands and the Blue Witch advertised and sold them.

If she'd used Cyanos to take down a giant kaiju attacking Tokyo so spectacularly, I was all for it. Perfect advertising!

The Blue Witch sighed at my confusion.

"There are limits to advertising. If you'd made guns, you could sell them. Even missiles, you could sell if you picked your buyers. But once you get to the nuclear-bomb class, you can't. You've become Oppenheimer, Ori."

She said it so gravely that I nodded along solemnly, but I didn't really get her point. What's an Oppenheimer?

"The other witches grilled me about where this overtechnology came from. I managed to fudge my way out and get away from them."

"Why? That was the perfect chance to pitch it!"

Once you've caught your target market's interest, you pitch right away. That was the job I'd handed the Blue Witch, so I didn't get why she wasn't even trying to sell.

When I tilted my head even further, the Blue Witch put her head in her hands.

I could tell I'd said something weird, but I had no idea what.

We can't sell it because it can cast magic on the level of a nuclear bomb...? Why? Isn't my wand the reason she could freeze that kaiju and take it down? Everyone's going to want one, right? So sell them.

When I asked her all that straight out, the Blue Witch, thoroughly fed up with me, explained.

She walked me patiently through an engineer's responsibilities, Big Stick diplomacy, and the concept of the balance of power, and I finally understood where I stood.

In short: if a nuclear weapon ends up in some bad guy's hands, it's a huge disaster, and it's all because of the tech you developed! That was the gist.

N-No way. That's awful!

All I wanted was to make awesome magic wands and show off.

I get why low-performance wands wouldn't sell, but can a wand really be too good to sell?

Still, once it clicked, I could totally see the Blue Witch's point.

Yeah, that's right. Not everyone's going to use my magic wands for good. It's obvious when you think about it.

This is a whole different thing from selling fan-made anime merch. What I've made is a weapon that can easily destroy the world.

Obviously some people are going to misuse it—to threaten people, kill them, rule over them.

It'd make me sick if a wand I made caused some disaster. I want my wands to make a name for themselves, just not a bad one.

NO to outrage marketing!

I stood there with my arms crossed for a while, agonizing over how serious this was, until I looked at the Blue Witch, brooding just as gravely, and it hit me.

She says my magic wand is a nuclear weapon, but she used it the right way, didn't she?

She's the hero who took down a kaiju that was wrecking Tokyo.

People always say it isn't the tools that are bad—it's the people using them.

So couldn't we just pick who we sell to?

Actually, we could even give them away for free.

A magic wand with tremendous power, granted to someone with a righteous heart!

That's so hype. I'm starting to like it better than dumping them on the market for top dollar.

"Hey."

"Hm?"

"I still want to sell them. But forget about the price for now. Can you find people who seem like they'd use them right and sell to them?"

The Blue Witch looked so troubled at the suggestion that I could tell even through her mask.

"I know that would be ideal. These are rough times, and the more power that gets used the right way, the better. But I'm a poor judge of character."

"You're a better judge than me."

"Well, yes. But there's no point comparing me to someone who scores zero in people skills."

Wow, you don't hold back! But zero's going too far—I've got like three points!

"Come on, please. I don't want the magic wands I worked so hard on just gathering dust. A piece only matters once it's in someone's hands, right? As long as they don't misuse them, we could sell them to people who just want one for display, or as a stage prop, or whatever."

"But what if we trust someone not to misuse it, sell to them, and it ends in tragedy? I trusted my father, mother, and childhood friend, and every one of them betrayed me."

"That's a lot of betrayal. But isn't that on the people who did the betraying? Not that I know what happened."

She talked like she had no eye for people, but getting betrayed by the big three of people you're supposed to be able to trust wasn't the Blue Witch's fault.

Even my parents, who'd gotten fed up with what a social misfit I was, had only washed their hands of me and told me to do whatever I wanted. They'd never betrayed me.

"If you sell to someone you trust and it still goes wrong, then oh well, don't sweat it. I don't have an eye for people—or rather, I can't even look people in the eye when I talk to them—so who to sell to is entirely up to you."

"Hey, what else can we do? If we sell them off to just anybody and it ends in a huge disaster, sure, that's on us. But if we properly vet the buyers and a huge disaster still happens, that's not on us."

If I took responsibility for every single thing I ever set in motion, I'd never get anything done.

The Blue Witch has this gloomy air about her, like she's been through all kinds of painful things, but I don't think she has to be so pessimistic.

There are lots of bad people in the world, but lots of good people too. It'll work out. Let's just say it will.

Sure, if I never made another magic wand, the whole to-sell-or-not problem would vanish at the root. But I want to make them and I want to sell them, so that's not happening.

I did my best to talk the Blue Witch around for the sake of my own desires, but if I could actually pull that off, I'd have to turn in my socially-awkward badge.

The Blue Witch reluctantly nodded and gave a noncommittal answer.

"Fine. If I find someone I'm okay selling to, I'll sell."

"That's like saying, 'I'll come if I can make it.'"

She's totally never going to sell any.

This is hopeless. Doesn't look like a single wand is going to sell.

Guess I'll give up for now and wait, however long it takes, for the Blue Witch to change her mind.

It's not like we're in any hurry to sell them anyway. As long as the Blue Witch keeps using Cyanos, my cowardly pride and arrogant shame will be soothed for now.

My job is making the magic wands, so there's no point in me fretting over advertising and sales strategy too. As a professional—as a craftsman—I should focus on my own work.

I was nodding to myself, having settled the whole thing in my own head, when the Blue Witch cleared her throat and turned formal.

"Uh, actually, I have one more thing."

"What?"

"Um, well... Cyanos... broke."

"Ugh."

I'd noticed she was kind of holding Cyanos behind her back, like she was hiding it, but had it really gotten this bad?

"Relax, it's fixable. The core's undamaged. If freezing magic did this, maybe it was frost cracking?"

"Frost cracking?"

The Blue Witch tilted her head, and I couldn't help doing a double take. Seriously? You use ice magic. You should at least know about frost cracking. It's your specialty!

For the sake of the Blue Witch—ex-high schooler, user of freezing magic, and total ignoramus about actual freezing—I reluctantly explained.

"It's called frost cracking—written with the characters for 'freeze' and 'split.' It happens when wood gets to around -25°C or lower. When wood's exposed to extreme cold, the water inside freezes and expands, and the tree splits wide open lengthwise. Go into Hokkaido's snowy mountains in the dead of winter and you'll sometimes hear what sounds like a gunshot. That's frost cracking too—a tree splitting."

"I see. Now that you mention it, I remember a sound like that when I cast Great Glacier magic."

I'd been showing her the huge crack in Cyanos's handle as I explained, and the Blue Witch nodded, apparently remembering something.

"Then it's frost cracking, all right. Wait in the living room. I'll fix it right away."

Cocky, huh? Well, you'd better not underestimate me either. I'm a dexterous man, and fixing a broken wand won't take any time at all.

Once I'd seen her off, I rolled up my sleeves, went straight to the workshop, and got to work swapping out Cyanos's handle.

I took off the old handle, which had split badly lengthwise and gone all wobbly, then carved a new one out of oven-dry wood with 0% moisture content. If freezing, expanding moisture was what had split the handle, I just had to use wood dried to the absolute limit: oven-dry wood.

Oven-dry wood was basically parched, flaky skin with all the moisture gone. It warped easily, developed drying cracks, and didn't look as nice, either. I wasn't a big fan of it as a material. But if the job called for oven-dry wood, then of course I'd use it.

I carved the new handle from the best heartwood I had on hand, checked how it fit the core, and fine-tuned it. Then I finished the surface with quick-drying varnish and treated it so it wouldn't soak up moisture easily, and it was done. Only the material had changed, and the basic design was the same as the last handle, so the work went pretty fast.

Everything a magic wand could do came from the core. Sloppy handle or careful handle, it made no difference at all to the magic's power or how easy the wand was to use.

Even so, my pride as a craftsman wouldn't let me do anything less than finish every last detail properly. I was a craftsman of the hardcore-realism school.

If I do say so myself, it was fast, careful work. This time, I was sure it was perfect.
