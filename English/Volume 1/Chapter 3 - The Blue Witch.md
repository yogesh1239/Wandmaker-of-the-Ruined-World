"There you go. Believe me now?"

The Blue Witch was quiet for a while. Then she wrote something on a sheet of drawing paper and held it up solemnly.

It was cute, round, super girly handwriting that didn't fit her cool look at all. The gap was enough to make my brain glitch.

> Ori, you should go into hiding. If this feat gets out, witches and mages all over Japan will come after you.

"That bad...?"

All I'd done was show off the processing I fiddle with to kill time, and she'd gone dead serious on me, which freaked me out a little.

Isn't that a bit much? Sure, I'm the self-proclaimed world's greatest Wand Maker. A president or a billionaire, maybe, but does anybody really go around kidnapping craftsmen?

While I was frowning over that, the Blue Witch turned the page and kept writing.

> Ori, you're like the one person who can make machine guns in a world that only has bows and arrows. Be aware of that.

"Oh crap."

Once she put it in concrete terms, it finally started to sink in.

This is world-changing stuff. Way too dangerous. If my abilities and identity get out, I'm getting kidnapped and locked up, guaranteed!

...But having the power to change the world is kind of exciting, isn't it?

Honestly, I want to crank out overtechnology magic wands only I can make, sell them by the truckload, and gloat.

The power can stay off for all I care, if only online auctions would come back. I want to sit there grinning while the bids on my magic wands climb higher and higher!

But that's a pipe dream: online auctions are gone, and they're never coming back.

I'd never make it in a business world that's gone backward, where you have to do the legwork and the talking to sell anything.

Just hearing the words "sales pitch" gives me goosebumps.

Is my only option to do what the Blue Witch said and live quietly with my head down?

That's way too depressing. Isn't there some way to stay hidden, never meet anyone, and still show the world my masterpieces, the finest of the age?

> Thanks for your help today. I'll see you back to Okutama. If you ever run into trouble, I'll help.

The Blue Witch held up the drawing paper again. A line reading ~~Want to stay for lunch?~~ had been struck out with a double line. She'd really taken a shine to me, considering she'd tried to kill me when we first met.

Outside, the sun was already past its peak. If I set off now, the three-hour trip home would get me back around dusk. It really was a good time to head out.

The supply run had turned into one unexpected mishap after another, but the haul had been big.

Still, more than anything, I was worn out from dealing with people. She seemed to be going out of her way to be considerate of this fragile little critter with zero tolerance for interpersonal stress, so I figured I'd excuse myself.

"Can I come to Ome now and then to collect supplies?"

The Blue Witch nodded silently.

All right. As long as I've got that promise, everything's A-OK.

I'll be careful not to bump into her from now on, and she'll probably do the same. We'll likely never meet again.

She really helped me out today, filling me in on all kinds of stuff.

Granted, when my conclusion after all that information was "best to keep living pretty much like before," the whole exchange felt kind of pointless. But at least I was free of the anxiety of having no clue what was going on around me.

My food problem had a solution in sight, and now that I knew the power wasn't coming back, it would probably be really hard to make my living conditions any better than they were.

Now that I understood the situation, I could more or less see where my life was headed.

I bet it's going to be the same old thing, day after day, forever.

To stay off the radar of dangerous people, I'll let all my wonderful magic wands gather dust, lie low day after day, grow old, and eventually die quietly.

I'm all for dying alone.

But it's a real shame that I'll never get to show my work to the world.

Ugh! Guess life doesn't go your way on everything, huh?

Oh well. I'll call it a win that my dream of dying alone is looking more realistic.

No.

Wait a second?

I'd shouldered my hiking backpack, picked up Hendensho, and gotten ready to head home when the thought suddenly hit me.

I froze like a statue, all my focus on the idea that had just flashed into my head, and the Blue Witch gave me a puzzled look.

Right.

The Blue Witch.

Here she was, dutifully putting up with my full-on social-misfit refusal to talk.

She seemed willing to go along with a few unreasonable requests, maybe because she thought fixing the music box was such a huge favor.

Wouldn't hurt to ask, right?

For a good few minutes I stood there frozen, fretting. Would a rude request offend her? Was it too pushy to ask on the day we met? What if she said no and got mad?

In the end, though, I decided to at least ask.

After all, the Blue Witch had waited quietly and patiently, without a word, the whole time I'd been frozen in place.

She did owe me one, but you don't often find someone this kind to a guy with zero social skills.

Actually, she was the first I'd ever met. Even my own parents blew up and cut me off when I told them, "I want to live without ever meeting anyone, so I'll make my living off online auctions."

Either way, I figured that if I could pitch this idea to anyone, it was her.

"Um, I've got a favor to ask."

The Blue Witch tilted her head slightly, prompting me to go on.

Not expecting much, I asked.

"Would you be the sales rep for my magic wands?"

Truth was, even in my online-auction days, I hadn't managed to avoid people a hundred percent.

When I bought something, a delivery company brought it to my door and left it there. When I sold something, a company would likewise come pick up the package I'd left outside.

I never met anyone face-to-face, but my online-auction business only worked thanks to all sorts of companies.

I'll have the Blue Witch take over what those companies did.

I'll stay shut in and make magic wands.

The Blue Witch will collect the finished pieces, keep their source hidden, advertise them, negotiate with customers, sell them, and bring me the proceeds (in supplies).

That way, I can stay shut in at home and lose myself in my hobby!!!

If this stroke of genius had a flaw, it was that the Blue Witch had no reason to take on such a ridiculously tedious chore. But she happily agreed, on the condition that I make her a wand of her own for free.

That's a dream deal for me too.

When a famous person uses your stuff, y'know, it's seriously great advertising!

The Blue Witch governs the city of Ome single-handedly and apparently has connections with other witches, so she's presumably famous. If I make her a wand, then every time she pulls off something big, the wand's reputation goes up with it. That's the plan, anyway. Heck, just carrying it around will work as advertising.

I don't want my own face or name getting out, but I want word of how great my wands are to spread far and wide.

To that end, I'll graciously make her the finest magic wand around, one worthy of the Blue Witch.

With the Blue Witch's magic stone in my care, I set to work processing it the moment I got home.

Hendensho, carved from a marble-sized Gremlin, had topped out at a double-layered structure.

The double layer had been an experiment in itself, and the stone was so small that trying for three layers would probably have cracked it.

But this blue magic stone was palm-sized: smaller than my Okutameteorite, but still plenty big.

The rough stone was pretty lopsided, but roughly spherical, so carving it down wouldn't waste much.

With this, I can go six layers... no, all the way to seven!

I kept working by candlelight, day and night.

At some point my hands started shaking and my vision went blurry, and I realized I hadn't eaten in 24 hours. I grudgingly headed out to maybe catch a fish or something, only to find a paper bag stuffed with food, plus some firewood, sitting outside the front door.

Huh!? Fairy-san!? Nah, this is definitely the Blue Witch's doing.

Inside were canned corned beef, fresh lettuce in Tupperware, and salted rice balls: about the best nutritional balance you could ask for these days. Rice had to be precious, too, so I was grateful.

With the Blue Witch backing me up like this, I could focus on my work.

Getting back all the time I usually spent gathering food every day was huge.

For three days and three nights, I did almost nothing but eat, sleep, and process the magic stone.

Now and then I felt Fairy-san watching me through the window, but once I hit deep focus, I stopped noticing.

Around the fifth layer, the work got difficult with only the tools I had on hand. Half resigned to it being a lost cause, I wrote the Blue Witch a letter asking for tools, and a day later, exactly what I'd ordered was sitting outside my door.

Seriously, what a lifesaver. She'd deliver anything—it was like delivery service had come back just for me.

In the end, all the processing and assembly, handle, joint, and protective materials included, took seven days. Massaging my rock-hard shoulders, I wrapped the finished wand in a simple layer of paper and left it at the front door. Job finally done.

Man, what a job. My original estimate had been two weeks, and I'd told her so by slipping a written estimate into the empty Tupperware when I left it outside to return it. But between getting better at the work and developing a new technique, I'd cut the schedule way down.

Mm-hm, mm-hm. I made a good piece, and I learned a lot, too.

The Blue Witch's custom wand, made from the processed blue magic stone, was a masterpiece: the culmination of every technique I'd developed so far.

It looked like a plain wooden wand, but the wood was tough, so it was sturdier than it looked and basically unbreakable under normal use.

I went all out on the design, too. I carved shallow Celtic-motif patterns into the wood and inlaid them with silver to make them stand out. For its name, I engraved "Cyanos," the Greek word for "blue" and the coolest of all the foreign words for it I'd looked up.

Every bit of design and ornament was arranged to show off the beautiful blue gem crowning the tip of the handle. I even mixed blue dye into the bonding agent so it would match the look of the whole piece.

No craftsman but me could make a better magic wand than this. Heh, how about that?

I left the Blue Wand Cyanos at the front door with a delivery spec sheet, and when I checked the next morning, it was gone.

I really hoped the Blue Witch would put Cyanos to work and do great things with it.

Though I'd be just as happy if she put it on display to admire.
