Three months had passed since the giant kaiju freezing incident.

I was still living my shut-in life in Okutama. The Blue Witch kept dutifully delivering food and supplies, so I went hunting far less often and spent more time tending my rice paddy instead.

There were still canned goods and preserved food left over from before civilization collapsed. Ironically, the population had dropped so far that consumption stayed low, and the stockpiles left in the cities hadn't been eaten through yet.

But they would run out someday. A stable food supply was a major challenge.

If this rice paddy worked out, it would at least solve my own food problem. Besides, I was sick of year-old and two-year-old rice. I wanted fresh rice for once.

I'd also found some chickens gone feral in the mountains, escaped from who knew where, and tried raising them, but they just escaped again, so that was a bust. When I found a feather stuck to the top of my two-meter fence, I despaired.

They'd put on a face like, "Our wings are stunted, we can't fly," and then the bastards flew off! They tricked me! Damn them!

Some things had worked out, though.

For one, my fishing had improved a lot. I used to rely on a rod or set traps, but now I'd learned to catch fish with magic.

When I spotted the shadow of a big fish, I'd blast it with a magic beam. The stunned fish would float up, and I'd scoop it out with a net. That was all there was to it.

It was simple, but effective.

According to the Blue Witch, I was on the high side when it came to magic power: about 1/200 to 1/250 of what she had.

Ever since the Gremlin Disaster brought down human civilization, every person had come to have some magic power, whether a little or a lot.

One theory held that people had always had magic power and it had simply become observable, but the leading theory was that people gained it when Gremlins reacted to their bioelectric current.

Every human body carried a bioelectric current. It was a truly tiny amount of electricity, but the strength of a person's bioelectricity was clearly linked to the size of their magic power.

Apparently, an electrically charged constitution like the Blue Witch's generated huge amounts of magic power and mutated its owner into a Transcendent, but at a price: Gremlins crystallized inside the body and destroyed it from within. So I was lucky to have only a moderate amount of magic power.

Witch-level magic power sounded great, but I didn't want it badly enough to risk my life.

Anyway.

I spent my days learning about a world gone fantastical and working hard in the fields.

Then one day, when the Blue Witch came by for Cyanos's regular maintenance, I told her about a monster that had moved into the wild wasabi patch up the stream. She agreed to kill it, just like that.

She'd taken down a giant kaiju bigger than a building when it came ashore in Tokyo, so some small-fry monster making a nuisance of itself out in the sticks was probably nothing to her.

Even so, she didn't hesitate at all. Part of that was probably her strength, but her whole mindset was different, too. Three months ago she'd wiped out Okutama's monsters at breakneck speed, and it was obvious she'd gotten completely used to fighting for her life, something that should have been alien to anyone living in modern Japan.

The Blue Witch and I were as far apart in power as heaven and earth, but we both worked magic the same way, by swinging a wand and chanting an incantation. Figuring I might learn something useful, I asked if I could tag along and watch her deal with the monster, and once again she said OK without much thought.

"I'll keep an eye on you, but look after yourself as much as you can."

"Don't underestimate me. I can use magic too. I can at least defend myself."

"Your magic is practically the weakest there is, got it? Don't get cocky."

The Blue Witch carried Cyanos and I carried Hendensho as we climbed the mountain path along the stream, chatting about this and that.

After a stretch without rain, the little stream got so low that it disappeared under the mossy rocks and undergrowth. The midsummer cicadas were screaming in full chorus, drowning out both the rustle of the forest and the sound of the water, and shoving that same old muggy summer feeling from before the Gremlin Disaster in my face whether I liked it or not.

The Blue Witch, though, seemed to be using some kind of magic, because it was nice and cool anywhere near her.

Just what you'd expect from an ice-type witch. I'd love to keep her around all summer as a replacement for my late AC-kun.

About ten minutes up the stream, we reached its source. Clear spring water seeped from a crack in a low cliff to form a small pond, and lush green wasabi plants spread their leaves around it.

The days when you could buy tubes of wasabi online were long gone. Now this wild patch was a precious source of spice, and I wasn't about to let some monster have it.

"Not here? No, it's hiding somewhere."

The monster that had taken over this spot looked like a turtle the size of a wild dog, with a freakishly overgrown tail. The last time I'd seen it, it had been sunning itself by the water. The instant it spotted me, it let out a shriek, took control of the pond water, and tried to slam it into me. When I fired magic back, its tough shell just took the hit. It was a real nuisance, and I hadn't been able to beat it.

No matter how many monsters you killed or drove off, more kept popping up. With ordinary animals turning into monsters overnight, there was no keeping up. Still, killing one would stop the damage for now.

Wand at the ready, I kept a careful watch on our surroundings, searching for the pond's occupant wherever it was lurking.

The Blue Witch, meanwhile, just stood there holding Cyanos, not tense in the slightest.

"Ori, it's up in that tree. It's watching us from the base of the thickest branch."

"Huh!?"

I looked where the Blue Witch had carelessly jerked her chin, and sure enough, there was the turtle monster, the bumps and colors of its shell blending perfectly into the bark.

Whoa!? How'd she even spot that thing?

"I'm clear to kill it, then?"

"Yeah. But seriously, how did you find it? I thought I was pretty good at spotting creatures in the forest too."

"I can sense magic power. That monster hid its body, but not its magic power."

The Blue Witch summed up her secret for finding enemies in a line and raised Cyanos. She was ready to go, apparently.

Very well. Then let's see the magical combat of the great Blue Witch-sama, who supposedly slew a giant kaiju in a single blow.

While I watched, holding my breath, the Blue Witch kicked hard off the ground and shot nearly ten meters into the air in an instant.

She used the momentum of the leap to spin her body and landed a vicious somersault kick on the turtle monster lying low on its branch.

Her boot sliced through the air in a flash, and the turtle monster blew apart like a popped balloon.

With a single kick, the Blue Witch had killed the monster that made an ordinary Wand Maker throw in the towel. She floated down onto the leaf mold carpeting the ground, and while I stood there dumbstruck, she said simply, "Done."

"O-Oh. Nice work. You're a witch, but you didn't use magic...?"

"No need. Spending magic power on a monster a jump and a kick can handle is a waste."

"Y-Yeah."

She was a woman people called a witch, so part of me had expected a fantasy-style battle, with her wielding a magic wand gracefully and fighting smart.

The reality, as you can see, was this.

It's a smart way to fight, sure, not wasting any magic power, but something about it just doesn't sit right with me.

I mean, talk about meathead brute force. Is Blue Witch-sama perhaps a <ruby>gorilla<rt>forest sage</rt></ruby>[^1]?

"What's with that face? I killed the monster like you asked."

"No, you really helped me out. Seriously. As thanks, I'll feed you some char marinated in wasabi soy sauce next time. But, see, I was hoping to pick up some pointers on fighting with magic. Then you showed me something totally useless for that, so I'm kind of bummed."

When I told her how I really felt, the Blue Witch seemed to get it.

"Oh. If that's what you wanted, you should've said so sooner. Your noisy magic that sounds like a screaming beaver is next to useless in a real fight. Want me to teach you some new magic? Something even you could handle."

"Seriously? Please do. Then write me up a concise report, about three A4 pages, on practical magic even I can use, and submit it."

"Looks like before I teach you magic, I need to teach you how to act when you're asking someone to teach you..."

I tried to push for doing this in writing, pointing out how handy it would be to reread later or pass on to other people, but the Blue Witch couldn't be bothered to write it all up.

Why did people always insist on doing things face-to-face? It was beyond me, but the person doing the teaching said a spoken lesson was easier, so what could I do?

I didn't want to annoy the Blue Witch and have her stop teaching me magic, so as soon as we got down the mountain, I set up a shooting target in the backyard and sat obediently through Sensei's lesson.

The Blue Witch was apparently going to teach me the most basic freezing spell, "Freeze[^2]," and an attack spell derived from it, "Freezing Javelin[^3]."

I held Hendensho at the ready and the Blue Witch went bare-handed, and we each took aim at a target drawn on cardboard and hung from a tree branch.

"Vaaah-ra!"

"Wrong. Vaa-ra. Don't draw out the 'Va.' Pronounce 'Va,' then a separate 'a.'"

"Vaa-ra?"

"Closer. Now the pitch. The best way is to listen and learn—Vaa-ra!"

The Blue Witch snapped out the incantation as a demonstration, and a pale-blue freezing beam shot from her outstretched palm and struck the target. It froze in an instant, glazed with thin ice and giving off white mist.

I clapped, impressed, and the Blue Witch gave a little hmph.

"This is as basic as it gets. Come on, pronunciation practice. Repeat after me: Vaa-ra."

"Vaa-ra."

"Again. Vaa-ra."

"Vaa-ra. This kind of reminds me of English class. It feels more like an actual language than an incantation."

"Of course. It's magic language."

"There's a magic language?"

When I asked out of curiosity, the Blue Witch could tell my focus was shot. She said, "Let's take a little break," and sat down on a log. I took the stump across from her.

Hands-on practice was on hold. Now it was time for a theory lecture.

"I'm not an expert either, but according to linguists who analyze incantations, it's a proper language with its own grammar and context."

"Huh. So where'd you learn magic language, Blue Witch?"

"Every witch and mage—in other words, every human whose body mutates into a Transcendent—wakes from the coma that follows the mutation already knowing the magic that suits them. It's a matter of feel, like realizing you've learned to swim or ride a bike at some point without noticing... It's hard to explain."

"No, I kind of get it."

She probably meant it was acquired rather than inborn, learned by feel, and never forgotten once you had it.

"I don't know why incantations get etched into our heads, but monsters also seem able to use magic that suits them right after they mutate, so I think the same principle is at work."

"So monsters are born knowing magic language?"

"It's the other way around. Magic language incorporates the sounds monsters make. For example, the Vaa-ra we're practicing means 'Freeze,' but according to the linguists, that meaning was assigned to a particular set of sounds."

"...?"

"What I mean is, say 'Vaa-ra,' and things freeze. So they went, 'Let's treat this pronunciation as meaning Freeze and fit it into the language system.' That seems to be the order the magic language system got built in."

"Um, so it's like translating the 'Eek!' people scream when they're scared as 'I'm scared!'?"

"More or less. Probably."

The Blue Witch gave a half-hearted nod.

She didn't seem to know all that much about it herself. Well, she had said it was all by feel.

"Transcendents can control magic power, so even if they pronounce an incantation correctly, they can keep magic from going off by accident. But you're not a mage, Ori. If you pronounce an incantation correctly with a Gremlin or magic stone nearby, the magic will activate on its own even if you don't mean it to, so be careful."

"Wait, that'd be bad if I accidentally chanted one mid-conversation."

"Magic-language pronunciation is completely different from Japanese. That almost never happens. Also, magic language contains at least seven sounds that only a Transcendent's mutated throat can produce. They're especially common in advanced magic."

"Sounds I can't pronounce? Those exist? I kinda want to hear one."

"Let's see... Let moonlight and cool breezes alike all become ice[^4]."

The Blue Witch chanted it lightly, like a chirping bird, and laid her palm on the log she was sitting on. The whole log turned to ice, keeping its exact shape.

Seriously? Holy crap! That isn't alchemy—it's ice-chemy!

"Ah. Sorry, was this log for firewood?"

"No, it's fine, it's fine. Don't worry about it. That's amazing!"

That was a real treat to see. When I gave her a big round of applause, she looked pretty pleased with herself this time.

Makes sense. Witch-only incantations, huh? Lucky.

But I've got an original incantation too—or rather, an original scream.

"Does my Okutameteorite natural frequency incantation mean something in magic linguistics too?"

"Natural... what?"

"My magic that shoots a white beam. The one with the incantation that sounds like a screaming beaver."

"Oh, that. Hmm, hard to say. Judging by the incantation and the effect, I'd guess it's close to the Northern Witch's shooting magic. But like I said, everything I just told you is secondhand. I'm no expert on magic language myself. I just know a few incantations, that's all."

"I see..."

"If you're that interested, want me to get you some materials from someone who researches incantations?"

"Seriously? Please do."

I nodded at the Blue Witch's offer without a second thought.

Knowledge of magic language was bound to be great inspiration for making magic wands.

"All right. I can't bring it until after the next Witches' Council meeting, so... wait five days."

Score! Five days can't come soon enough!

## Translator Notes

[^1]: The ruby's baseline “gorilla” undercuts its grand upper gloss, “forest sage.”

[^2]: The written spell name means “Freeze,” while its spoken magic-language form is `Vaa-ra`; the pronunciation lesson distinguishes it from Ori's mistaken elongated version.

[^3]: The source writes “Freezing Javelin” but gives its spoken incantation as `ドウ・ヴアアラー`.

[^4]: The source gives the spoken form as `××・××フイフイ・イイヴアアラー`; the `××` marks sounds ordinary humans cannot pronounce. The English renders the written meaning without supplying those redacted sounds.
