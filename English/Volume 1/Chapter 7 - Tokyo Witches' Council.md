"I'm so glad you're joining us, Ao-chan. I wish I could've put out some tea and snacks for you. I'll send some over next time."

"No need. I'm the one who asked to join remotely."

It was the day of the Witches' Council, and the Blue Witch was attending from home through one of the Eyeball Witch's familiars.

These days, the gathering of witches who controlled the heart of Japan was called the Tokyo Witches' Council, but it had started out as a loose get-together in the chaos right after the Gremlin Disaster.

It started with the Eyeball Witch. She'd wanted to know what was happening in the world and what had happened to her, so she set up a place where people in the same situation could meet and trade information regularly.

Once the Bloodsucking Mage joined, it turned more political. They divided Tokyo into districts and drew clear lines around each witch's and mage's territory. By reassigning them to keep things balanced, they stopped the strongest people, the ones who could actually keep order, from clustering in one place, and public safety across Tokyo improved enormously.

They'd made plenty of other useful political decisions, too. They set up a system for calling in other witches when a monster turned out to be a bad matchup, and an exchange where districts could trade for supplies they were short on, like food, medicine, and fuel. They sorted out the refugees pouring in from Saitama and Chiba, and they gathered engineers and medical workers with plans to preserve their skills and put them to use.

When the Iruma Mage staged his coup, they crushed it, at heavy cost, and even managed to turn the victory into public support.

But most witches and mages were clueless about politics.

They were just ordinary people who'd been handed power. Hunting monsters was simple enough: kill them with magic and you were done. Politics, built on a tangle of competing interests, was a lot harder.

The Bloodsucking Mage had set the pace and kept everyone in line. When he died and left the stage, attendance at the Witches' Council plummeted.

The witches clashed more and more, the friction piled up, and a number of ongoing projects were halted or put off.

At its peak, the Witches' Council had gathered six mages and twenty witches. Now it was down to one mage and eighteen witches.

Only six of them showed up to the regular meetings.

After the Bloodsucking Mage's death, the Eyeball Witch had taken over as coordinator of the Witches' Council. To put it kindly, she valued harmony; to put it unkindly, she couldn't make a decision.

She knew it herself: she could have handled something like PTA president in peaceful times, but holding together a Witches' Council that decided Tokyo's fate was more than she could carry.

Still, nobody was better suited to the job. Keep holding Witches' Council meetings regularly, keep up at least the appearance of cooperation, and stop things from getting any worse—that alone took everything she had.

Since the Blue Witch never left Ome, she'd only been a regular at the Witches' Council in Shinjuku back when it first formed.

Today's remote appearance was her first since the emergency meeting held in Akiruno right after the giant kaiju invasion incident.

"I'm not here to play politics. Once I'm done with Foresight, I'm out."

"Really? You don't have to say anything. Couldn't you at least listen to the meeting? I'm sure you'll hear something that's good for you, Ao-chan."

"I'm good. Foresight, are you there?"

There was a pause after the Blue Witch called out. Someone hissed, "Answer her! It's the Blue Witch asking!" and then a weary middle-aged man's voice answered.

"I'm here. Unfortunately."

"Just one thing, and it'll be quick. You've been sheltering a magic language researcher, haven't you?"

"...You planning to kill them?"

"Of course not! I don't kill people at random. Lend me the materials they've got. The magic language ones."

"Magic language research hasn't come together at all yet. What would you even use the materials for?"

"Studying magic language. What else?"

"Hah! You've never shown a shred of interest before, so color me suspicious! Far be it from me to defy the wishes of Blue Witch-sama, but lending you the materials is going to be difficult. Unfortunately."

"Why?"

"There's only one complete set of the original research materials. The copier doesn't work. If I lend them out and they get lost, I'm in trouble."

The Foresight Mage had a point: these were confidential documents with no copies, and anyone who handed those out without a second thought would be the strange one.

But the Blue Witch needed him to lend them. She had promised Ori she'd bring him materials on magic language.

"You don't need to worry about Ao-chan. She always gives back what she borrows."

The Eyeball Witch chimed in to back her up, but the Foresight Mage ignored her and kept going.

"If you want them that badly, I need collateral. This is the secret of a huge project run by the Bloodsucking Mage's prized protégé, one that's going to revolutionize the world someday. If I'm lending that to you and you alone, I need collateral."

"Hmph. Fine. Let's see, then... How about food and water for a thousand people for a week?"

"Don't make me laugh. I want the magic wand Cyanos in exchange."

"What?"

Her voice dropped low before she could stop it, and that was when she realized.

The Foresight Mage had wanted Cyanos all along. When the Blue Witch asked for the magic language materials, he'd just seen his chance and jumped on it.

The Blue Witch clicked her tongue.

"Didn't that Foresight you're so proud of show you a future where I say no? I told you again and again at the emergency meeting. I'm not handing Cyanos over to anyone. I'm not lending it out, either."

"I heard you. But listen to me, Blue Witch. I've been saying this over and over at the Witches' Council meetings you weren't at."

With that preface, his listless middle-aged voice found some strength.

"At this rate, two years from now, food shortages are going to cause a famine like nothing we've ever seen. Even next year, nobody will actually starve to death, but people will be dropping from malnutrition left and right."

"Which is why you're pushing the agriculture, forestry, and fisheries reforms."

"I knew it. You don't get it. Even if everything goes according to plan, it won't be anywhere near enough.

"Listen. You know farm machinery stopped working, right? That alone tanked production efficiency. Depending on the crop, it's down anywhere from 20 to 75%.

"Food imports stopped. That one hurts too. Japan lost 62% of its food supply.

"Chemical fertilizer is down a full 100%. We can't farm on the assumption of plentiful fertilizer anymore.

"The roads are cut off, so freight trains and delivery trucks aren't running. Crops grown in the breadbasket regions can't reach the cities anymore.

"The average age of the people working in farming, forestry, and fisheries was 68. With monsters running wild, medical care stopped, and so did heating and air conditioning in summer and winter. The elderly were the first to die. The people who knew how the work was done were all but wiped out."

The Foresight Mage rattled off the grim state of Japan's food production, backing every point with numbers.

Even the Blue Witch got serious. She'd been sitting on a huge food stockpile all by herself (or with one other person, counting Ori) and had never felt much alarm about the food problem.

The post-apocalyptic age after the fall of human civilization—an age of looting and of the old civilization's lingering echoes—was coming to an end.

If they didn't build industries fit for the new era, and fast, an already exhausted humanity would take another beating, this time from hunger.

"There's more.

"All of Japan's roughly 100,000 fishing boats are broken. The fishing industry's wiped out. That's 2.5 million tons of annual catch gone, food that should have fed three million people. Add up whatever's left—river fishing, sea fishing, fish farms, yachts and boats—and it's still a drop in the bucket.

"Even the farmland, stables, and fish farms that are somehow still producing get devoured by monsters. Funny, huh? By the time we sense a monster's shown up, get there, and kill it, the precious food's already in its stomach. Deer, monkeys, sparrows—ordinary pests like that are cute by comparison. The newly cleared farmland in Katsushika Ward got stripped bare.

"Seed and nursery companies literally got crushed, and hundreds of high-yield varieties were lost. That damage can't be undone. It probably doesn't mean much to you, but anyone with time to guard the National Diet Building should've been guarding even one of those companies instead.

"About 80% of Tokyo's population died, so the food we need dropped 80% too, but food production fell by a lot more than 80%. Listen—"

"Enough. I get it."

Fed up, the Blue Witch cut off the depressing lecture.

It had been her own choice to hole up alone in Ome and ignore the problems outside. Even so, hearing how bad things were spelled out all over again left her feeling bleak.

She'd brought down the giant kaiju single-handedly and felt as if she'd saved Tokyo. But faced with the food crisis he said was coming in two years, maybe Tokyo's end had only ever been a question of sooner or later.

"That's why I need your wand."

The Foresight Mage's voice lost its strength again, as if all that talking had worn him out.

She'd always thought he seemed worn out, but given the size of the problem he was taking on, it made sense.

"We need power. Firepower, manpower, some unknown new energy source—anything. We need a convenient superpower that can fix everything in one go, right now.

"We only have two years left.

"I paid a ridiculous price to learn fertility magic from the Flower Witch. All I need now is for you to lend me Cyanos. Then I can amplify the fertility magic, spread it everywhere, and send food production through the roof. We can change that shitty future where over three million people starve to death around Tokyo alone."

"..."

The Blue Witch was torn.

If Ome's people had still been alive, and someone had begged her to do it for Ome's 130,000, she would have hesitated, but in the end she would have lent him Cyanos.

But three million strangers she'd never met just didn't feel real to her.

The Blue Witch was the kind of woman who would stay up three days and nights nursing her sick little sister, and also the kind who never put a single yen in a convenience-store donation box.

Maybe her silence had rattled him, because the Foresight Mage pressed on desperately.

"I understand, I really do, that you're worried Cyanos will be misused. I get why you're hesitating, but I need it no matter what. At this point I don't even care where Cyanos came from. I absolutely won't misuse it. I'll give it back, guaranteed. So please! Lend me Cyanos!"

"The Edogawa Witch lent a magic stone to the Iruma Mage when he said something like that, and he killed her the moment she handed it over."

"Ah..."

Through the Eyeball Witch's familiar, the Blue Witch could practically see the middle-aged man clutching his head and groaning.

"Unfortunately, I can't argue with that. That brat left us the worst possible precedent and then went and died... No! Even so, please! All I can say is trust me, but I absolutely won't misuse it. I swear. Oh, I know. If you want to study magic language, I'll send the researcher over instead of the materials. You could even hold them hostage if you— No, that's not okay, is it. That's going too far."

The Foresight Mage had nearly crossed a line, but he caught himself just in time.

He'd decided he couldn't take one person hostage, even to save three million lives. No wonder he'd been close to the Bloodsucking Mage.

Going purely by the numbers, it was a foolish call, but the Blue Witch thought a little better of him for it.

She thought it over and offered a compromise.

"I'll think up a solution to the food problem on my end. I can't lend you Cyanos, but can we call it settled with that?"

"You'll think one up? Come on."

"I've got something in mind. I didn't pull Cyanos out of thin air."

The Blue Witch was hinting at a trump card to buy his trust, stopping just short of revealing that Ori existed, and her words left the Foresight Mage agonizing for a good while.

But true to his name, the Foresight Mage could see the future. To look ahead at the outcome of his choice, he recited an incantation.

"<ruby>××× Kunatsuku<rt>Tell me</rt></ruby>[^1], <ruby>Subasuhasu Toshiyachia<rt>will there be a full moon next month?</rt></ruby>"

The Foresight Mage boasted that he could see the future, but nobody except him knew what he actually saw, or how far.

There was no telling what that spell had just shown him.

The Blue Witch had no intention of going back on her word, so he shouldn't have seen any future that was bad for him.

Still, the future wasn't set in stone, so the Blue Witch asked the man who'd just seen it what he'd found.

"What did you see?"

"It kinda looks okay. So it's a promise, then."

She was dumbstruck by his slurred, babyish voice.

At first she thought someone else was talking, but none of the other witches, who had to be listening right there, said a thing. Apparently that really was his voice, changed beyond recognition.

"Hey, what's wrong? Are you even okay?"

"Eahh... me fine!"

"You definitely got hit with magic backlash..."

The Flame Witch's voice came next, gently coaxing the middle-aged guy like a toddler: "Okay, let's go to the break room now. There's a good boy." The Foresight Mage's voice faded into the distance.

The Witches' Council members, who had listened to the whole exchange in silence, started whispering among themselves, and over the murmuring came the Eyeball Witch's voice, openly moved.

"Hey, Ao-chan. Won't you come to the meetings every month after all? I haven't seen a negotiation wrap up this peacefully in ages. You both said what you thought, heard each other out, and met in the middle. Onee-san's getting all choked up."

"I'm not coming again... probably. Later."

Before any more talk could drag her into some messy political drama, the Blue Witch cut the conversation short and tossed the relaying eyeball familiar out the window.

The food problem was serious. But if she talked it over with Ori, she was sure he'd figure something out.

After all, he was the man who had made Cyanos, a magic wand that held the power to transform the world.
