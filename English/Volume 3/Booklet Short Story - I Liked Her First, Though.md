One afternoon, a few days after the Daidarabocchi hunt.

A summons from his superior brought Murakumo Kariya to a reception room in the Sendai Toshogu[^1] shrine office. Bears, griffons, and other hunting trophies were solemnly displayed around the room. Only <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> was there. He gestured to a sofa and gently urged Murakumo to sit.

“Thanks for coming. Sit down. Make yourself comfortable.”

“Yes...”

Murakumo kept his twitching hand from flipping him off and obediently sat on the sofa across from <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>.

Murakumo hated the handsome bastard and his languid air. Whenever their eyes met, he wanted to rip out that flashy, overgrown hair at the back of <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>'s head. All because <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> had stolen <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby> from him.

Of course he couldn't like the man who had stolen the woman he liked.

He knew full well that all this irritation was just a selfish grudge. Those dark feelings came from the resentment and jealousy of a pathetic loser in love. His pride wouldn't let him do anything as lame as take a grudge over a woman out on <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>, and he wasn't stupid enough to snap at his superior over a private matter.

That didn't mean he could simply shrug it off. I only have myself to blame for losing <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby>, so I should try to get along with <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>? Not happening. Murakumo's feelings were complicated.

He'd wanted to stay away because he knew seeing <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> would only make him miserable. Yet here they were, face-to-face.

“About your resignation letter.”

True to the Tohoku Hunting Association's no-nonsense ethos, <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> got straight to the point.

“You listed your reason for leaving as ‘personal circumstances.’ But the Tohoku Hunting Association very much wants an excellent wizard like you to keep working with us.”

“I'm grateful to hear that, but...”

“If those ‘personal circumstances’ are something we can resolve, we'll take care of them. In short, we're asking you to stay. What do you say?”

“...”

The awkwardness nearly killed Murakumo, and he said nothing.

<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>'s eyes were dead serious.

He clearly thought something serious had driven Murakumo to this.

The Tohoku Hunting Association governed the Tohoku region from Sendai. Five Transcendents stood at the top, with various administrative bodies beneath them. Murakumo had worked directly under the Transcendents as a Daidarabocchi watcher for about four years. He was highly rated as an excellent watcher (wizard): with him, a two-person team could do the work of three.

Watching Daidarabocchi was dangerous work.

A long-term assignment tracking a powerful monster that could kill even a Transcendent frayed the nerves. Plenty of people had broken under the stress and quit.

Yet Murakumo had stayed at his post alone, calmly filing detailed reports and brushing aside the assorted monsters that sometimes appeared near the watchtower. To an outside observer, he must have looked like an exceptional employee: patient, levelheaded, and skilled in combat.

After lasting four whole years on Daidarabocchi watch duty, surely he could handle future assignments with even more room to spare. That was how it should have been.

But Murakumo had submitted his resignation letter.

<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> seemed to think someone as capable as Murakumo would only quit for a very serious reason.

He was wrong.

The truth was that Murakumo was a mage—a Transcendent who used incredibly precise magic-power control to pass perfectly as an ordinary person.

A Daidarabocchi watch assignment harsh enough to break an ordinary person with stress was easy with a Transcendent's specs.

As far as Murakumo was concerned, <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>'s high opinion of him was completely misplaced. Sure, he was exceptional for an ordinary person. For a Transcendent, though, he was just a coward who hid his strength and quietly coasted through an easy job.

There was no grand reason behind Murakumo's resignation.

He only wanted to quit because the woman he had a crush on had gotten together with his direct superior, and working in the same place had become painful.

In other words, this was the sorry end of a workplace romance.

And there was no way he could tell his direct superior—<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>—why he was really leaving.

Misreading Murakumo's long silence, <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> spoke gently.

“We may have relied too much on you. We kept increasing your workload just because you could handle it alone. I'm sorry. Now that Daidarabocchi has been defeated and we have some breathing room, we can bring in more people and spread out the work—”

“No, that isn't it. Um... It's embarrassing, but... there's a woman involved.”

Murakumo cut him off and gave the vaguest answer he could without lying. <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> seemed convinced.

“I see. You're at the age when marriage enters the picture. I'm sorry that didn't occur to me. If you're planning to make things official, I can see why you'd want to leave a dangerous job.”

Murakumo had chosen that answer because he figured it would keep <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> from prying. Besides, this is your fault, so don't you dare pry. <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> seemed to understand.

<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> had clearly read far too much into it and reached the wrong conclusion. But if his romantic rival ever grasped the truth, Murakumo would burst a blood vessel and die. This was fine.

Murakumo seized on the misunderstanding and moved to end the conversation.

“That's right. Of course, with this much magic power, I intend to keep contributing to Tohoku, but I'd like to avoid dangerous work.”

“I see. I understand the situation. Do you already have someone you're seeing?”

“Huh? No, I don't.”

“Then leave it to me. I can introduce you to a woman who understands the demands of your work. My wife, <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby>, is that sort of person, so she can give you advice too.”

“...That's very kind of you, but I'll have to decline.”

Murakumo declined as tactfully as he could while howling inside. How good would it feel to grab <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> by the collar and yell, “Don't brag about stealing her away! I'll deck you!”

He was going to lose his mind. <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> had no idea Murakumo was heartbroken over his wife. He was only sincerely trying to keep an excellent employee from resigning.

Murakumo knew <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> meant no harm. Everything he said was perfectly reasonable. Murakumo was the one being ridiculous, working himself up over a sense of defeat and inferiority.

He knew all that, but he still couldn't bear it.

He'd lost at love. He'd lost as a man. Let him at least play it cool and lose with some dignity.

Screaming his ugly jealousy at <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> would be too pathetic. <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> needed to stop getting on his nerves before some self-important line like “I saved you from Daidarabocchi's clutches, you know” burst out of his mouth.

Murakumo took a deep breath, gathered up his last scraps of composure, and put on a calm face.

<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> still seemed to be searching for another way to persuade him to stay. Murakumo looked anywhere but at him and considered his options.

Objectively, taking <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> up on his offer was probably the right call. No matter how wonderful <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby> was, she was married now. If her husband mistreated her, Murakumo would do whatever it took to beat <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> down. But she looked happy. Stealing her away was out of the question. He had no choice but to give up.

Clinging to <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby> now that she was married wouldn't help anyone. A stalker chasing another man's wife was scum.

If his love had failed, he should choke back his tears, get over it, and find someone new.

Then again, if he could move on so quickly after getting his heart broken and have his superior introduce him to someone, he wouldn't have submitted a resignation letter in the first place.

Hindsight made it easy to say, but workplace romance really was a terrible idea. Brooding over that, Murakumo tried to bring the conversation to a close.

“Anyway. I—”

Before he could continue, the door to the reception room opened, and the cutest woman in the world poked her head in.

Murakumo stopped mid-sentence. His heart nearly stopped too.

“Hey, <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>. About mailing the Daidarabocchi Gremlin—”

“—Oh, Murakumo-san.”

<ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby> gave the frozen Murakumo a small nod.

“Were you in the middle of something? Sorry, I'll come back later.”

“No, no. Nothing important at all. Please, go ahead.”

“Are you sure...?”

He hoped his panic hadn't shown on his face.

<ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby> slipped into the room, briefly spoke with <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> about the Daidarabocchi Gremlin to be mailed to the Wand Maker in Tokyo, and left again without lingering. On her way out, she gave Murakumo a polite, noncommittal smile that was clearly nothing like the ones she gave her husband.

Once he was alone with that bastard again, Murakumo spoke in total resignation.

“Um, as I was saying. Anyway, I understand your point, <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>-san. I'll withdraw my resignation letter.”

“Oh! That would be a big help!”

<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> welcomed that sudden about-face without questioning it.

Seeing <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby> one more time, hearing her voice, being in her presence—it drove the truth home.

If falling in love meant losing, Murakumo had raised the white flag and surrendered unconditionally long ago.

Even her distant customer-service smile was so unbearably cute that he'd fallen for her all over again.

He could never forget her. <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby>'s heart belonged to <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>, while Murakumo's belonged to <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby>.

He had no choice but to keep his feelings hidden forever and carry on this one-sided love.

His desire to see <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby>'s face, even if only now and then, had grown far stronger than his desire not to see <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>'s.

“My conditions for staying are, first, that you not give me too many dangerous assignments. Second, even though I brought the subject up, that you stay out of my love life.”

“Of course. I'm sorry I overstepped. I won't pry into your private life.”

<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> held out his hand, and the two shook hands, both smiling pleasantly.

Murakumo returned the firm handshake, smiling on the outside and crying on the inside.

All the while, he thought, Ah, I liked her first, though.
