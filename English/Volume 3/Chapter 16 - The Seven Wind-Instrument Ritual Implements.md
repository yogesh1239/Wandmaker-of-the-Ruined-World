Unpronounceable sounds appeared in incantations but couldn't be produced by the human voice. According to the Yoshida Conjecture, there were twelve in all, nine of which had been confirmed so far.

Unpronounceable sounds were a major obstacle to humans imitating the magic of witches and mages. Researchers had been trying to get around that obstacle ever since Professor Ohinata’s father led the field of magic linguistics.

Of all those approaches, using musical instruments to produce unpronounceable sounds had a long history.

The idea was to use instruments to produce sounds humans could not make. Early researchers tried string and percussion instruments too, but the results were poor and the theory held little promise. Now they focused solely on wind instruments, which produced sound from a person's breath.

Certain wind instruments could produce one unpronounceable sound, and only one.

Researchers with absolute pitch had found that the harmonica could perfectly imitate one of the unpronounceable sounds made by witches and mages.

But until now, producing an unpronounceable sound with a harmonica had been useless. The sound didn't stir any magic power, so no magic activated.

Reciting the spell normally, then putting the harmonica to your mouth only for the unpronounceable sound, didn't work either.

Magic incantations required extremely precise pronunciation. If an unnatural pause came between the sounds of one continuous incantation, that alone made the incantation fail.

My Thirteen Ritual Implements had opened a path through that problem.

As one application of the Thirteen Ritual Implements, six people could recite the first half and the remaining six the second half, making it possible to complete a single spell.

Of course, if the handoff between the first and second halves was off by even a tiny bit, the spell failed. It was an advanced technique that required extremely precise coordination, but it solved half the harmonica problem.

There was no need for one person to frantically switch between reciting aloud and moving a harmonica to and from their mouth. They could perform it as ritual magic instead.
