<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>, who had come from the Tohoku Hunting Association, brought three new technologies to Tokyo.

One was secret sauce, a magical concoction that made monster meat edible—meat you couldn't eat whether you boiled it or grilled it.

Another was the Monster Trap, a time-stasis trap magic tool I'd put to use in Himori Wand.

And the last was a monster-material processing method.

Normally, monsters began deteriorating as materials as soon as they died. Their magic power drained away over time, taking their magical properties with it and leaving them brittle and weak. Depending on the material, they lost all their magic power and became useless within a month at most.

The Tohoku Hunting Association had developed self-blood tanning to prevent that magic-power deterioration.

Between eating monster meat, magic traps, and material processing, the old hands in Tohoku really had built their magic technology around hunting. If Tokyo was on the wizard tech tree, Tohoku was on the hunter tree. Trading technology was paying off big-time. Very nice.

For the past month, I'd been making Himori Wand, messing around with the Monster Trap, and building a big barrel for soaking things in my homemade secret sauce. At last, all of that was wrapped up, and I could start on self-blood tanning.

“All right.”

I pulled on my gloves, stretched my arms overhead, and looked up at the sky.

Self-blood tanning involved a lot of blood, so I did the whole job in the yard instead of the workshop. The sky was cloudy, but the rainy season was still a little ways off and there was no sign of rain. It was shaping up to be a cool, comfortable day for outdoor work.

I was working on a rabbit monster that had been caught in a boar trap. Judging from the rigor mortis, it had probably been dead for about two hours.

I tied on a work apron to protect myself from blood spatter, sat down on the blue tarp I'd spread out, and got straight to work.

First, taking care not to damage the hide, I cut an artery and hung the carcass upside down to drain into a washbasin I'd prepared.

While the blood drained, I dug out the Gremlin and ground it as finely as I could with a mortar and pestle. Then I sprinkled the powder into the washbasin and gave the blood a light stir to distribute it evenly.

Then I butchered the bled-out carcass and separated what I needed from what I didn't. The meat and organs were all useless to me this time. I only needed the hide and bones.

Once I'd made short work of skinning and deboning it, I lowered the hide and bones into the Gremlin-and-blood solution... Except they wouldn't go all the way under. There wasn't enough liquid. Even with weights on top, bits of them still poked above the surface.

I checked the manual again to make sure I had the procedure right and looked up what to do in this situation.

“Uh, step three... If the materials you want to process cannot be fully submerged... dilute the solution with saline up to twice the original liquid volume... If they still cannot be fully submerged, reduce the amount of material until they can... Okay, okay. Saline.”

I had everything I needed on hand. I opened a plastic bottle of saline I'd prepared beforehand and poured it into the washbasin. The materials immediately disappeared beneath the pool of blood. There we go.

With the prep work finished, I set the washbasin on the fire stand and lit a fire beneath it. There wasn't much liquid in the basin, so it came to a boil in about ten minutes.

Once it came to a boil, I took the washbasin off the heat and let it cool slowly on its own.

Once it cooled to around room temperature, the processing would be almost done. Then I just had to pull the materials out of the self-blood solution, rinse off the blood, and dry them.

Hmm. That had been easier than I expected. It hadn't been the kind of difficult job worth bracing myself for. Very bloody, though.

With nothing to do until it finished cooling, I flipped through the Tohoku Hunting Association's book of secret techniques (a copy of the booklet Magic University had compiled from <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>'s oral teachings).

The more I read, the more I felt that how people adapted to the age of magic brought on by the Gremlin Disaster said a lot about their region—or maybe just the people who lived there.

Maybe because Okutameteorite had inspired me to drive Tokyo's technological development, magic-wand technology was flourishing here. Thanks to Professor Ohinata, magic linguistics was too.

Tohoku, by contrast, was led by dyed-in-the-wool hunters who'd started out as actual licensed hunters, so its technology had developed in that direction too.

Processing monster meat was exactly the kind of thing hunters would do, and the Monster Trap was plainly inspired by a bear trap. Self-blood tanning was also close to traditional brain tanning.

It made sense that magic-wand technology had not developed in Tohoku. Self-blood tanning used a monster's Gremlin by smashing it to pieces. To the Tohoku crowd, Gremlins were things to break, crush, and put to use. That was fundamentally different from Tokyo's magic-wand technology, which prized bigger chunks of Gremlin and larger magic stones.

If Tohoku's tech tree had already branched off this far, how different would places even farther away be? If there were this many differences within Japan, what about overseas? Brazil? America? For all we knew, they had established whole systems of magic technology no one in Tokyo had ever imagined.

Of course, that assumed they'd survived the Gremlin Disaster...

From what the Dragon Witch had seen while flying around the Japanese archipelago, Japan had five large, well-known survivor communities that had managed to keep civilization functioning.

Hokkaido's “Hokkaido Magic Beast Farm.”

Tohoku's “Tohoku Hunting Association.”

Tokyo's “Tokyo Witches' Council.”

Shiga's “Lake Biwa Pact.”

Kyushu's “Arataki Group.”

Those were the only five known. No one knew anything at all about the situation overseas.

With electricity gone, civilization must have been set back and thrown into chaos all over the world. How well had other countries managed to cope?

It was entirely possible that, by sheer chance, humanity's survivors had been concentrated in Japan, leaving it as the only remaining civilization on Earth.

Even Japan would have been wiped out by the mushroom pandemic without the Flower Witch. Similar magical disasters might have struck overseas on top of everything else...

At some point, I had stopped turning the pages of the Tohoku Hunting Association's book of secret techniques and gotten lost in thought. The washbasin had cooled completely.

When I lifted the monster materials out of the cooled blood solution and rinsed them with well water, the blood washed away to reveal a beautiful white pelt and white bones.

Yep, they look good. Not that I have enough experience to tell whether I've processed them properly.

Apparently, Transcendents could somehow tell processed monster materials from unprocessed ones with their magic-power sense, even without learning how. Those guys are cheating. I want a magic-power sense like that too.

But there was no point wishing for what I didn't have. I hung the damp monster materials in a shady, well-ventilated spot under the eaves. Then I took a bath, got the smell of blood off, and went right to bed.

They should be dry by morning. I can't wait to see how they turn out...

---

Secret sauce, a new technology passed down from the Tohoku Hunting Association, revolutionized the dinner table.

Now that all the monster meat we'd once thrown away was edible, Tokyo's protein supply improved dramatically. Meat production, which had temporarily fallen due to the mushroom pandemic, rebounded past its pre-pandemic level, and my meals, which had felt a little bare lately, grew lavish again.

I even treated myself to five homemade monster-meat sausages first thing in the morning. I added some asparagus from the yard too, so it was a balanced meal. When I shoveled it all down with white rice, the rich, nourishing flavor spread through my whole body. Eating meat really does make me feel powerful.

After that fine breakfast, I opened the storm shutters with my toothbrush in my mouth and felt the monster materials drying in the shade. They'd dried nicely.

Frankly, the materials from this rabbit monster had no special magical properties to speak of. Its fur never tangled, and its bones rang like glass when struck. That was it.

That was exactly why I wouldn't mind ruining them. They were perfect materials for a self-blood-tanning beginner.

As a memento of the first monster materials I'd processed myself, I hung the pelt and bones on the workshop wall as trophies. Not bad. Not bad at all. My workshop's really coming together, isn't it? I want to keep improving both how it works and how it looks.

While I prayed to Okutameteorite and adjusted the pelt's tension, the Blue Witch sent me a short message through an eyeball familiar.

I stepped outside, and the Blue Witch emerged from beyond the thin Lost Mist blanketing Okutama.

She was casually pulling a large dump truck loaded with monster carcasses, its tow rope gripped in one hand.

“Morning, Ori. I brought them.”

“Y-Yeah. That's incredible.”

She was so absurdly powerful that I gave a strained laugh. Why is she moving a large vehicle so easily? What kind of power is that? Even walking a dog takes more effort than that.

“Where do I put it?”

“Uh, just park it on the street for now. I'll be working in the yard, so pull it up next to the yard.”

“Got it.”

The Blue Witch pulled the massive vehicle over and parked it by the yard as easily as if it were a tricycle, then climbed into the cab and set the parking brake.

I'd only asked her to load some unprocessed monster materials onto a cart and bring them over. I hadn't expected her to use a 10-ton dump truck as the cart. Should I admire her for being a witch or fear her as human heavy machinery?

“Sorry. I'm always asking you to deal with one hassle after another.”

“It's fine. I'm doing it because I want to—because it's you asking, Ori. What's this for?”

“Of course I'm using it to make magic wands.”

“Again? That's all you ever do, Ori.”

“That's a good thing.”

The very best craftsmen never stop improving.

Or what? Am I supposed to coast on my natural dexterity and the technology I already have, slack off, and turn into some outdated fossil? Huh? Of course not!

“Aren't you interested in butchering monsters, Blue Witch?”

“Hmm... Even if they're monsters, I don't like handling corpses. I don't like the smell of blood either. I can't bring myself to do it. I think it's amazing that you don't flinch at this stuff, Ori.”

That little bit of flattery felt pretty good. Heh heh. Well, maybe I am amazing.

Even in these hard times, with death far closer than it had been in the Reiwa era[^1], plenty of people were probably still squeamish about cutting up and processing corpses.

Personally, I think the Blue Witch's readiness to take a human life without hesitation when necessary is more impressive, though.

As I'd requested, the Blue Witch had brought only tough monsters and slime-type monsters. Together, we unloaded them from the dump truck into the yard.

The Blue Witch nudged a squishy, translucent slime carcass that looked like a jellyfish off the truck with her toe. Then, grimacing, she wiped the sticky residue from her shoe onto the tarp.

“Can something like this really be used for a wand? Its Gremlin is small, and it doesn't look usable for handle material either.”

“It can, it can. Slime-type monsters are soft when they're alive, but I hear they get hard once they die and their magic power drains out.”

“Is that so?”

“Ah, you didn't know?”

I'd assumed she knew, but to my surprise, the Blue Witch shook her head. I'd have figured a witch who killed monsters nonstop would know a lot about this stuff.

“No. Until now, when I killed a monster, I only took out the Gremlin, then burned or buried the rest.”

“Ah.”

Of course. Until now, the only monster materials Tokyo had used were Gremlins.

“Well, I only know secondhand too. But apparently <ruby>slime<rt>soft mud</rt></ruby> gets hard like glass when all its magic power drains out, so I thought it could be used as a void filler for wand cores.”

“I see...?”

The Blue Witch gave a vague nod that said she didn't really get it.

This isn't someone else's problem. This could lead to an update patch for Cyanos too.

A multilayer design weakened magic-wand cores like Cyanos's. Building one meant carving a sphere inside another, which left a gap between the two and made the core fragile.

For now, I was using acrylic resin to fill that gap and reinforce it, but the stuff was a problem.

Acrylic resin lasted about 5–7 years. On top of that, production had stopped completely when civilization collapsed, and what little stock remained was already nearing the end of its lifespan. The acrylic resin in Cyanos would start deteriorating in another 2–3 years, cracking, yellowing, losing its transparency, and crumbling away.

This is a seriously big problem.

I could ignore it if practicality were all that mattered. But it would wreck a magic wand's appearance.

Unacceptable. Seriously.

Just imagining Cyanos-chan aging and deteriorating over time made me want to tear at my hair. I have to do something.

That was why I had my eye on slime material. Apparently, the Tohoku Hunting Association shaped it and used it in place of glass, and it showed no sign of deterioration even after three years.

I didn't know how long slime material would last, but unlike acrylic resin, which could never be manufactured again, it was something I could keep obtaining and replacing indefinitely. That was huge.

Slime material hardened once its magic power drained regardless of individual differences, so I had the Blue Witch pick the weakest carcass, the one with the least magic power and therefore the quickest to drain. I squeezed and filtered it to extract viscous, nearly transparent mucus.

After that, all I had to do was borrow Cyanos for a moment, scrape out the acrylic-resin filler with a hooked tool, carefully pour in the slime solution without letting bubbles get into it, and seal it up.

I quickly finished the whole job, gave Cyanos back, and asked what she thought.

“How is it? Did the weight change? If there's anything you want adjusted, tell me. Even if it's something tiny.”

“Mm... No, it has barely changed. Nice work, as usual.”

The Blue Witch lightly shook Cyanos and checked how it felt. She looked satisfied.

Excellent. Then the upgrade from Cyanos Ver. 2.2 to Cyanos Ver. 2.3 is complete without a hitch.

Little updates like this might be a pain for the user, but as the maker, I wanted to stay on top of them. Maintenance is important.

“I sealed it, so it shouldn't leak. But don't swing it around hard for about a week, until the solution has properly hardened.”

“Got it.”

The Blue Witch nodded obediently. Usually, the Blue Witch tended to nag me about being tactless, socially awkward, lacking a sense of danger, and whatever else. But when it came to magic wands, she trusted me and was easy to deal with.

That made me a little happy. When you make things, people sometimes use them for unintended purposes... Man, it's great to have a customer who uses her magic wand as intended, follows the precautions, and doesn't turn it into a brace, club, or shield.

Once a product leaves my hands, its owner can be as rough with it as they like. But it still feels good when they take care of it.

I'm glad my friend is the kind of person who takes care of her wand. I hear the Setagaya Witch is forever leaving hers lying around, snapping it, and otherwise breaking it. I don't really want to make wands for someone like that.

“From now on, I'll use slime solution for multilayer wand-core filler, so I'd appreciate it if you'd bring me some regularly. What you brought this time will be enough for a while, though.”

“All right. I'll bring you slime. You also said to bring tough monsters. What are those for?”

“I'm planning to use them for tools.”

“Tools.”

The Blue Witch glanced at the stone-like and metal-like monster carcasses in the dump truck and thoughtfully rested a hand on her chin.

“I often hear about turning monster materials into weapons and armor. But tools...”

“When I'm doing fine work on Gremlins or magic stones, I use finely sharpened hook needles and stuff. But they aren't hard or strong enough, so man, does it take forever. You know how monsters are ridiculously tough, right? Even hairs as thin as thread can be as hard as steel. I bet the work would go a lot faster if I could make precision tools out of durable monster materials infused with magic power.”

“I see. If you say so, Ori, then I guess so.”

“I call them magic tools!”

“You just wanted to say that.”

She sounded exasperated, but she was smiling. Come on, starting with the name matters too. For all your complaints, I know you like the stylish name “Dragon-Furnace-Carved Seven-Layer Blue Wand Cyanos” yourself.

I could finally work with monster materials I'd never been able to use before. From here on, they were sure to solve all kinds of niggling little problems.

I'm the guy who repurposes every technology for magic-wand making. I'll keep magic wands evolving forever.
