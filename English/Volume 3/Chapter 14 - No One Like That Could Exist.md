The last red glow had vanished behind the mountains, and stars were beginning to twinkle in the night sky. <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> finished the final tabletop exercise for the next day's Daidarabocchi hunt and left the temporary command post.

People bustled in and out of the tents beside the watchtower at the summit of Azuma-Kofuji.

Some drove tent pegs back in after the wind had nearly pulled them loose. Others stirred a huge pot of miso soup in the cooking area, checked items off a list as they pointed to a pile of supplies, or raised a marker flag over the medical tent. Everyone focused on their own work, and a healthy tension hung in the air. Reports came in smoothly from each watchtower. Nothing was wrong, and they were ready to respond at once if that changed.

They had planned carefully for this day. They had trained, gathered information, bought weapons, and held exercises.

As Sun Tzu said, “The victorious army wins first, then seeks battle.”

Put simply, it meant, “Prepare enough to win before you fight.”

Trying to find a path to victory after the fighting began was foolish. By that standard, the Tohoku Hunting Association was fully prepared.

The fog of war was frightening, and the monsters of the mountains were impossible to fathom. Even perfect preparations couldn't guarantee victory. Still, they had done everything they could. Now all they could do was await fate.

<ruby>Itazu<rt>Great Bear</rt></ruby> had left the command post a little before sunset. He was apparently somewhere out of sight, performing an old hunters' rite to pray for a successful hunt.

<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> didn't believe in traditional rites like that, but he understood the urge to drag even fate onto one's side. Especially now that magic had been proven real, he couldn't entirely dismiss occult ideas like luck, good fortune, and momentum.

He couldn't entirely dismiss them, but he still didn't believe in them. <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> wasn't going to pray to a god for a successful hunt. He planned to return to the tent he shared with <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby> and go to bed early.

Enough sleep mattered more than unreliable luck. It was the safe, practical way to get the best performance from his mind and body.

The Tohoku Hunting Association's five members were each preparing for tomorrow's decisive battle in their own way. <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> was about to enter his tent, wondering whether to have some ginger tea before bed, when he found a man waiting beside the entrance.

“<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>-san, got a moment? I finished a new weapon.”

The huge man wore work clothes and a headband, and his voice boomed with enthusiasm. This was Sanukino Banzo, the technician and craftsman who was the pride of the Tohoku Hunting Association.

Soon after the Gremlin Disaster, Sanukino had developed self-blood tanning for monster materials. His tremendous talent and passion had kept him at the forefront of the monster-material magic-item industry ever since. He was unquestionably the genius supporting the Tohoku Hunting Association from behind the scenes.

“Sorry, Sanukino, but I was planning to turn in early tonight.”

“No, no, won't take but a minute. Be over in no time. One look and you'll all toss those magic wands aside and want one of these.”

<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> tried to brush him off politely, but the overeager Sanukino wouldn't listen. He opened the pack basket beside him and began pulling out his new weapon.

<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> sighed deeply and scratched his head.

Sanukino had once been much quieter. He had kept to the background, never asserted himself, and worked away in silence.

The mushroom pandemic had changed Sanukino. He had lost his wife to it and become a single father, and he still nursed a personal grudge against Tokyo for bringing mushroom disease to Tohoku.

Because of that, he had no faith at all in the custom weapon bought from Tokyo, the gun-wand Giant Slayer. He insisted they didn't need anything like it, vowed to make a stronger and more reliable weapon himself, and threw himself into proving it.

At first, <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> had sympathized with the big man who had lost his wife. But after hearing the same bitter complaints again and again, with Sanukino showing up at every opportunity, he was getting sick of it.

Tokyo really had brought the disease to Tohoku, and thousands had died.

But it had also brought fertility magic, which had saved tens of thousands from starving to death.

Tohoku had swallowed its resentment and decided to support Tokyo.

That matter was over.

It had to be treated as over.

No matter how much resentment people held inside, they had to join hands.

These were harsh times. They wouldn't survive otherwise.

“Sanukino. I told you the competition results, didn't I? We had no intention of favoring Tokyo or cozying up to them. We simply compared your weapon with 0933's and chose theirs because it performed better. That is all.”

“I know that much. You hunters don't lie. You're fair and square, always thinking of all of us. I trust you. More than anything, <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>-san, you found my boy when he was lost and brought him home. I'm truly grateful for that.”

Sanukino said this in a slightly softer tone. He folded his huge frame into a deep bow.

“Then—”

“The ones I don't trust are Tokyo folk. They taught us fertility magic, saying they wanted to help us, then sent disease along with it, didn't they? They've done it before! Even if it's a custom weapon made by Tokyo's best craftsman, even if it looks good at a glance, who knows what kind of trap's hidden in it!”

Sanukino waved his fists, his face red as he argued hard enough to spray spit. He had a point, and <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> growled low in his throat.

He was clearly looking for faults because of a personal grudge. This was plain nitpicking.

If he started digging into Tokyo's faults, Tohoku's own shameful past would come under scrutiny too. They would have to answer for ruthlessly reducing the number of mouths to feed by throwing many innocent, defenseless people before monster hordes. Tohoku hadn't protected its people as well as Tokyo had.

Sanukino had a point in not trusting Tokyo.

But one valid point didn't outweigh simple reason.

They needed Tokyo's help to take down Daidarabocchi. If anger and resentment could bring Daidarabocchi down, <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> would of course use them too.

“I trust your weapons, Sanukino. More than Tokyo's wands. But the gap in maximum firepower is simply too large.”

<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> tried to carefully turn down one of Tohoku's leading figures without slighting him, but Sanukino pulled a weapon like a cross between a gun and a cannon from his pack basket, shoved it at him, and launched into a rapid-fire explanation.

“This here's a new hand cannon. It's an amazing piece—hits almost twice as hard as the old model! Up to now, we've used black powder because it was easy to make, but this one uses higher-output dragon marrow fluid as liquid propellant. It's so reactive I sealed it in a hardened-slime ampoule. The hammer breaks the ampoule's neck, mixing the chemicals so they explode all at once and fire the projectile. So the blast pressure won't split the barrel, I carved it from the outer surface of that monster that looked like living metal, the one <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby>-san hunted the other day...”

<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> tapped Sanukino on the shoulder mid-explanation and shook his head.

Even that brief outline told him Sanukino had achieved a huge increase in performance in only one month. He really was a first-rate craftsman.

But it was not enough.

“No. It would need at least ten times the power to come anywhere close to the firepower of 0933's magic wand.”

“Magic wands? Wands, wands, wands! What's so great about wands!?”

The rejection only got Sanukino more worked up.

People working in the other tents turned to see what was happening, but <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> waved them back to work.

“The twenty-first century was the age of firearms, wasn't it? Remember those barrages from the Self-Defense Forces' machine guns? They mowed down monster hordes in no time, and nothing could beat 'em as long as the ammunition held out. That's right. Guns are the strongest. That's already settled! Your magic's something else too, <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>-san, but if we can win by pumping 'em full of lead, all the better. Am I wrong? What's the point of waving around magic wands, flashy new toys like that? You mean to trust your lives to a new weapon with only four years of history, when we don't even know what flaws it has? Guns have hundreds of years behind 'em. That's a whole different level of reliability!”

“Bullets don't work on some monsters. Only magic can kill them. Guns are powerful, but magic is our main weapon now. That is the age we live in. Please try to understand.”

“No. I ain't accepting that. Tokyo's weapons can't be trusted!”

Sanukino stubbornly refused to budge, and <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> lost all interest in arguing further.

The Daidarabocchi operation had already been planned down to the smallest detail around the gun-wand Giant Slayers made by Tokyo's Wand Maker 0933. Changing weapons the day before the operation would only cause confusion. No matter how much better the replacements were, going into battle with weapons they had barely trained with could easily end in failure.

Besides, <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> was trying to go to bed. He couldn't listen to Sanukino air his grievances forever.

Fed up, <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> shoved the hand cannon back at Sanukino and fetched his customized gun-wand Giant Slayer from the tent.

Sanukino's personal grudge had clouded his thinking and made him stubborn, but he was a craftsman at heart.

One master craftsman recognized another: the wand had impressed even <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>, an outsider to the trade, and won over the straitlaced <ruby>Itazu<rt>Great Bear</rt></ruby> at a glance. Once Sanukino saw it, touched it, and felt its quality for himself, he was sure to accept it.

The Tohoku Hunting Association dealt harshly with anyone who damaged the whole group's interests for personal reasons.

If he still couldn't accept it after seeing the undeniable genuine article for himself, then that was all he amounted to.

“Sanukino, this is 0933's magic wand: gun-wand Giant Slayer No. 4. Hold it. Can you still say your weapon is better after seeing this?”

“Oh, here it comes. Calls itself a god-slaying weapon, but it's a break-action rifle? Hah! Amateur thinking. This thing's just a cheap trick. Stuff like this always has weak joints—”

Sanukino took the gun-wand Giant Slayer without hiding his mockery. The instant he gripped it in both hands and tried to flex it with all his strength, his face went blank.

“—It's strong. Ridiculously strong. This is... Did they use monster bone as structural material? I can see signs of self-blood tanning. W-well now? Fine, I'll grant the joint strength. Whoever made this doesn't seem foolish enough to neglect the basics. But what about the barrel?”

Sanukino had been shaken, but he quickly recovered.

He took a small hammer from the tool belt at his waist and tapped the barrel lightly, listening to the sound. He tried several different spots, and his eyes widened with every tap.

“Huh...? Wh-what the hell is this!? That's some astonishing heat treatment. How did they temper this? Toughness... hardness... Hmm, what kind of furnace could forge steel this beautiful...? More than that, the barrel's polished absurdly smooth. It's so smooth it circles back around to creepy. Not a dent or even a trace of warping. Absolutely zero. Is this really something a person did...?”

None of the huge man's earlier prejudice remained in his eyes; a craftsman's focus had taken its place. He frowned at the wand, determined to miss nothing, and ran his fingers over the entire gun-wand as he inspected every inch, then sighed in admiration.

“The balance is perfect too. W-well. Fine, fine. When it comes to metalwork, whoever made this is a master craftsman. I'll admit it. Talent like this is wasted on a Wand Maker. They could've become a god among gunsmiths. Why'd they ever think to stick a needless wand function on a gun? Ah, ah, are they showing off by polishing a magic stone this round... Huh? ...Huh?”

Sanukino finished inspecting the gun portion and turned to the wand portion. His admiration gave way to fear, and his voice trembled.

“Wh-wh-wh-why's this a three-layer structure!? M-monster!! Laser processing—no, they can't use electricity. By hand? They did this by hand? That can't be... No, any way I look at it, this is handmade. Huhhhh??? A-a monster! There's a monster in Tokyo calling itself a Wand Maker!”

Sanukino worked himself up so much that his legs gave out and his whole body began to shake. <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> laughed.

Sanukino was one of the finest craftsmen of his time after all, and a craftsman's eye couldn't lie. Apparently, 0933's wand was more than enough to blow away his prejudice against Tokyo.

“What're you laughing at!? This is serious. That Tokyo Wand Maker ain't human! Not even a mage could do something like this. They're a god or Buddha, or else some kind of yokai!”

Muttering, “Scary, scary,” Sanukino returned the gun-wand to <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> as though handling a sacred treasure too dazzling to touch.

“Convinced now?” <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> asked the big man, whose attitude had completely changed.

“...Hate to admit it, but it looks like I was the one who couldn't see straight. You'd have to be a fool not to trust the work of a craftsman who can make a masterpiece like this. Though they don't seem human, mind you. Tokyo can't be trusted, but I've got to trust Wand Maker 0933 completely. A craftsman who makes a masterpiece like this would never swindle the person using it. It ain't possible even if heaven and earth turn upside down,” Sanukino declared firmly.

Then his shoulders slumped, and he slowly put the hand cannon back into his pack basket. All the eager energy he'd had before seeing the gun-wand was gone.

<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> was glad Tohoku's great craftsman had come to his senses. But he'd swung so far the other way that he seemed to have lost all his energy too. Or maybe what he'd lost was confidence.

Tokyo was strong.

Stronger than Tohoku.

Tokyo had ten times Tohoku's population, so simple arithmetic said it had ten times as many exceptional people too. It was no surprise Tokyo had produced a monster capable of crushing the pride of Sanukino, the master craftsman who had supported Tohoku for so long.

<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> could not defeat even the Dragon Witch, who ranked second to the Blue Witch, the strongest of the Tokyo Witches' Council. In almost every field, Tohoku lagged behind Tokyo.

But there was no reason to lose heart. Sanukino couldn't make weapons as powerful as 0933's, but 0933 didn't know how to work monster materials.

Sanukino's broad back had curled in on itself. <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> gently patted him on the shoulder.

“Don't let it get you down. The Tohoku Hunting Association has depended on you all this time, Sanukino. We always will.”

“I'm glad to hear it, but after seeing something like that... Maybe it's time I retired. In the end, I was just a frog in a well.[^1]”

“No. That is not true. You still have plenty left in you, Sanukino. We need you to keep at it. I'm not saying this to console you or out of sympathy. Tohoku needs you. Japan needs you.”

Sanukino had completely lost his confidence, and <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> tried to rally him with heartfelt words.

“The same goes for anything. Concentrating everything in one place makes us weak. Competition is what drives progress. What will we do if we leave everything to 0933 and something happens to them? Unlike 0933's methods, yours can be copied. It's a practical craft that can spread far and wide. In a sense, that's far more useful than a one-of-a-kind superweapon.”

Back before the Gremlin Disaster, far weaker guns had remained the main weapon on the battlefield even after nuclear bombs were invented.

0933's special magic wands certainly had the power to change the course of a battle.

But that didn't mean every other weapon would disappear from the world.

“You are Tohoku's pride, Sanukino. I will keep counting on you.”

Whether <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>'s words had reached him was unclear. After a short silence, however, Sanukino nodded, shouldered his pack basket, and left.

...

<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> watched him go, then scratched his head and entered the tent.

He had lectured Sanukino as if he had everything figured out, but he didn't know how much he could actually pull off himself.

Class A-1 monsters were powerful. A monster of the same class in Tokyo had supposedly killed the Bloodsucking Mage, the powerful Transcendent who had been the mainstay of the Tokyo Witches' Council.

If Daidarabocchi was about as strong, then they had to be prepared for the Association's first-ever fatality.

Even with 0933's wands, enough people, and complete preparations, victory was not certain.

If they lost to Daidarabocchi and the hunters were wiped out, arrangements had been made for Tokyo and Hokkaido to somehow take in Tohoku's people between them. If that happened, the less bad blood there was between Tohoku and Tokyo, the better.

Of course, they intended to win. But the hunters were not foolish enough to ignore what would happen if they lost.

Would they win, or lose?

The outcome of the Tohoku hunters' life-or-death battle would become clear tomorrow.
