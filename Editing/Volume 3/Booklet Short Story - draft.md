One afternoon, a few days after the Daidarabocchi hunt.

After being summoned by his superior, Murakumo Kariya arrived at a reception room in the Sendai Toshogu[^1] shrine office. Bears, griffons, and other hunting trophies were solemnly displayed around the room. <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> was the only one there. He motioned Murakumo to a sofa and gently urged him to sit.

“Thanks for coming. Sit down. Make yourself comfortable.”

“Yes...”

Suppressing the twitch in his hand as it tried to raise its middle finger, Murakumo obediently sat on the sofa across from <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>.

Murakumo hated the good-looking bastard and his languid air. Whenever their eyes met, Murakumo wanted to rip out the flashy hair <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> wore long at the back. It was all because <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> had taken <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby> from him.

There was no way he could like the man who had taken the woman he liked.

He knew full well that all his irritation was nothing but a selfish grudge. At the root of his dark feelings were the envy and jealousy of a pathetic loser at romance. His pride would not let him do anything as lame as take a grudge over a woman out on <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>, and he was not stupid enough to snap at his superior over a private matter.

That did not mean he could simply tell himself, I only have myself to blame for losing <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby>, so I'll get along with <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>. Murakumo's feelings were complicated.

He had not wanted to see <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> because he knew it would only torment him, but today they had ended up face-to-face anyway.

“About your resignation letter.”

<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> got straight to the point, like a member of the Tohoku Hunting Association, which valued simplicity and fortitude.

“You listed your reason for leaving as ‘personal circumstances.’ But the Tohoku Hunting Association very much wants a fine wizard like you to keep working with us.”

“I'm grateful to hear that, but...”

“If the ‘personal circumstances’ behind your resignation are something we can resolve, we will address them. In short, I am asking you to stay. What do you say?”

“...”

It was painfully awkward, and Murakumo fell silent.

<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>'s eyes were dead serious.

They were the eyes of someone who absolutely thought there had to be some very serious reason.

The Tohoku Hunting Association was the governing body of Tohoku, centered in Sendai. Five Transcendents stood at its head, with various administrative bodies beneath them. Murakumo had worked for about four years as a Daidarabocchi watcher directly under the Transcendents. He was highly regarded as an excellent watcher (wizard), so capable that a two-person team with him could do the work of three.

Watching Daidarabocchi was dangerous work.

Long-term observation of a powerful monster that could even kill Transcendents wore people down. In fact, many had suffered nervous breakdowns and quit.

Even so, he had stayed at his post alone and kept calmly submitting detailed reports. He had also brushed off the assorted monsters that occasionally appeared near the watchtower. To an onlooker, he had to look like an extremely capable man: patient, calm, and skilled in combat.

If he had endured the Daidarabocchi observation assignment for four whole years, he should have been able to handle future work with much more room to spare. That was how it should have been.

But Murakumo had submitted his resignation letter.

<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> seemed to think someone as capable as Murakumo had to have a very serious reason for quitting.

But that was not it.

In truth, Murakumo was a mage. He was a Transcendent who perfectly passed himself off as an ordinary person through extremely precise control of his magic power.

Even the harsh Daidarabocchi observation assignment, which would drive an ordinary person mad with stress, was easy work with a Transcendent's abilities.

Murakumo thought <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>'s high opinion of him was off the mark. Seen as an ordinary person, he was certainly capable. But seen as a Transcendent, he was only a coward hiding his strength and sneaking around while doing an easy job.

There was no grand reason behind Murakumo's resignation.

He only wanted to quit because the woman he had a crush on had gotten together with his direct superior, and working in the same place had become painful.

In other words, this was where workplace romance had gotten him.

There was no way he could tell his direct superior—<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>—the real reason for resigning.

Maybe <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> had read something into Murakumo's long silence, because he spoke gently.

“We may have been relying too much on you. We gave you too much because you could handle it alone. I'm sorry. Now that Daidarabocchi has been defeated and we have some breathing room, we can bring in more people and spread out the burden—”

“No, that isn't it. Um... embarrassing as it is... it's about a woman.”

Murakumo cut him off with a vague but truthful answer. <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> seemed to understand.

“I see. You're at the age where you would be thinking about marriage. I'm sorry I did not think of that. If you're planning ahead to make things official, it would certainly be hard to continue dangerous work.”

That was what Murakumo had been counting on: surely <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> would not probe any further if he put it that way. More like, this is your fault, so don't you dare start probing. <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> seemed to understand.

<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> had clearly read too much into it and misunderstood, but if his romantic rival understood the real situation instead, Murakumo would die of rage. So this was fine.

Murakumo wasted no time going along with the misunderstanding and tried to wrap things up quickly.

“That's right. Of course, I intend to use my magic power to keep contributing to Tohoku, but I would like to avoid dangerous work.”

“I see. I understand the situation. Do you already have someone you're dating?”

“Huh? No, I don't.”

“Then leave it to me. I can introduce you to a woman who will understand the demands of your work. My wife—<ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby>—is that kind of person, so she can give you advice too.”

“...I appreciate the offer, but I'll have to decline.”

Murakumo politely declined while raging inside. How good would it feel to grab <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> by the collar and yell, Don't brag about stealing her away! I'll deck you!

He felt like he was going out of his mind. <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> had not the slightest idea that Murakumo was heartbroken over his wife. He only meant to persuade an excellent employee who had submitted a resignation letter to stay.

Murakumo knew <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> had no ill intent at all. He was not saying anything wrong. Murakumo was the strange one, getting irritated while feeling defeated and inferior all on his own.

He knew that. Even so, he could not bear it.

He had lost at love. He had lost as a man. Let him look cool. At least let him lose with dignity.

Ranting at <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> about his ugly jealousy would be too pathetic. He wished <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> would stop getting on his nerves before something as self-important as, I saved you from Daidarabocchi's clutches, you know, slipped out of his mouth.

He took a deep breath, gathered up his last scraps of composure, and put on a calm face.

He did his best not to look at <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>, who still seemed to be thinking of ways to keep him, and considered his options.

Objectively, taking <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> up on his offer was probably the right choice. No matter how attractive <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby> was, she was someone else's wife now. If her husband mistreated her, Murakumo would overcome every obstacle and beat <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> down, but she herself looked happy. Stealing her away was out of the question. He had no choice but to give up.

Clinging to <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby> now that she was married would do no one any good. A stalker pining after another man's wife was scum.

If his love had failed, he should swallow his tears, get over it, and look for new love.

That said, if he could brush off his heartbreak, move on, and have his superior introduce him to a good woman, he would not have submitted a resignation letter in the first place.

Hindsight or not, workplace romance was a terrible idea. Mired in gloom, Murakumo tried to bring the talk to a close.

“Anyway. I—”

But just as he began, the door to the reception room opened, and the cutest woman in the world poked her head in.

Murakumo's words stopped, and his heart nearly stopped too.

“Hey, <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>. About mailing the Daidarabocchi Gremlin—”

“—Oh, Murakumo-san.”

<ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby> gave the frozen Murakumo a light bow.

“Were you in the middle of something? Sorry, I'll come back later.”

“No, not at all. It isn't anything important. Go ahead, go ahead.”

“Is that so...?”

He hoped his agitation had not shown on his face.

<ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby> stepped lightly into the room, spoke briefly to <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> about the Daidarabocchi Gremlin to be mailed to the Wand Maker in Tokyo, and left without lingering. On her way out, she gave Murakumo a polite, noncommittal smile clearly different in kind from the one she gave her husband.

Once he was alone with that bastard again, Murakumo spoke in total resignation.

“Um, continuing from before. Anyway, I understand what you're saying, <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>-san. I'll withdraw my resignation letter.”

“Oh! That would be a big help!”

Even though Murakumo had abruptly reversed himself, <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> did not question it and welcomed the decision.

Seeing <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby> one more time, hearing her voice, being in her presence—it drove the truth home.

If the one who fell in love lost, Murakumo had long since raised the white flag and surrendered unconditionally.

Even if it was only a customer-service smile that kept him at arm's length, she was still unbearably cute. He had fallen for her all over again.

There was no way he could forget her. <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby>'s heart belonged to <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>, and Murakumo's heart belonged to <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby>.

All he could do was keep his feelings hidden and carry on this one-sided love for the rest of his life.

His desire to see <ruby>Iwatsura<rt>Rabbit</rt></ruby>'s face, if only once in a while, had become far stronger than his desire to avoid <ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>'s.

“My conditions for staying are that you do not give me too many dangerous assignments. And though I brought the subject up myself, that you stay out of my love life.”

“Of course. I'm sorry for saying something presumptuous. I will not pry into your private life.”

<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby> held out his hand, and the two shook hands with smiles.

Murakumo shook his hand firmly, smiling with his face and crying in his heart.

All the while, he thought, Ah, I liked her first, though.
