The day after next.

Hiyori came over to visit with the stoat on her shoulder. When I brought the fire-salamander decoration into the living room, she let out the exasperated groan I expected.

“So this is what you made. Wait, does that thing move?”

“Nope. It’s just a decoration.”

“...Ugh. Well, it’s yours, Ori. Do whatever you want with it.”

Unlike Hiyori, who was very clearly giving off a what-a-waste aura, the stoat jumped down from her shoulder, landed in front of the fire-salamander decoration, and was genuinely impressed, her eyes sparkling.

“A fire salamander! Nice, so nice! It’s super cute and wonderful!”

“Oh, Professor, you get what’s good about it? Actually, it has a hidden function. Watch this... Ta-da! Its tail can catch fire!”

“Whoa!? Th-That’s amazing! It really looks alive!”

Now that was a great reaction.

Gahaha, it took me back to those long-ago online-auction days. Life-size anime mascot dolls and figures used to sell for a lot. I even had a two-week wait for preorders once. It all felt so nostalgic.

“Is that for sale?”

“Nope. I’m displaying it in the workshop.”

“That’s better. If you put it on the market, it might spark a different kind of bidding war from your magic wands.”

Hiyori, voicing a concern fitting for our workshop’s negotiator, gently stroked the fire-salamander decoration with a fingertip.

I wondered about that. At this point, I thought only witches and mages had the money to spend on decorations like this.

The new currency would be issued in another month. Once the currency economy eventually stabilized, I wanted to put one of my works up for auction. I was interested in how much it would fetch.

The three of us spent a while chatting excitedly around the fire-salamander decoration, but the topic naturally turned to what to do with the remaining 90 mm Gremlin.

How should I use the stupidly huge Gremlin that had set a new record for maximum size?

“Well, I’m thinking a wand.”

When I said that, the two of them nodded.

Using it for a wand was a given. I was a Wand Maker by trade. I wasn’t going to use both of them for decorations, obviously.

“If you get large flakes out of it, it might be interesting to turn those into little animal decorations too.”

“You might have a point.”

I nodded at the professor’s suggestion. She seemed to really like the fire-salamander decoration. Any flakes I got would probably be too small to use as decoration material, but it was worth considering.

“As the point person for negotiations, I have to say there’s high demand for multilayer magic wands. At 90 mm, you could make five or six layers, right? ...No, a wand at that level might upset the balance of power. What would happen if it fell into the hands of a witch with bad intentions? Never mind.”

“Huh? I already made fifteen sealing rounds. Isn’t it a little late for that?”

“I’m still not convinced about those. Don’t go spreading around overtechnology weapons that might become more than I can protect you from.”

“Come on, you’ll protect me, right? It’ll be fine. The Blue Witch with Cyanos is the strongest in the world. I’m counting on you.”

“...”

Apparently happy to be relied on, Hiyori went quiet and radiated a soft, happy air.

“What about you, Professor? Any hot research right now, or wands you want? Is it the Nameless Epic stuff after all?”

“Hmm. There hasn’t been much progress on the Nameless Epic Hypothesis. We need many, many more incantation samples.”

The stoat twitched her whiskers, looking thoughtful.

Magic knowledge and techniques had arrived from Hokkaido and Tohoku, and magic research had advanced a lot compared with when Tokyo had been on its own. But it was still not enough to see the whole picture.

They had not been able to collect incantation samples from regions south of Tokyo. And if there were Transcendents overseas too, and all of them had learned their own magic, then the incantation samples Professor Ohinata currently knew of probably made up only a few percent of the whole at best.

“A recent research topic that might interest you, Ori-san, would be... wind-instrument ritual-magic implements, perhaps?”

“Huh? What’s going on at magic university? You do an endless amount of awesome-sounding research. Tell me more.”

When I pressed for details, the stoat professor carefully explained.

Wind-instrument ritual-magic implements were an attempt to use the principles of the Thirteen Ritual Implements I had made to recite unpronounceable sounds.

Unpronounceable sounds were sounds mixed into incantations that humans could not produce. According to the Yoshida Conjecture, there were twelve unpronounceable sounds in all, and the existence of nine had currently been confirmed.

Unpronounceable sounds were an extremely high barrier to humans imitating witches’ and mages’ magic. Attempts to get around that barrier had continued ever since Professor Ohinata’s father led magic-linguistics research.

Among those, research into producing unpronounceable sounds with musical instruments had a long history.

The idea was to use instruments to produce sounds humans could not make. String instruments and percussion instruments had been tried in the earliest research, but none of them had produced good results and the theory was not promising either. Now, only research into wind instruments that made sound when people blew into them continued.

Certain types of wind instrument could produce just one kind of unpronounceable sound that humans could not make.

Researchers with absolute pitch had found that among wind instruments, the harmonica could perfectly imitate one of the unpronounceable sounds made by witches and mages.

But until now, producing an unpronounceable sound with a harmonica had been useless. Even if it made the sound, magic power did not move, so magic did not activate.

Trying to recite the spell normally and put the harmonica to your mouth only for the unpronounceable sound did not work either.

Magic incantations required extremely precise pronunciation. If an unnatural pause came between the sounds of one continuous incantation, that alone made the incantation fail.

My Thirteen Ritual Implements had opened a way through that problem.

As one application of the Thirteen Ritual Implements, six people could recite the first half and the remaining six the second half, making it possible to complete a single spell.

Of course, if the handoff between the first and second halves was off by even a tiny bit, it failed, so it was an advanced technique that required extremely skilled coordination. But it solved half the harmonica problem.

Instead of making one person frantically switch between reciting aloud and bringing the harmonica to and from their mouth, they could run it as ritual magic. One person could recite the spell aloud, then hand it off only at the unpronounceable sound to the wind-instrument player, who was already ready to blow the harmonica. That would complete one smooth, uninterrupted incantation.

The Department of Gremlin Engineering and the Eyeball Witch had also found a way forward on the problem of magic power not moving even when the harmonica produced an unpronounceable sound.

When they made the harmonica’s mouthpiece out of Gremlin, the Eyeball Witch observed definite movement of magic power. It was not enough movement to become magic, but magic power had definitely moved.

And the amount and smoothness of the moving magic power rose as more of the harmonica’s parts—parts that actually affected the sound it produced—were replaced with Gremlin.

So, to sum it up.

Just like the Thirteen Ritual Implements with Moebius rings I had made before, if I made twin harmonicas entirely out of Gremlin and identical in shape, humanity could overcome one unpronounceable sound. With several people, anyway.

After listening to all that and almost tying my brain in knots a few times, I checked just in case.

“So basically, I just have to make several exactly identical harmonicas out of this 90 mm Gremlin, right? Then humanity will be able to recite some advanced magic it couldn’t recite until now?”

“That is correct. I realize this may sound odd after all that, but Ao-san’s multilayer processing plan is hard to pass up too. A multilayer magic wand that one person can use would definitely be easier to handle and immediately useful than wind-instrument ritual-magic implements, which require several people, practiced coordination, and vocal training. With a five- or six-layer multilayer magic wand, humans might even be able to stand against Class A monsters.”

“But?”

“Yes?”

“Tell me honestly. Forget whether it’s useful. Which do you want to see, Professor: a multilayer wand that’s just a rehash of existing technology, or wind-instrument ritual-magic implements that break new ground? I can’t make both.”

The stoat pressed her lips together and made a troubled face at my question, then gave in and confessed.

“I want to see wind-instrument ritual-magic implements... Ugh, the magic linguist’s blood in me wants to see the potential of magic linguistics.”

“All right. Let’s make wind-instrument ritual-magic implements. Even if multilayer magic wands look better right now, this is an investment, an investment. Rather than keep doing the same thing for immediate profit, it’s more important to open up new possibilities, even if it means a small loss and some inefficiency.”

“That’s right, isn’t it? In the long run, it is, right?”

The professor said it as if she were telling herself.

No, I really thought so.

We had just recently been talking about the threat of a Class A monster called Daidarabocchi, so we were only leaning toward multilayer wands that might let humanity stand against Class A monsters.

Let’s keep making new wands, yeah?

The conclusion seemed to satisfy Hiyori too, since she opposed multilayer wands. I promptly got the research materials on wind-instrument ritual-magic implements and started making them the next day.

I had already carved things of exactly the same size and shape out of one Gremlin for the Thirteen Ritual Implements. The shape I was cutting had just changed from Moebius rings to harmonicas.

From the harmonica shape shown in the design materials, I identified the minimum structure needed to produce sound. I cut out six extremely thin, fragile harmonica prototypes and one slightly larger prototype for the focus wand. Seven in total. Unless the prototypes were thin and fragile, cutting out seven was impossible.

90 mm had seemed stupidly huge, but when I tried to cut several items out of the same lump, I found myself wishing it were even bigger. People are greedy.

Once I had cut out the thin, fragile harmonica prototypes, I fit them into outer shells made of metal and wood.

The Gremlin prototypes were the cores and handled the pronunciation function. The outer shells covered and protected them, giving them strength. Together, they made the Seven Wind-Instrument Ritual Implements, which looked like some stylish folk craft adorned with reddish-bronze gems.

I put my mouth to the mouthpiece, placed my fingers on it, and blew a soft note.

The psychedelic electronic sound that suddenly echoed through the workshop made me jump.

“What!? Wait, what? Why does it sound like that!?”

I couldn’t believe my ears. It was a nostalgic sound I hadn’t heard in years.

I blew the harmonica again. I hadn’t misheard it. It was definitely a mechanical sound.

A synthetic-sounding artificial noise, electronically programmed and once so common everywhere before the Gremlin Disaster, which should have been impossible to hear without electricity, was coming out of the harmonica!

“...No. But, I see. Could that happen?”

It was shocking. But when I thought about it, it kind of made sense.

If you made an instrument out of Gremlin that grew by sucking up electricity, its sound would become electrical. Maybe. They probably were not completely unrelated.

Research had been going on for a while into treating Gremlins that grew on electricity as magical storage batteries and extracting electricity from them. Burning them with fire magic made them scatter electricity-like purple lightning and disappear, so there was no question they had an electrical side.

Interesting. Sure enough, humans could not make a sound like this with their mouths. Ordinary instruments could not make it either. Now that every electronic instrument had fallen silent, only Gremlin instruments could make this sound.

On the other hand, what the hell was going on with the throats of Transcendents who could make a sound like this with their own mouths? They seriously weren’t human.

It was funny that something made by magic produced electronic sounds. As I slowly played game music I knew, searching out the notes one at a time, the three fire salamanders heard the music, lined up, and poked their heads into the workshop.

“Mii?”

“Mi?”

“Mimimi...?”

The fire salamanders hid half their bodies behind the workshop entrance post, peered suspiciously around the room, and flicked out their tongues.

When I tried blowing the harmonica for them, all three of them stared hard at my face at once.

Their faces were funny, so I played a short beep-beep phrase for them. The fire salamanders swayed restlessly, then immediately got into the rhythm and started headbanging.

“Mimimii! Miimi! Miimi!”

“Miimi, miimi!”

“Mimmi, miimi!”

The three of them apparently really liked the sound of the <ruby>harmonicas<rt>wind-instrument ritual-magic implements</rt></ruby>—the Seven Wind-Instrument Ritual Implements. I was only playing the same phrase over and over, but they got super hyped. I was having fun too, and blew the harmonica louder and louder.

Come to think of it, this might have been the first time the fire salamanders had heard music. Life without TVs, computers, or smartphones was far removed from music.

But the fun little concert was forcibly ended when the audience went wild.

“Miimi, miimi, mimimimi!!”

Excited Tsubaki stomped its little feet, shook its head wildly, and breathed fire.

Sekitan and Mokutan got carried along and breathed fire too, setting the workshop floor and the legs of the table on fire in an instant.

“Aah!? Wait! Stop! Stop, stop! Fire extinguisher!”

The fire salamanders, excitedly spraying fire everywhere, were startled by my scream and shout and froze. The extinguisher’s white smoke left them completely white.

The three of them coughed, looked up at me reproachfully, and let out protesting cries.

“Mii...”

“S-Sorry. It was my fault. You were having fun.”

“Mi!”

When I bowed to apologize, Tsubaki hit my heel with its tail, Sekitan bowed its head too, and Mokutan began vigorously rubbing its body against my sock to wipe itself clean. Come on, I said I was sorry.

I had taught them not to breathe fire in the workshop.

But fire salamanders breathe fire when they get excited. I was the one at fault for forgetting that and working them up with music.

Once I realized that playing the wind-instrument ritual-magic implements got the fire salamanders too excited, I quickly packed up the completed implements in a box and sent them to the professor.

I left the performance tests and everything else to Tokyo Magic University.

I was worried that making ritual-magic implements in a shape other than a Moebius ring would make them self-destruct from abnormal vibration. But even if they broke, it would be fine if they could get valuable data.

Even just once would be enough. A single successful case of humanity activating magic that contained an unpronounceable sound would be a great step, paving the way for research that came after.

...But I had used an ultra-valuable Gremlin to make these implements. I still don’t want them to break after one use.

But against my wishes, the results of the Seven Wind-Instrument Ritual Implements’ function test at Tokyo Magic University were mediocre.

They had managed to activate magic containing one unpronounceable sound—the Night Witch’s magic—but the implements cracked after just one use. They could probably be used one or two more times.

On top of that, the Gremlins in the Seven Wind-Instrument Ritual Implements were not spherical, or even geometric, so their amplification ratio had dropped to 0.8x.

It was such a waste for an experimental magic tool made from a huge Gremlin from a precious Class A-1 monster to break after two or three tests. Getting data was better than nothing, but couldn’t it have lasted a little longer?

On the positive side, even accounting for the 20 percent drop in power, the fact that humanity had overcome one unpronounceable sound was a huge deal. It was a much better result than self-destructing after one shot from abnormal vibration.

They were now carefully analyzing the valuable data from the first experiment in hopes of further development and stabilization.

Going forward, they planned to recruit experts in spatial geometry and acoustic physics, then go back to the slow, steady work of mapping out the relationship between shape and effect in more detail.

It looked like advancing the research would take quite a while. Well, that’s how it is. With everything. I wanted the university professors and students to do their best.

And just send me the research results in writing. I’ll be waiting.
