## Exact English by region

### Header band (left-aligned)

`The Nameless Epic Hypothesis`

### Body — twelve paragraphs, in this order

1. Every magic incantation confirmed to date was acquired naturally by a Transcendent at the moment of mutation. What that fact means has been researched and debated at length within the Department of Magic Linguistics.

2. One long-standing answer is the Gremlin Language Hypothesis, which holds that the magic language used in incantations is the Gremlin language — that is, the language of the magic stones. On this account the stones that fell to Earth with the Shantak Meteor Shower are not merely magic-activation media but social inorganic life-forms possessing thought, language, and culture, and the magic language is the native tongue of the stones and of the Gremlins descended from them. The claim was a focus of argument for years.

3. Language is shaped by the constraints of the body. Plants cannot vocalize, but they communicate with nearby members of their own species by secreting chemicals instead. Acacias growing wild on the African savanna release a volatile compound when giraffes and other browsers strip their leaves. The compound disperses through the air and reaches every acacia in the vicinity as an alarm signal. Those that receive it load their leaves with toxins and take up a defensive posture against being eaten. A creature that cannot vocalize still possesses a language in whatever form suits it. Human sign language and braille are likewise languages that arose from the constraints of the body.

4. Turning back to what Gremlins actually are, it is unnatural for a mineral with no vocal organs to have built a language system that depends on vocalization. The magic language is hard to credit as the innate racial tongue of a creature like the Gremlin.

5. There is, on the other hand, a defense of the Gremlin-language theory that turns on the Gremlin's capacity for symbiosis. Gremlins absorb electricity and grow explosively, but they also live in symbiosis with animals. (Whether that relationship is mutualism, commensalism, or parasitism is a dispute best left to the Department of Monster Studies.) If the theory that Gremlins transform their symbiotic partners into monsters and confer magical ability on them is sound (Inuikita Saburo et al., *The Influence of Magic-Medium Minerals on Biological Mutation*, 2027), then the magic language may be regarded as a system the Gremlins developed by borrowing the vocal organs of the creatures they partner with.

6. Beyond the Gremlin Language Hypothesis, no shortage of other hypotheses attempt to account for the magic language. Among the most prominent: the Akashic Record Hypothesis, that it is a language spoken on Earth in the distant past; the Golden Record Hypothesis, that it is a language by which extraterrestrials announce their existence to civilizations in other star systems; the Prophetic Revelation Hypothesis, that it is a revelatory cipher handed down by some higher being; and the Collective Unconscious Language Hypothesis, that it is a lowest-common-denominator language distilled by the unconscious of all humanity from the tongues of every age and region.

7. The Nameless Epic Hypothesis was put forward by Associate Professor Nanase Nanami of the Department of Magic Linguistics. As matters stand, it enjoys the support of the field as the hypothesis that accounts for the magic language most rationally of the many available.

8. The Nameless Epic Hypothesis takes the position that every magic incantation is quoted from a single epic poem. That vast epic, whose title is unknown, is provisionally called the Nameless Epic.

9. If the hypothesis is true, then the epic seen whole could reveal the truth behind the Shantak Meteor Shower and the Gremlin Disaster that followed it.

10. Should the Shantak Meteor Shower come a second or a third time, there is no guarantee it would bring the same loss of electricity as the first. Next time it may be fire that is lost. It may be gravity. There is no telling what humanity would lose.

11. Deciphering the contents of the Nameless Epic is expected to yield critical information: whether a second and third occurrence are possible and how they might be prevented, and whether the first was an attack on Earth, an accident, or a natural phenomenon.

12. In the shorter term, even an unfamiliar incantation could be inferred and identified — the way one fills in a blank on a test — so long as the lines of the epic around it are known. Purely as a means of obtaining useful incantations, working out the Nameless Epic matters.
