## Notes and uncertainties

- **`継火の魔女` and `火継の魔女` are two different characters.** The glossary distinguishes them: `継火の魔女` is the `Flame Witch` (row 2, sealed on Apr 9) and `火継の魔女` is the `Flame Heir Witch`, her human successor (rows 3 and 4). The kanji differ only in order and are easy to conflate; both were checked against `glossary.md` lines 29 and 30 before rendering. `火継魔女杖` in row 4 is the Flame Heir Witch's wand and is rendered possessively rather than as a coined proper name, since the glossary has no standalone form for it.
- **Ruby flattened.** Filed prose sets `大狼` as `<ruby>Okyaku<rt>Great Wolf</rt></ruby>` and `巨神殺し` as `<ruby>Giant Slayer<rt>Giant-God Slayer</rt></ruby>`. Ruby cannot be typeset on this page, so both are flattened to their baseline readings, `Okyaku` and `Giant Slayer`, which are the glossary's baseline forms. No meaning is lost that the timeline's terse register carries; the full ruby remains in the chapter prose.
- **Date format.** The source sets dates as slash numerics (`4/5`, `11/23`) with the year on the first row of each year. Rendering these as bare numerics in English risks a day/month reversal for non-US readers, and the column at `x = 170–341` is too narrow for fully spelled-out months at the source's date size. Abbreviated months (`Apr 5`, `Nov 23`) are unambiguous, fit the measured column, and preserve the source's year-on-first-row structure. The showcase pages' fully spelled `Month D, YYYY` form is not used here because that form belongs to prose fact-rows, not to a dense chronology column.
- **Single-column structure retained.** The Volume 2 timeline page (`Editing/Volume 2/image-localization/s-p022.md`) was rebuilt as a two-column English layout, but only because its source was set vertically and read right-to-left, so it had to be restructured to be readable at all. This page's source is already horizontal, single-column, and top-to-bottom, so it is preserved as-is; restructuring it would be an unrequested redesign.
- `オコジョ―人間変身` uses a horizontal bar joining the two nouns; rendered with an en dash (`stoat–human transformation`), the English equivalent for a paired-noun relationship.
- `田植え` is literal rice planting, the agricultural work at Okutama, not a metaphor; rendered plainly. The fire salamanders are discovered the same day, and filed Chapter 7 prose confirms there are three of them from the outset, so the plural is used.
- No illegible or ambiguous glyphs on this page. Every Japanese string has one certain, source-supported English rendering.
