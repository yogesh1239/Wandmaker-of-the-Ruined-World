## Exact English by region

### Hand-lettered headline

`Touring Tokyo`

### Body entries, in order

**1 — `Flower Ward`** (teal)

> In Flower Ward, travelers may enter only the shops on the outer rim; getting into the inner shops means moving there for good. The cooking, lavish with the spices, sugar, and nuts the Flower Witch grows, is mouthwatering fare that gives up nothing to the old era. Every shop charges accordingly, though mages and researchers get a discount. The café Flower Garden sells syrups and spices to take away, a limited number per customer. That alone makes the trip worth it.

**2 — `Mermaid Ward`** (teal)

> Mermaid Ward is rich in seafood. Order the sashimi platter anywhere and you cannot go wrong. At Isonabe Honpo, if the catch is kind to you, you may get a pot of nine-headed octopus — a dish said to have rewritten what octopus cooking can be. Pure Seafood Mermaid occupies a ship run aground at the wharf, taken over just as it stands. It handles no monster-derived seafood whatsoever, serving the old Edomae flavors — horse mackerel, golden cuttlefish, striped beakfish — and now and then the Mermaid Witch looks in and performs a strange, lovely song.

**3 — `Bunkyo Ward`** (teal)

> If you come to Tokyo, visit Bunkyo Ward at least once. It is the Foresight Mage's home ground, and the town that grew up at the gates of Tokyo Magic University. Beyond the safety and the bustle, everything you lay eyes on there makes magic feel like a matter of dreams and hope rather than resentment and fear. The wands, amulets, monster encyclopedias, and spell dictionaries sold in the magic shops do just as well as practical goods or as souvenirs.

**4 — `Shinagawa Ward`** (teal)

> Shinagawa Ward, where metalworking thrives, is a town of fire and metal. Its spotless great bathhouse is busy at all hours. Metal arms and armor are sold here — swords, shields, chain mail. They fall a little short of monster-material gear, but given that they never degrade with age, they are more than good enough.

**5 — `Eyeball Ward`** (teal)

> Eyeball Ward is large, and it has the sights to match. The flea market in Yoyogi Park on weekends is full of finds that loosen the purse strings before you notice. The streets around Shibuya Station are a fashion district; there is no garment you cannot buy there. Most shops will take alterations, or back a garment by sewing monster leather into the lining. Several tower blocks have been opened as lodgings for visitors. If you do not mind a long climb, an enchanting night wrapped in fine furnishings and a spectacular view is yours.

**6 — `Tobacco Ward`** (teal)

> If it is grown-up entertainment you are after, Tobacco Ward is not to be missed. Tobacco, drink, gambling — whatever you crave is here. Wrestling by day and wrestling by night: enjoy either at your own risk. The night establishments often carry no sign at all, but free information stands are dotted throughout.

## Notes and uncertainties

- **`東京巡り` → `Touring Tokyo`.** `巡り` is the circuit-making sense — going around a set of places — so `Touring Tokyo` carries the meaning and alliterates, which suits a hand-lettered display headline. `Around Tokyo` is the nearest alternative. The phrase is not in `glossary.md`; flagged for addition.
- **Shop names.** `喫茶「フラワーガーデン」` is katakana English and returns to `Flower Garden`. `磯鍋本舗` is rendered as the transliteration `Isonabe Honpo` rather than translated, consistent with this page keeping Japanese place names (`Yoyogi`, `Shibuya`, `Edomae`) untranslated; `本舗` marks it as the original purveyor of the dish. `純海鮮マーメイド` is a mixed kanji/katakana name rendered `Pure Seafood Mermaid`, translating the kanji half as the source's own katakana half is already English. All three are flagged for addition to `glossary.md`.
- `九頭蛸` rendered `nine-headed octopus` — a monster species; the count is data and must not be altered.
- `アジ`, `甲イカ`, `石鯛` rendered `horse mackerel`, `golden cuttlefish`, `striped beakfish`. These are the standard English names for the Japanese food fishes, and the list is presented as ordinary (non-monster) seafood, which is the point of the sentence.
- `昼のプロレスも夜のプロレスも` is a wink: daytime professional wrestling, and `夜のプロレス` as a euphemism. `Wrestling by day and wrestling by night` preserves the double meaning at the same level of coyness as the source rather than making it explicit, which suits the locked Casual/Comedy register.
- `裏打ち` is the tailoring sense — backing a garment by sewing a second layer into the lining — rendered `back a garment by sewing monster leather into the lining`.
- The two entries above the headline continue a ward list begun on an earlier booklet page; this page is translated on its own terms and the entry order is unchanged.
- No illegible or ambiguous glyphs on this page. Every Japanese string has one certain, source-supported English rendering.
