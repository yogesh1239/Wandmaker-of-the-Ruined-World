### 13 — Gun-Wand Giant Slayer
```
The ones doing the actual fighting are the Tohoku people. But what those people carry are weapons the protagonist built. The protagonist is a genuine behind-the-scenes craftsman, so he does not go out into the open slugging matches. Don't go out there. Seriously. Stay somewhere safe. As the author I keep thinking a protagonist who never fights won't look good on the page — but the situations this protagonist would be fighting in are far too far gone, so he stays out of it and someone with more motivation and more ability to fight takes that part on.
```

### 14 — No One Like That Could Exist
```
Ori is not the only craftsman, and craftsmen do not exist only in Tokyo. There are people who occupy that same position in Tohoku too. Even to a first-rate craftsman, Ori is a glitch in the system. God, buddha or spirit? The more you learn about him, the less he seems like a person. But a person is what he is.
```

### 15 — Proof of a Hero
```
A story that was popular in the web version. There was nothing in particular to add to it, so it is here almost unchanged. A monster hunt by a hero history never recorded.
```

### 16 — The Seven Wind-Instrument Ritual Implements
```
In job terms, this is the craft that fills the "Bard" slot. Or rather, its forerunner. There is no reason a system of technique for working magic through instruments shouldn't exist. That said, whether the shoot of a technique grows into the tree you were hoping for is something you cannot know until you have grown it. It may come up a scrubby little bush and stop there, or it may become a tree so great you have to look up at it. Sow plenty of seeds of every kind, and water them diligently. Give it time and raise it, and you will find out.
```

### 17 — Let's Measure Magic Power!
```
Well then — a god-tier item is born. Readers who know their public administration, their research or their history will, I think, grasp how enormous this is from the single line "we have weights and measures." What would make a clear comparison? "Your phone now shows how much battery is left," maybe, or "your bankbook now prints the balance"? That it did not exist until now is the truly alarming part. Up to now the bankbook did not give you a figure — it said something more like "a lot" or "not much." That's alarming. It's genuinely impressive that they got this far.
```

### 18 — Side Story - Three Red Ones, One Blue One
```
The corner where we write the unusual character pairings the main story never covered! There is a distance between the Blue Witch and the fire salamanders. From the Blue Witch's side, they are the pets in the house of the man she has her eye on, and they are simply cute, so she would like to get along with them. But their first meeting being what it was, she still holds back a little. The fire salamanders, for their part, are jumpy about a strong, frightening creature striding around their territory — but Ori seems unbothered, and Ori protects them, so little by little they are getting used to her.
```

### Pass B — publication-English refinement

Reread as finished English copy.

- 「末期過ぎる」 given as "far too far gone" rather than a literal "too terminal"; the source is describing how dire the situation is, not a medical stage.
- 「モチベ」 is clipped slang for モチベーション; rendered plainly as "motivation" because English has no equivalent clipping that reads as casual rather than as a typo.
- Entry 14's closing pair 「知れば知るほど人とは思えない。でも人なんだよね。」 set as "The more you learn about him, the less he seems like a person. But a person is what he is." — the inversion carries the source's soft final concession.
- Entry 16's arboreal metaphor kept running through the whole entry (shoot → bush → tree → sow → water → raise) rather than mixed, since the source sustains one image.
- 「ヤバすぎる」/「ヤバいよ」 rendered "the truly alarming part" and "That's alarming" — the repetition is in the source and carries its escalation, so it was kept rather than varied.
- Entry 18's 「初対面が初対面だったから」 reads as a doubled noun in Japanese; given as "their first meeting being what it was", which is the natural English of that construction.
- Checked sentence openings across all six entries for repetition; entry 13 had three consecutive sentences opening with "The", and two were recast.
- Verified the exact-English section and every quoted string in the production prompt match verbatim.
