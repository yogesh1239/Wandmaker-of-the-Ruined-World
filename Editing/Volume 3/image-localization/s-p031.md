俗に言うスライム系の魔物は血液を持たない。
自己血鞣しのためには下処理が必要となる。
スライムの体は大きく分けて三層構造になっている。
つまり核となるグレムリン、グレムリンを包む特に弾力性の高いゴム質、ゴム質を包み体の 99％以上を占める粘体である。
下処理ではまず粘体を潰し目の細かい布などで裏ごしする。得られた粘液を手動遠心機などを用い（2000rpm 以上の回転数を確保できればよい）分離する。遠心分離を行うと上層成分と下層成分に分かれる。この上層成分を自己血鞣しの血の代用品として使用できる。

植物系あるいは植物的特徴を持つ魔物は種差が激しく、加工法も一様ではない。

樹齢、樹高、狩猟時期（季節）などによって様変わりするため、目下、情報収集と系統分類の最中にある。

## Exact English by region

### Header title
```
Tohoku Hunting Association Secrets — A Guide to Special-Material Processing
```

### Intro
```
Monster materials lose magic power as time passes, and deteriorate.
Self-blood tanning is a treatment that greatly slows that loss and holds materials at high quality for a long time.
This section covers the applied methods for those kinds of monster material that the basic practice of self-blood tanning cannot treat.
```

### L panel — header
```
For Slime Types
```

### L panel — body
```
The monsters commonly called slime types have no blood.
Self-blood tanning them therefore requires preparatory treatment.
A slime body is broadly a three-layer structure.
At the core is its Gremlin; around that a highly elastic rubbery layer; and around that the viscous body, over 99% of its bulk.
For preparation, crush the viscous body and strain it through fine cloth. Separate the resulting mucus in a hand-cranked centrifuge or similar (2000 rpm or above suffices). This divides it into an upper and a lower fraction; the upper fraction serves as the blood substitute for self-blood tanning.
The mucus can serve as a blood substitute without centrifuging, but the reduction in deterioration is then negligible.
Treating the rubbery layer with substitute blood yields an elastic material highly resistant to physical attack.
There is also a use that skips self-blood tanning deliberately. A slime body hardens once its magic power drains away completely, and can be used in place of glass.
```

### R panel — header
```
For Plant Types
```

### R panel — body
```
Plant types and monsters with plant-like traits vary enormously by species, and their processing methods vary with them.
To process an unknown plant-monster material, first try sap as the blood substitute. If that works, it is the simplest and the best method.
If it does not, the next step is the category-blend method.
In the category-blend method, the plant monster body is first dismantled into three parts.
The above-ground part: leaves, buds, stems, branches, trunk and so on.
The below-ground part: roots and anything similar buried in soil.
The reproductive part: flowers, fruit and anything else involved in reproduction.
Crush, grind and press the two parts you are not treating, and use the resulting blend as the blood substitute. To treat above-ground material, blend the below-ground and reproductive parts; to treat below-ground material, blend the above-ground and reproductive parts.
The right blend ratio and the dilution limit vary by species and, to a large degree, by individual.
They shift with age, height and hunting season, so information is still being gathered and a systematic classification is still under way.
```

## Editing and refinement record

**Pass A — source and continuity edit.** Checked line by line against the visible Japanese, the glossary and the filed Volume 3 prose.

- `自己血鞣し` set to the glossary form *self-blood tanning* at every occurrence.
- **`血液鞣し` in the right panel is a one-off variant of `自己血鞣し`.** It appears once, in the category-blend paragraph, on a page otherwise entirely about 自己血鞣し. ### Correction pass

One pass, two defects. The panels, frames, leading, body copy and folio were correct in the first render — the s-p030 render record was supplied to the renderer up front and none of that page's three frame defects recurred.

**Defect 1 — the header title was broken.** Measured against the source: glyph bottom edges scattered across a **17 px spread** where the source's sit within 2 px; ink height 20 px against the source's 26 px; and a light weight where the source is heavy bold. The result read as a ransom note. Root cause: the title was drawn glyph-by-glyph to apply letter-spacing, with each glyph positioned by its own bounding box rather than a shared baseline — so an x-height letter like `o` was drawn with its *top* aligned to `T`'s top and sank below the line. Fixed by computing one baseline and drawing every glyph with `anchor="ls"`, advancing x by the glyph advance plus the tracking value. Rather than merely matching the source's 26 px, the title was checked for headroom and set at **30 px** with 3 px letter-spacing, which ends at x 1232 and leaves the full accurate title intact — no shortening required.

Post-fix the title has five baseline outliers, all legitimate: three descenders (`g`, `p`, `g`, ink height 24, bottom row 86) and two dashes (the em dash at x 637–664 and the hyphen at x 939–945, each 3 px tall and correctly above the baseline).

**Defect 2 — the left chip's left edge sat 6 px too far right.** This was an error in this spec, not in the render. The chip bounds were originally measured with too tight a colour tolerance, giving x 175 where the true left edge is x 169. The renderer followed the spec, so above y 542 the source's own chip pixels survived out to x 169 while below it the redrawn chip began at x 175, leaving a visible step in what should be a single straight vertical edge. Corrected to x 169 (left chip) and x 799 (right chip).

**QA-method note.** A first continuity check reported 58 and 53 white pixels on the horizontal strokes. That was a measurement bug, not a defect: the check scanned the *outer* rectangle's x-range against the middle and inner rectangles, where white field is the correct value. Each rectangle must be scanned against its own bounds.
