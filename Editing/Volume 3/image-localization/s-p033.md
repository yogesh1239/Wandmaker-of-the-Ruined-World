### 08 — Secret Techniques of the Hokkaido Magic Beast Farm
```
This is the chapter where a new town and a new job class unlock. "The third village" and "Job: Monster Handler" go live. And a jewel set in the forehead, a jewel set in the back of the hand — isn't that sort of thing just wonderfully fantasy? What I want is for thick, gaudy fantasy to have some necessity and some reasoning behind why it turned out that way, you see…
```

### 09 — Together with the Fire Salamanders
```
You can't just up and tame some creature you know nothing about! That is what this chapter is about. I have never forgotten it. When the author was a child, I rescued a sparrow that was hurt and could not fly. More precisely, I convinced myself I had rescued it. The truth is I only wanted to keep a cute little creature close at hand. I was not thinking of the sparrow at all. I emptied a dresser drawer, lined it with tissue, and shut the sparrow inside it in place of a birdcage. So it could not escape. The next morning the sparrow had gone cold. That was how a child's idea of "rescue" ended.

Rescuing a creature you do not really understand is, in truth, exactly that. You kill it without meaning to.

But this book is an exciting fantasy brimming with dreams and hope, so no fire salamander is ever killed in the name of rescue here. If anything, it is the owner who gets hurt and run ragged. If you are going to keep a creature you know nothing about and live alongside it from now on, that much is only right.
```

### 10 — The Magic Beasts
```
Introducing three kinds of magic beast: plant, bird and mammal. Each of them is a splendid fellow, good for defence, for haulage and for gathering materials respectively.

Inventing imaginary creatures is fun. Breeding method, survival strategy, habitat, predator-prey relations, outward features — and then all or part of it turned to fantasy. It feels less like "inventing" and more like "estimating from the few clues available." It gets a worldbuilding nerd's blood going.
```

### 11 — The Nameless Epic Hypothesis
```
The name comes from Nameless Cults in the Cthulhu Mythos. It sounds cool and it looks cool on the page, so it wins. What troubles me slightly is that, since this is fiction, the hypotheses are usually right. In reality you form a hypothesis, discover where it is wrong, form yet another hypothesis, find that one is wrong too — and only on a mountain of dead hypotheses does a "theory that seems to be true" finally stand. But do that in fiction and the reader gets lost. If a reader takes in a piece of setting, thinks "oh, I see," accepts it, and is then told "no, that was wrong, it was a lie," over and over, they stop being able to believe anything. So the hypotheses that appear in this book are overwhelmingly of the correct sort. At most they contain the occasional misunderstanding.
```

### 12 — New Currency for a New Era
```
It's slow. Honestly. Issuing currency is slow. Couldn't something have been done about it sooner? For that matter, restarting compulsory education is glacial too. But when you're being hammered by every trouble under the sun, one absurd blow after another, and things are this deep in chaos, can anyone really get around to that sort of thing (the part not directly tied to survival)? Asked that, I think the answer is NO. Even with the delays in response, at a pace that doesn't turn fatal
```
