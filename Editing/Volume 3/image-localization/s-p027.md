## Exact English by region

### Header band (left-aligned)

`Tokyo Sightseeing Guide`

### Hand-lettered headline 1

`Where to Start in Tokyo?`

### Body paragraphs, in order

1. Since the new currency was issued, traffic between Hokkaido, Tohoku, and Tokyo has grown busier and busier. Beyond official personnel exchanges and technology sharing, plenty of people come to Tokyo for private business or to study. This section is for them: the ABCs of arriving in Tokyo.

2. **`Minato Ward`** (teal) — If you arrive by sea, you will disembark in Minato Ward. Start by sorting out your luggage at the shopping street right off the boat, the Minato Ward Central Wholesale Market. Gremlins, monster materials, foodstuffs — every bulky thing you are carrying can be turned into currency there, and you can buy a carry case as well. If converting all of it makes you uneasy, you would do well to hold back a few large Gremlins for barter.

3. Once your purse is in order and you are travelling light, take a room early. Unlike the old era there are no taxis and no trains, no bright neon signs to steer by, and hunting for lodging on foot is a considerable chore. Do keep in mind that you cannot book a hotel room over the internet or by telephone.

4. **`Mermaid Ward`**, **`Shinagawa Ward`** (teal) — Look for lodging next door to Minato Ward, in Mermaid Ward or Shinagawa Ward. Minato Ward has almost no inns, and what there is amounts to renting the end of a hastily thrown-up building. If you want seafood where you stay, go to Mermaid Ward; if you want to soak in hot water in a broad open bathhouse, go to Shinagawa Ward.

5. **`Kita Ward`** (teal) — If you arrive overland, Kita Ward is the gateway. Shops, lodging, and a police box — the guard corps station — are all gathered in front of the station, so it will meet your needs. The Northern Witch on patrol and the staff at the Kita Ward police box are obliging, but their main work is handling monsters, so if your trouble is with people, turn to an advice bureau rather than the police box.

6. **`Bunkyo Ward`** (teal) — Bunkyo Ward draws a wide variety of ingredients from every region, and it is lined with restaurants whose repertoires call the old era to mind. Famous names come thick and fast: the ramen shop Yonakiya, proud of its chicken-bone broth; the izakaya Yoidoraku, a delight for doburoku that never sells out; and Bistro Monster, for grilled meat and shabu-shabu. Brace yourself for the prices and the waiting times.

7. **`Eyeball Ward`** (teal) — Eyeball Ward keeps the strongest traces of the old era. The survivors of the cut-throat food districts of Shinjuku, Shibuya, and Ikebukuro may have changed their ingredients, but not their skill. Catching sight of the sign of a once-famous restaurant, or of a chain you grew up with, moves you all the more for it. The limited katsu curry at DODO Curry is by reservation only. Donbee, formed when beef-bowl chains merged, serves monster-meat rice bowls at reasonable prices.

8. **`Chofu Ward`** (teal) — Chofu Ward's only place to eat is the Chofu Public Canteen. Two set meals are served, priced in new yen at 50 yen and 100 yen, their contents changing daily, and on a lucky day dessert comes with it. Meals are bought by ticket, and minors can have a large portion free of charge. For those of you minding your wallet who still want to eat well.
