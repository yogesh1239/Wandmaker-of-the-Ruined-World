By early winter, when I'd started wishing for a hot-water bottle at bedtime, some good news reached me.

The Tohoku Hunting Association had used the wands I'd made... My wands! The wands I made! ...to beat Daidarabocchi to a pulp and win big.

Zero dead. Zero injured. A complete victory.

Even the Tohoku Hunting Association hadn't been able to take down Daidarabocchi, a Class A-1 monster that had blocked off the middle of Japan for ages.

But the moment they used my wands and sealing rounds, it was a perfect victory.

Well, you know? How do I put this? I guess this is what you call talent. My own genius scares me.

As promised, the Tohoku Hunting Association sent me the Gremlins harvested from Daidarabocchi via the Tokyo Witches' Council and Hiyori. I couldn't help doing a happy dance.

But the letter included with the huge Gremlins had two things in it I didn't understand.

A heartfelt thank-you from <ruby>Itazu<rt>Great Bear</rt></ruby>, the Tohoku Hunting Association's coordinator, written in an expert, old-fashioned brush hand. That was fine. It made me proud too.

The suggestion that I take on an apprentice because there was a talented craftsman in Sendai wasn't good at all, but I was going to ignore it, so that was fine too.

But,

“Does gun-wand Giant Slayer have something like an interception system built in that automatically activates in an emergency?”

I didn't really get that question.

What's that? Never heard of it. Scary...

According to the explanation in the letter, when Daidarabocchi tried one last act of desperation just before it died, its torso suddenly burst apart, and that finished it off.

The Tohoku Hunting Association thought the Tokyo Witches' Council had helped, but the Council denied it.

So they'd asked in case it was a hidden function the Wand Maker had built into the magic wands.

No, I don't know either. Nothing comes to mind.

I hadn't built any kind of emergency interception system into the wands, and in principle, it was impossible for some accidental interaction to cause that kind of phenomenon.

It was not a malfunction.

But the cause was a mystery. Creepy.

My wands had nothing to do with it, so maybe some wandering do-gooder witch like the Hell Witch happened to help them...? Even then, I had no idea why it would suddenly explode.

I didn't really get it, but it seemed my wands' performance alone hadn't completely beaten Daidarabocchi. It had been too soon to get cocky and think it was all thanks to my wands.

Still, it seemed gun-wand Giant Slayer's reputation had shot way up within the Tohoku Hunting Association, so I would humbly take it as meaning that I'd played a part in the major operation to hunt Daidarabocchi.

The other thing in the letter that I did not understand was a mysterious black Gremlin.

Daidarabocchi had used two kinds of magic: poison-gas magic and regeneration magic. But apparently, at the last moment, it had used a third kind: time-acceleration magic.

Three Gremlins were harvested from Daidarabocchi.

A reddish-bronze Gremlin 90 mm in diameter.

A reddish-bronze Gremlin 60 mm in diameter.

And finally, a black Gremlin 15 mm in diameter.

The 90 mm and 60 mm Gremlins probably corresponded to regeneration magic and poison-gas magic, respectively.

Daidarabocchi had used both kinds of magic often before. It made sense that huge Gremlins were used as the magic-activation media for powerful magic.

By that logic, the time-acceleration magic used only once corresponded to the smallest black Gremlin.

Only the black Gremlin was different.

The other two were reddish-bronze, apparently an intrinsic color derived from Daidarabocchi's blood components. Only the 15 mm Gremlin was black.

On top of that, the unnatural black Gremlin had crumbled to dust on its own before they sent it to me.

The letter said they wanted my opinion as a craftsman who processed Gremlins, but I had no clue.

All I could say was that something weird had happened.

I did think it was interesting.

But there was nothing to be done if the actual sample had turned to dust and disappeared.

For now, I figured they should dump that kind of thing on a magic university. Tokyo Magic University had lots of smart people, so they would all get together and unravel the mystery nicely. They might not solve it, or it might take them a while, though.

The letter's contents had made me tilt my head, but the main dish of the Tohoku Hunting Association's gift was not that scrap of paper. It was the wonderful crystals of magic.

I washed my hands thoroughly, put on my steel-sheep work gloves, said a prayer of thanks in front of the box, then opened it.

“Whoa, huge...!”

I knew they would be big, but I still let out a gasp.

Reading the catalog specs in a letter was nothing like seeing the real things, after all.

The reddish-bronze Gremlins had a rough but powerful gleam and measured an astounding 90 mm and 60 mm. The larger one beat the giant kaiju's Gremlin—previously the record holder at 80 mm—by a whole 10 mm. Now that's big.

The giant kaiju had two 80 mm ones, and Daidarabocchi had a 90 mm and a 60 mm one. Apparently, that was the sort of Gremlin size you got from outrageously powerful Class A-1 monsters.

Both reddish-bronze Gremlins were smooth and shaped like chicken eggs. Like most Gremlins harvested from monsters, they were close to spherical.

Ah, beautiful.

They couldn't beat the bewitching appeal of magic stones, but Gremlins weren't bad at all. You didn't get to see gems of this size and quality very often.

In another age, it would not have been strange for them to be used in the crown of a major power. Hell, I would not be surprised if some survivor community somewhere in the world was using Class A-1 monster Gremlins as a crown or royal scepter even now.

After admiring and enjoying them for a while, I immediately put the 60 mm one on the workbench.

If I could take photos, I would've captured this natural miracle before processing it, but I couldn't, so nothing I could do.

Actually, ever since I learned I would get Daidarabocchi's Gremlins, I had secretly decided what to use them for.

If I only got one Gremlin, I would use it for a wand.

But if I got two or more, I would use one for a wand and waste the rest as extravagantly as possible!

Right now, my workshop had my prized treasure Okutameteorite enshrined in it, a small furnace the fire salamanders came to play in, and a shelf lined with six little bottles full of magic stone powder from polishing. The map case holding the blueprints for every work I'd made so far was a low-key favorite too.

I liked that it already felt pretty much like a legendary Wand Maker's workshop, but there was still plenty of room to decorate it more.

This workshop still needs some nice decorations, you know? Like carved bears or decorative swords.

I wanted the workshop furnishings to be magic-related. A titanium-alloy knife mixed into a workshop full of magic items would feel kind of out of place.

So I would turn the 60 mm reddish-bronze Gremlin into a decoration.

It was first-class wand material, but using it extravagantly for a decoration would still make a one-of-a-kind luxury item.

A plain decoration with no effect at all, made from a Gremlin harvested from a Class A-1 monster! It was so extravagant I was getting excited.

I'd been given it, so it was mine, and I could use it however I wanted. But if I told Hiyori-mama, she'd probably get mad at me for wasting something this valuable, so I would keep it secret from her until the decoration was done.

I'd already had the decoration's design in mind for a while. A creature would be best.

I had considered making a miniature Daidarabocchi out of a Gremlin harvested from Daidarabocchi, but judging from the sketch of it the Tohoku Hunting Association sent me, its face was creepy, so I decided against it.

Why would I want to display an ugly monkey face?

If I had a green Gremlin, I would make the Flower Witch (she's scary, but she really is beautiful). If I had a blue Gremlin, Hiyori. If I had a white one, Professor Ohinata. I had decided, “If I get a Gremlin this color, I'll do this,” for all sorts of colors, but reddish bronze was in the red family.

Red meant fire salamander.

I eagerly picked up my magic tools and got started cutting the Gremlin.

The monster-material processing tools I had made from giant bat monster fangs were hard and strong and did not break even when I sharpened them as fine as spider silk. They were useful for fine processing.

I had to soak them in fresh blood regularly or they would go bad, but on the other hand, I was grateful that was all the maintenance they needed.

A genius craftsman like me did not need to draw lines on the Gremlin to mark out the shape. I could shave it away easily, like I was digging out the fire salamander buried inside the Gremlin.

“Mi? Mimimi!?”

Midway through, laid-back Sekitan came ambling into the workshop to hang out. When it saw the half-finished fire-salamander decoration, its mouth fell open and its little eyes went wide in surprise.

Wahaha. It looks like it, right? It looks like it, right?

“Mi? Mimi?”

Sekitan climbed up my pants and onto the workbench, letting out little cries as it poked the decoration with its nose and licked it with its tongue.

But it quickly seemed to notice that the decoration in front of it was not a companion or even a living thing. It blew out smoke with a hiss, jumped down to the floor, and slowly crawled into the ashes in the furnace. Cute.

Monsters use the Gremlins attached to their bodies to recognize their own, after all. No matter how similar it looks, the misunderstanding clears up right away.

Over half a day of careful work, I carved a detailed one-third-scale fire salamander from the 60 mm reddish-bronze Gremlin.

I hollowed out the tail and body, so I could pour alcohol inside, light its tail, and use it as a lamp. It could only hold a little alcohol, so the feature was just for fun, but little gimmicks like that were fun.

I didn't want the pets breaking the finished ultra-luxury fire-salamander decoration through mischief, so I went and showed it to Mokutan and Tsubaki too and taught them not to break it. Then I put it by my pillow and slept soundly.

Man, I did good work.
