What is a true best friend!?

If your best friend is in trouble, you go help them even if they tell you not to come!

I'd read that in all kinds of manga, and I agreed with it too.

This wasn't about whether going to help might be pointless, or what good I'd do by going to help. That wasn't the issue.

They're your best friend, so you go help them...!

I'm not like the trash who go on about friends and best friends, then make excuses not to help and pretend they don't know them when a friend is in trouble—all while waving around some fake idea of friendship.

I still didn't really understand the concept of a "normal friend."

I had my own definition of what a friend was.

To me, a friend was someone who went to help Hiyori after she told me to stay put in Okutama and headed for the battlefield.

Hiyori herself had said, "What you want someone to do and what's good for you are different." Even if Hiyori told me to do something, doing exactly that wouldn't necessarily be good for her.

Also, Bunkyo Ward sounded pretty bad. Which meant Professor Ohinata was in trouble too.

Blue Witch-sama was the strongest, so she'd definitely kill every last one of those rotten Arataki Group attackers. But she was kind of clumsy. I worried she'd use too much force and get Professor Ohinata caught up in it.

After praying to the Okutameteorite enshrined in my workshop that my wish would come true, I took my combat magic wand Hendensho, slung on my tool bag, hefted a bag of my secret stash of chemical fertilizer, and left home.

First up: recruitment and bribery on the mountain behind my house.

"Tsubaki, Sekitan, Mokutan. Gather round!"

"Mee."

"Mih."

"Mimimi."

Unlike the three who wagged their tails and meeped, Fuyo wasn't on board with us heading out. She wrapped roots around my legs to restrain me.

Yeah, that figures. You always want to keep me within reach of your roots.

"Fuyo. I have a bag of delicious chemical fertilizer here."

"!?"

Fuyo gave in to her appetite and closed her eyes. I set the chemical fertilizer down at her roots, completing the bribe, then left Okutama with the fire salamanders in tow.

If going to help ended up being a wasted trip, that was fine.

Even if I couldn't be any help at all, at worst I could run back home.

Even I understood that the super-genius Wand Maker risked getting captured by the enemy, despite Hiyori always saying I had no sense of danger. Though maybe I was only aware of it thanks to all those lectures.

Protect myself. Help my friend. Those were the two goals of this expedition.

Well, it'll work out somehow. Things worked out during the mushroom pandemic too.

During the pandemic, I'd moved overground while hiding my face, but Tokyo was currently under attack by enemy forces. I couldn't walk around showing myself aboveground.

So I went underground.

I would use Tokyo's subway network, hide from the enemy, and go to Bunkyo Ward.

Underground passages could only exist if they constantly kept the water out. In big coastal cities like Tokyo and New York especially, they would flood as soon as the drainage equipment stopped.

More than five years had passed since the Gremlin Disaster, and five winters had gone by with none of the electric drainage pumps working at all. The flooded underground passages had repeatedly frozen and thawed—expanded and contracted—badly damaging the concrete walls. They were collapsing far faster than aboveground.

Sneaking along the tracks, I entered the huge underground network also called the Tokyo Dungeon, holding Tsubaki in one hand and using her tail flame as a lantern. I admired the perfectly post-apocalyptic sight.

Lichen that almost looked like blackish stains crawled across walls where paint and tiles had peeled away in places. Near the entrance, fallen leaves that had blown in from aboveground had piled up in the corners, and thin, spindly weeds were growing there.

When I looked deeper in, the weeds disappeared and mushrooms appeared instead. There were bits of animal droppings too. Spiderwebs covered the ticket machine with its cracked screen, and there were traces of a pretty old campfire around the ticket gates.

The air was cooler than aboveground and smelled of animal droppings and dust. It was creepy, but had the exciting feel of another world. I kind of got how people who liked exploring ruins felt.

Checking the route maps covered in graffiti, I made my way through the underground passage.

There were many flooded sections along the way, but I cut through the fall-prevention fences with the fire salamanders' flames, welded them together into a simple raft, and crossed on it. The three leaned over the edge of the raft and stared down at the muddy water with great interest, looking as excited as could be. I grinned too.

Amazing. Five years after civilization collapsed, I'm finally having a real ruins adventure.

The subway was collapsing quickly, and in places with especially bad flooding, even pillars had crumbled. But if I watched the cracks in the walls, tapped the walls with the back of my hand to check how they felt, and listened carefully to the sound, I could sort of tell which places were really dangerous.

In another three or four years, it looked like Tokyo's underground network would become impassable. But for now, as long as I avoided the dangerous places, I could get through.

After going on and off rafts like that for about two stations, the tracks suddenly looked much tidier. There were obvious signs that someone had maintained them. There weren't any animal droppings, and the cracks in the walls had been repaired.

I wondered if someone lived there and braced myself. Then something appeared in the light of the fire salamanders' tails, and I understood.

"I see, a charcoal car!"

"Mimimi?"

"Ah, sorry, not Mokutan."

I apologized to Mokutan, who looked up at me thinking it had been called, then ran my hands all over the rugged charcoal car sitting on the tracks and examined it.

Come to think of it, I remembered hearing that charcoal cars ran on the Tokyo Metro Marunouchi Line. Since I was a shut-in in Okutama, I'd thought it had nothing to do with me and chucked that memory into oblivion. Turns out it did.

The charcoal car seemed to be for transport. Its connected freight car was loaded with piles of scrap metal and clothes. A paper reading, "SERVICE SUSPENDED UNTIL THE EMERGENCY DECLARATION IS LIFTED!" was stuck to the window in red letters.

"Hmm..."

I looked around. There was no sign of people. It seemed that after the emergency declaration, the station staff and everyone else had all evacuated long ago.

The charcoal car's door wasn't locked. When I got into the driver's seat and checked inside, there wasn't a key there either. There were only two levers, one red and one black, labeled "Speed Control" and "Brake." There was also a speedometer. Easy to understand.

It looked like even I could get it moving.

I skimmed the driving manual in the dashboard under the driver's seat, detached the freight car with my tools, lit the fuel, and got the charcoal car moving.

This was way faster than walking. Sorry for moving it without permission, but this was an emergency.

And we're off.

The charcoal car started moving slowly, then gradually accelerated, maybe thanks to leaving behind the heavy freight car, and carried us at an amazing speed.

"Mee!"

"Mih!"

"Mimimi!"

The three fire salamanders had crowded at the driver's window and pressed their faces to the glass. They were wildly excited by the speed of their first ride in a car. These guys probably thought this rescue operation was some kind of adventure trip they were taking with me. Cute.

The charcoal car got me there much faster. Once inside Bunkyo Ward, I stopped at Korakuen Station.

The station had been maintained enough to run the charcoal car, but there were lots of signs of leaks and puddles here and there. Maybe because of that, a thin mist hung in the air and made it hard to see.

I stopped the charcoal car, got off with the three, and crossed my arms to think about what to do next.

Once I stopped to think, I realized something obvious: I'd come here almost entirely on impulse.

I'd made it to Bunkyo Ward, but what could I actually do?

I didn't even know the situation.

I'd heard Bunkyo Ward was somehow the hottest danger zone in Tokyo right now, but I had no idea what was happening, where my friends were, what they were doing, or what kind of situation they were in.

Professor Ohinata might have already evacuated to some other ward, for all I knew.

First, gather information? Send the fire salamanders out to scout so I could get a handle on the situation... No, they couldn't talk, so even if they found something, they couldn't report back. Someone might spot them, mistake them for wild monsters, and attack them.

Then should I scout myself? No thanks.

Bunkyo Ward was crawling with enemies, and even before that, it was packed with people. Couldn't someone write the information down and send it to me on a paper airplane or something? No, that was impossible.

As I thought it over, I suddenly felt some kind of presence.

When I looked around, I could just make out Hiyori through the thin mist. She was waving at me.

"Oh, small world! Hey, Hiyori! It's me! Me! I was worried about Professor Ohinata, so I came because I thought maybe I could do something!"

I waved back and called out to her, but she didn't answer. Hiyori silently beckoned me over.

Yeah, well, even if this was underground where people were less likely to notice us, it was a battlefield under enemy attack. Yelling like that was stupid.

I went over to Hiyori so we could talk quietly.

But when I got closer, I understood.

That wasn't Hiyori.

It was a humanoid doll made of some unknown mineral-like material, nothing at all like Hiyori.

"Huh? ...Whoa!?"

The moment I stopped in confusion, something grabbed me and yanked me into the air.

My body spun sideways around and around through the air at incredible speed, and by the time I knew it, my whole body was wrapped around and around in white thread.

What, what, what was this!? I'd suddenly been wrapped up like bacon-wrapped asparagus.

When I squirmed around like a caterpillar, I saw the three fire salamanders hanging in the air, wrapped around and around just like me. Their jaws had been forced shut, and they couldn't breathe fire. The thread binding us seemed fireproof; even when their tail flames scorched it, it showed no sign of burning.

We all hung there together and flailed around.

As it slowly sank in that we'd been taken out in an instant, I broke out in a cold sweat.

Did I just walk right into an enemy trap?

Crap. I might've screwed up.

I traced the threads holding us up and looked at the ceiling above the subway platform.

And there was an absurdly huge spider stuck to the ceiling.

I-It was huge! Way too huge! It was as big as a car!?

Seven compound eyes reflecting the flickering light of the tail flames stared right at me, and its mouth clicked and snapped. The tips of its legs, thick with fine hairs, poked at me.

Spiders were carnivores. Monsters mutated from them were obviously carnivores too.

I was in huge trouble.

But.

So, where do you live? Hobbies? Are humans your main food?

I've always liked spiders. Because they're dexterous.

Spiders could spin those geometric radial webs from the moment they were born. Without anyone teaching them, they knew instinctively how to handle thread. They were craftsmen from birth!

Amazing, right? I seriously respect them. They're way too good at being alive.

To me, spiders and beavers, the masters of dam building, shared the top spot among animals I respected.

As I stared at those sparkling, jewel-like compound eyes and marveled, the giant spider tilted its head with an insect-like, jerky movement.

"Why aren't you scared...?"
