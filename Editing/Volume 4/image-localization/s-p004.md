## Template continuity

These profile pages repeat across volumes, so the furniture is taken verbatim from Volume 3's
equivalent rather than re-invented: `Age`, `Birthday`, `Height`, `Weight`, `Combat Sense`,
`Physical Ability`, `Magic Power`, `Intelligence`, and the legend `By witch/mage standards` with
`AA Maximum`, `A Exceptional`, `B Excellent`, `C Average`, `D Below Average`, `E Poor`, `F Minimum`.

A reader meeting seven of these pages in one booklet will notice furniture that shifts between
them; keeping it fixed is what makes the set read as one designed template.

## Terminology decisions

- **`おじさん` — `Uncle`.** Locked by `glossary.md:56` and capitalized, because Fuyo uses it as a
  name rather than a description. `Mister` was the tempting alternative and is wrong here: the
  glossary lists it among the forms to avoid.
- **`芙蓉` — hibiscus.** The flower she is named for. Its two florigraphy readings are given in the
  source in quotation marks and are kept as quoted phrases in English, because the sentence is
  explaining what the name means.
- **`拠点防衛戦` — holding a fixed position.** Literally a base-defence engagement; the point is
  that she is strong when defending somewhere, which is what the English says.
- **`物量の暴力` — brute volume.** Not "violence of quantity". The grade is awarded for
  overwhelming by sheer number, and English has an idiom for exactly that.
- **`地頭` — raw intelligence.** Native wit as against learned knowledge, which is the whole point
  of the sentence: the raw figure is top-grade, the assessed grade is not.
- **`生意気で火を吐く友達` — her cheeky, fire-breathing friend.** Left as the source has it, an
  unnamed friend described by two attributes.

## Layout decision — the body is broken into paragraphs

The source runs this as one solid 21-line block. That works in Japanese, which has no inter-word
space and reads as an even grey texture down the column. Set in English at the same measure it
becomes a justified wall — 200 words with no entry point, the one part of the page a reader is
least likely to finish.

So it is broken at the source's own topic shifts, which are already there in the Japanese and are
simply unmarked: who she is and what her name means; what she is like as a child; what her species
is and how Ori changed that; and how she actually spends her days, closing on the line that she is
a plant. Nothing is added, reordered or cut — only the four seams the text already has are made
visible.

Paragraph spacing must read as larger than line spacing, or the break does no work.

## Editing and refinement record

**Pass A — source and continuity.** Every string read at 2.5×–3× off the image, since these pages
have no text layer. All four vitals, all four grades and the `300` were read individually. Glossary
terms are locked: `Uncle` for `おじさん` (not `Mister`, which the glossary lists as a form to
avoid), `Magic Power` for `魔力`, `Flower Witch`, `Ori`, `Okutama`. Stat labels and the rating
legend are taken verbatim from Volume 3's page so the template holds across volumes. The bold run
in the quote falls on `ぜんぶす`, and the English bold is placed on the phrase that carries the
same beat.

**Pass B — publication English.** Read as a finished profile. `物量の暴力` was taken as a statement
about force of numbers rather than calqued. The quote is a small child's line and is kept plain and
cheerful, which is what makes it unsettling — the source gets its effect from the sweetness of the
delivery, not from menace in the wording. `些か異なる` became `somewhat different`, keeping the
source's understatement rather than escalating.

**Pass C — external review by Codex.** The raw Japanese for every region was sent alongside the
Pass B English, with the locked constraints attached, for an independent read. It returned roughly
twenty findings and a full corrected block. Taken:

- `芙蓉` is specifically the **cotton rose**, not an unspecified hibiscus.
- `しとやかな恋人` is `graceful lover`; `demure sweetheart` had altered it.
- `すって` is `suck up`, which `drink you all up` had softened.
- `拠点防衛戦` is `base-defense battles`; `a fight to hold a stronghold` was translationese.
- In `Eクラスのパワーの根や蔦`, E-class describes the **power**, not the roots and vines themselves.
- `責任を押しつける` is foisting **responsibility**, not blame.
- `利用し吸い尽くす` is two acts — exploit, then drain dry — which `using up` had collapsed into one.
- `一目置いている` does not reach `real respect`; it is de-emphasised to `holds in regard`.
- `慕っている` carries affection as well as admiration; `ウザい` is stronger than `tiresome`.
- `母の心配通り` is `just as her mother worries`.
- `人に似ていても` is a plain concessive, not a matter of degree; `However much she resembles` added one.
- `lock together at odd angles` and the word `garden` before `pests` were invented and are cut.
- The spaced hyphen in ¶2 is a true em dash.

**The merge-back detour, and why it was abandoned.** Renders E–G were driven by an attempt to
composite the model's type over the source's artwork. It does not work on this page and the reason
is worth recording, because the other six profile pages share the layout.

The artist knocks a white box out of the skirt behind every single Japanese line, so the skirt's
left contour is a staircase cut to the Japanese line rhythm. English sets at a different pitch, so
those knockouts land mid-line and cannot be kept. Repairing the contour locally — closing the gaps
from the surrounding pink — got most of the way but left a ragged fringe, and every variant traded
one artifact for another: a tonal seam where the model's skirt met the source's, a hybrid character
when the model repositioned her, white bites out of pale artwork read as paper.

The method was wrong, not the parameters. Redrawing a knockout to fit new type is an image-editing
job, and the image-edit model does it directly: render H simply gave the text column clear space
and let the skirt begin beside it, which is what an English edition would have done from the start.
**Localization goes through the image-edit model. Local compositing is not a fallback for it.**
