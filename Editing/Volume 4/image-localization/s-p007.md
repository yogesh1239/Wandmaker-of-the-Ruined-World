- **`浄園心春` is a new character name.** It appears in neither `glossary.md` nor
  `character-reference.md`: this page is the reveal that the Spider Witch was once a human woman
  with a name. Family name first per project convention, no macrons per the romanization rule.
- **`化け物` vs `怪物` vs `魔物`.** The source uses all three. `魔物` is the glossary term `monster`
  and is reserved for it; `化け物` is rendered `monster` too but inside the fixed phrase
  `man-eating monster`, and `怪物` — which is Koharu describing *herself* — is rendered `creature`,
  so the reader can hear that she is not using the official word for the things she fights.
- **`御簾越しに` → `through her bamboo screen`.** A `misu` is a hanging split-bamboo blind. The
  English names the object rather than romanizing it, because the point of the sentence is that the
  people of Nerima thank her without having to look at her.
- **`魔女の業` → `the karma of a witch`**, not `burden` or `sin`. `業` here is the accumulated
  consequence she carries, which is what `karma` means in English.

## Exact English by region

**Body — four paragraphs.** The source sets one unbroken block, but 34 unbroken lines of English
prose is a wall of type no English-language book would print. The break points are the source's own
topic shifts: who she was → what the mutation cost her → the Bloodsucking Mage's intervention →
where she stands now. No sentence is moved across a break.

¶1 `Her real name is Kiyozono Koharu. She was originally a quiet, inconspicuous woman who worked at
a secondhand bookshop in Jinbocho. Throughout her years as a student and then as a working adult,
she was always on the fringes of large groups, smiling quietly. Whenever the mood grew tense, or she
sensed a quarrel brewing or someone being shut out, she would discreetly work behind the scenes or
quietly say something to help, preserving the group's harmony without anyone ever realizing she was
the one holding it together. What Koharu loves is being included among people she is fond of, and
seeing everyone get along.`

¶2 `When Koharu mutated into a Transcendent and became the Spider Witch, she was deeply shocked by
everything about what she had become. People were afraid of the Spider Witch. Ordinary people could
not tell a former human—one whose appearance and physical nature had become exactly those of a
man-eating monster—apart from the fearsome monsters that ran rampant through the city streets and
destroyed the peace of the human world. Mounting fear whets the Spider Witch's appetite. Even when
she saved people from monsters they were terrified of her and fled, and it wounded her deeply.
Tormented by self-loathing, she agonized: an ugly creature like her ought to die, but dying was
frightening—so what on earth was she supposed to do?`

¶3 `It was around then that the Bloodsucking Mage came to see her. He saw at once that the Spider
Witch was a kind woman at heart, and he listened to her, stood by her with genuine concern,
encouraged her, and made arrangements to turn her situation around. Specifically, she was reassigned
to Nerima Ward. Rumors of a terrifying, sinister giant spider had already spread among the people of
Jinbocho, and once fear has taken hold it is no easy thing to dispel. On top of that, the Eyeball
Witch was already maintaining public order in Jinbocho, in Chiyoda Ward, and two witches in one area
was more force than it needed. Nerima Ward had no Transcendent of its own, so the Spider Witch was
welcomed without prejudice when she arrived as its new guardian. Thanks to the groundwork the
Bloodsucking Mage had laid in advance, the fact that she never appeared in public was accepted
without fuss—and more than that, people thanked her through her bamboo screen and sent her gifts to
show their gratitude. The Spider Witch cannot put into words how happy that made her.`

¶4 `The Witches' Council, which she joined at the Bloodsucking Mage's invitation, accepted her as
well. Every member had personally experienced a transformation of their own body, so they understood
what had happened to hers, and many of them were sympathetic. Their shared plight gave them a sense
of solidarity. Even now the Spider Witch dreams that everything would surely have worked out if only
that hateful incident had never happened—the decisive rift caused by the Iruma Mage's coup, which
set comrades killing and hating one another. She feels deeply indebted to the Bloodsucking Mage, who
took her by the hand and led her back among people. Her feelings for Ori, who is not frightened of
her in the slightest, are just as powerful, if not more so. Intelligent and modest, carrying the
karma of a witch, the Spider Witch goes on quietly doing her part to protect Tokyo.`

The heaviest review of any page in this run — roughly twenty flags across the four paragraphs, most
of them substantive. It endorsed three of my considered choices unprompted: the three-way
`魔物`/`化け物`/`怪物` split, `through her bamboo screen` for `御簾越しに` without romanizing

**Taken — meaning corrections:**

- **`大人しく目立たない` mis-cast.** `unremarkable` says she was dull; the Japanese says she did not
  draw attention. → `inconspicuous`.
- **`跳梁跋扈` — invented imagery.** I had written `swarm the streets`; nothing in the source is
  swarming. → `ran rampant through the city streets`.
- **`一度染みついた恐怖` mistranslated.** I read `一度` as an intensifier and produced `fear that has
  soaked in that deeply`. `一度` is `once` — fear that has *once* taken hold. → `once fear has taken
  hold it is no easy thing to dispel`.
- **`具体的には…配置転換が行われた` over-attributed.** The Japanese is passive; my `he had her
  reassigned` gives the Bloodsucking Mage administrative authority the source does not claim for
  him. → `she was reassigned`.
- **`治安維持` softened.** `was already kept safe by` loses the specific sense of maintaining public
  order. → `was already maintaining public order in`.
- **`我が身に起きた変異を体感していた` weakened.** `had felt a mutation in their own body` misses
  that each had *personally lived through* their own transformation — which is precisely why they
  understood hers.
- **`きっと全てが上手くいったのに…夢に見る` — wrong antecedent.** My `still dreams about it` most
  naturally reads as dreaming about the incident. She dreams of the outcome that never came.
  Rewritten so the dream attaches to `everything would surely have worked out`.
- **`禍々しい` dropped.** `hideous, monstrous` lost the ominous, ill-omened sense. →
  `terrifying, sinister`.
- **`お礼の品` flattened.** `sent her gifts` loses that they were gifts *of thanks*.
- **`変わり果てた自分の全てに大変なショックを受けた` abstracted.** My `the sheer completeness of the
  change devastated her` shifts the focus onto the degree of transformation; the source says she was
  shocked by everything about what she had become.

**Taken — English quality:**

`姿と体質` from the stiff `shape and constitution` to `appearance and physical nature`;
`掻き立てる` from the weak `stirs` to `whets`; `自己嫌悪に懊悩し` from the freer `Sick with
self-loathing` to `Tormented by`; `連帯感` from the translationese `There was a solidarity in it` to
`Their shared plight gave them a sense of solidarity`; `恩義` to `deeply indebted`; `大きな感情` from
the unidiomatic `holds a great feeling` to `feelings … just as powerful`; `聡明` restored from the
under-powered `clear-headed` to `Intelligent`; `寄り添い` from `stayed close to her`, which reads as
physical proximity, to `stood by her with genuine concern`; and the `学生時代から社会人時代まで`
span reworded, since `school years` is narrower than `学生時代`.

**Taken — caption:** `※身長比較` → `* Height comparison`, not my `* Size comparison`. `身長` names
height specifically, and the unusual drawing does not license broadening what the source states.

**Overridden:**

- **`さり気なく言葉をかけたり`.** The reviewer called `drop a casual word` unnatural and
  potentially dismissive. Half taken — the wording is now `quietly say something to help` — but I
  kept the sentence's own rhythm rather than its full rewrite.
- **`立役者`.** The reviewer's own rewrite reduced my `the one holding it together` to `she was
  responsible`, while its notes explicitly called my version a good rendering. Kept mine.
- **The final interior thought.** The reviewer preferred reporting it flatly for cleaner tense
  control. The source marks it with a quoting `と`, so it *is* quoted interior voice; kept as a
  direct thought after a colon, which is what the `と` is doing.
- **`並大抵の事ではない` tense.** The reviewer put it in the past. The source is present, as a
  general truth, exactly as `掻き立てる` is; both are kept in the present so the source's own tense
  shift survives.
- **`今日も`.** The reviewer flagged `today as ever` as an added continuity formula. `今日も` *is*
  that formula in Japanese; carried instead by `goes on quietly doing her part`.

## Notes and uncertainties

- `自分の手を引いてを人の輪の中に戻してくれた` contains a stray `を` in the source
  (`手を引いてを人の輪`). This is a typesetting error in the original booklet; the intended reading
  is `自分の手を引いて人の輪の中に戻してくれた`. Translated as intended, not as printed.
- `※身長比較` is literally "height comparison". Rendered `* Size comparison` because what the
  drawing compares is a human figure against a detached spider leg, and `height` reads oddly against
  a limb. Flagged rather than silently changed.
- Everything else on the page was legible at zoom and has one certain English rendering.
