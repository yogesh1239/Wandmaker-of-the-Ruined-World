| `鉄砲塚佐波理` | `Teppozuka Sahari` | **new** — family name first, no macrons |
| `ニート` | `NEET` | **new** |
| `オタク` / `非オタ` | `otaku` / `non-otaku` | **new** |
| `ホビーバトル` | `hobby battle` | **new** |
| `球体関節人形` | `ball-jointed doll` | **new** |

- **`ニート` stays `NEET`.** It is an English-derived acronym (Not in Employment, Education or
  Training) that Japanese borrowed and English readers of this genre already know. Translating it to
  `layabout` or `freeloader` would add a judgment the profile's affectionate tone does not carry.
- **`子供部屋おばさん`** is the female form of a specific Japanese internet coinage for an adult
  still living in their childhood bedroom. There is no English noun for it, so it is unpacked into
  a clause rather than given a false equivalent.
- **`自宅警備`** is a joke: 〈home security guard〉 is internet slang for a NEET who never leaves the
  house. Here she literally has golems standing guard, so the English keeps the literal picture and
  lets the joke sit where the Japanese leaves it.
- **`サボり` → `slacking off`**, and the bold falls on `slacking` to match the source's bold on
  `サボり`.
- **The furigana is dropped**, as on every profile page so far.

¶1 `Her real name is Teppozuka Sahari. A NEET to the core. She does not want to work. After
graduating from high school she neither went on to further study nor took a job, and stayed a grown
woman living in her childhood bedroom. Games, anime and manga rub off on her instantly, so she has
an eclectic set of skills that are unusually strong in very narrow areas: she cooks certain dishes
freakishly well, knows a great deal about cells and microbes, and has extensive knowledge of rice
cultivation. Her parents tried everything they could think of to get their daughter standing on her
own feet, but she had so little drive that they finally gave up on her, handed her a lump sum and
effectively cut ties.`

¶2 `She burned through that money too keeping up her NEET lifestyle, and just as she was starting to
think next month's living costs were looking seriously dicey, the Gremlin Disaster struck. Becoming
a witch turned Sahari's body into that of a ball-jointed doll. Overjoyed at the tremendous power she
had suddenly come into—my whole life just turned around in one shot, hooray!—she settled in to carry
on as a NEET exactly as before. The world's in a terrible mess, but the important people will sort
it out sooner or later, right? I can look after myself. Easy, easy…`

¶3 `While she was thinking that, the Bloodsucking Mage, who had heard rumors of a witch with golems
standing guard over her house, came calling. On the first day she turned him away at the door. On
the second he brought a rare trading card worth several hundred thousand yen, so she at least heard
him out through her closed front door. On the third he challenged her to a three-round hobby battle
and she lost all three. Losing a match she had been certain she would win left Sahari devastated. He
had known nothing whatever about otaku culture when they first met, and yet in a mere three days he
had surpassed a professional NEET—in one field, at least. Under their agreement the loser had to do
whatever the winner asked, and as Sahari waited in dread to hear what that would be, the Bloodsucking
Mage said: "Want to try saving the world with hobby battles?"`

¶4 `Sahari knew she had lost. Her soul admitted that he outranked her as an otaku. That is not an
invitation a non-otaku merely pretending to understand otaku could ever have come up with. And so
Sahari reluctantly went along with a fellow otaku's request, joined the Witches' Council, and began
doing her part to protect Tokyo. (She is not really protecting the public with hobby battles. She
just uses golems, in the ordinary way.) But she still hardly works. The world has changed and she
has become a witch, and a NEET is still a NEET.`

### Pass C record

Sent to Codex headless (`gpt-5.6-sol`, medium reasoning, text only, imagegen explicitly forbidden):
the verbatim Japanese by region including the full body, the proposed English, and the locked
constraints. It confirmed the `球体間接人形` → `球体関節人形` typo reading, and endorsed the `NEET`,
`子供部屋おばさん` and `自宅警備` decisions.

**Taken — the important one:**

- **The interior monologue was flattened.** `世界はエラい事になっちゃった、でも偉い人たちがそのうち
  なんとかするでしょ。自分の身は自分で守れるし、らくちん、らくちん……` is immediate direct thought,
  marked by the casual `～ちゃった` and the rhetorical `でしょ`. I had reported it in third-person
  past, which kills the voice the whole profile is written in. Restored as unmarked direct thought
  in present tense — which is also what project rule 1 requires. Kept roman, not italic, because the
  source does not mark it as discrete quoted thought. The same applies to `人生一発逆転万歳`.

**Taken — meaning and idiom:**

- `待機` — `It's standby` is not English. → `I'm on standby.`
- `見た目よりもずっとパワーがある` — my `There is far more power in her than she looks like she has`
  is cumbersome calque. → `She's much stronger than she looks.`
- `なかなか覚えられない` overstated as `take her forever`; it means she has difficulty, not that she
  gets there eventually after a long time.
- `菌` is broader than `bacteria`. → `microbes`.
- `手切れ金` — `settlement` implies a legal dispute; it is a final lump sum paid while severing ties.
- `食いつぶし` — `ate through` is calque. → `burned through`.
- `超パワー` — `super-strength` narrows it to the physical; the source says tremendous power
  generally, which matters because her power is magical.
- `三本勝負…全敗` — `best-of-three` contradicts losing every round, since a best-of-three ends after
  two. → `three-round hobby battle` … `lost all three`.
- `酷く打ちのめされた` — `badly crushed` reads physical. → `devastated`.
- `なんでも言う事を聞く約束` — not merely listening to the winner; the loser had to *do* whatever the
  winner asked.
- `～てみないか` — `Why don't you save the world` loses the tentative invitation. → `Want to try
  saving the world with hobby battles?`
- `世界が変わっても魔女になっても` — my `The world may have changed` makes an accomplished fact
  hypothetical. Both changes have happened.
- Plus `席を置き` → `joined`, `一端を担う` → `doing her part to protect Tokyo`, `そう考えていた
  ところに` → `While she was thinking that`, and `一枚何十万円もする` corrected to one card's worth
  rather than a per-card price.

**Overridden:**

- **The reviewer flagged every male pronoun for the Bloodsucking Mage as invented gender**, noting
  it only if not established elsewhere. It *is* established: `glossary.md` line 21 records
  `吸血の魔法使い | Bloodsucking Mage | Vampire Mage | Male`. The reviewer could not see that file.
  Male pronouns kept.
- `ほどほど` → the reviewer preferred `Moderate` over my `Middling` as less dismissive. Taken, but as
  a preference rather than a correction — `ほどほど` is genuinely offhand in the source.
- The reviewer's rewrite of `絶対に勝てると踏んだ勝負に負けた` dropped the match she had sized up as a
  certain win. Kept that clause: `Losing a match she had been certain she would win left Sahari
  devastated.`

## Notes and uncertainties

- **The source misprints `球体関節人形` as `球体間接人形`** — `間接` (indirect) for `関節` (joint).
  A ball-jointed doll is `球体関節人形`; `球体間接人形` is not a word. Treated as a typesetting error
  in the original booklet and translated as intended.
- The `知力` grade is set as a bold burgundy `C` followed by a lighter, smaller `＋`. The English
  render sets `C+` as a single unit, the treatment given to `B+` on `s-p006` and `A-` on `s-p008`.
- Everything else on the page was legible at zoom.
