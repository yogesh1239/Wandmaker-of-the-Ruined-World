## Layout overview

The wrap divides at exactly x1024. Left of it is the front cover; right of it is the back.

**The front panel is `s-h1`.** Not similar to it — the same file, scaled to 1024 × 1456. Measured:
resampling `s-h1.jpg` to the panel size and differencing gives MAD 1.37, which is JPEG and
resampling noise and nothing else. The column profile is flat at 2–3 across the whole panel, so no
back-panel element bleeds across the fold.

The back panel is the front's **mirror image** — the same design elements reflected, as though seen
through the paper: the same corner blocks, the same small Latin sub-title, the same round stamp,
each flipped horizontally, with a different wand as the illustration. One element is not mirrored:
the large magenta `secret` set in lowercase down the fold, rotated a quarter turn. It is cropped
hard at x1024 so its letters bleed off the fold.

## Layout decision — the front panel is not re-rendered

The panel is `s-h1` at 71.1 % scale, and `s-h1` has already been localized, QA'd and accepted. So
the shipped `s-h1.jpg` is resampled to 1024 × 1456 and placed into the wrap directly.

Re-rendering it would be worse on both counts that matter. It would cost a second, independent
interpretation of the same cover — the two would drift in word spacing, stamp texture and title
colour, and a reader who sees the jacket and the cover page in the same booklet would notice. And
it would put the accepted result at risk for no gain. The panel is a scaled copy; treating it as
one is the accurate thing to do, not a shortcut.

Only the back panel is sent to `imagegen`, cropped out at x1024–2048 so the front cannot be
disturbed. The crop is self-contained: the large `secret` starts exactly at x1024.

## Layout decision — un-mirroring

Both mirrored elements are set upright and left-to-right in English.

The mirroring is a real design idea — the back reads as the front's reflection — and it is not
being discarded lightly. But it only works while the mirrored strings are decoration. To a Japanese
reader, `Wandmaker of the Ruined World` reversed is a Latin ornament; the meaning was never being
read off it. In an English edition that same reversal is the reader's own title, printed backwards,
and it stops being a device and starts looking like a plate flipped in error.

The rest of the mirror survives untouched: the corner blocks stay in the top-right where the front's
sit top-left, the stamp stays on the right where the front's sits left, and the back wand is
unchanged. Only the two strings a reader would try to read are turned around.

Volume 3 made the same call — its back-cover sub-title ships upright — so this is also what keeps
the two jackets consistent on a shelf.

## Layout decision — the ghost stamp

The faded stamp is the front stamp's echo, so its English is the front's English: `SECRET`, one
word, set at the same slight angle, at the ghost's density rather than the front's.

The rect has to cover the glyph *and* the English, and horizontal `SECRET` is wider than the
mirrored `秘` is. So it spans x1405–2005, which sits inside the circle: the ragged outline at
x1355 and x2035 stays outside the rect and therefore comes through from source untouched, and the
arcs that do fall inside are rebuilt by the render.

Left edge 1405 is set by the back wand — its vines reach x1370 around y900–1100, and the rect must
clear them.

## Editing and refinement record

**Pass A — source and continuity.** Both strings check out against the source and the filed
references. The source's own Latin reads `Wandmaker` as one word; `novel.config.md:7` locks the
title as `Wand Maker of the Ruined World`, so the compound is split — the same correction `s-h1`
and Volume 3 both made. `秘` maps to `SECRET` per the glossary row added with `s-h1`; the large
lowercase `secret` is left alone because it is source English, not a translation. No honorifics, no
names, no register question arises on a jacket.

**Pass B — publication English.** Read as finished jacket copy. The sub-title's two-line break
falls at the natural phrase boundary and matches the front panel's break exactly, which matters
because both are visible at once when the jacket lies flat. `SECRET` alone is right for the stamp:
the title above already carries `Top Secret Files`, so a stamp reading `TOP SECRET` would repeat it.
