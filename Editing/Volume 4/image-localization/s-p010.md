### The missing name

**The page prints the furigana `きわだ　ようこ` but no name kanji.** This was checked rather than
assumed: the crop was converted to greyscale and contrast-stretched across its full range
(min 25, max 255) to rule out white or near-white type. Nothing is there — the space below the
furigana is blank paper.

Furigana annotate the kanji they sit beside, so the name belongs on this page and is missing from
it: a production defect in the original booklet, not a design choice. Accordingly the English page
**sets the name `KIWADA YOKO` in the name position and drops the furigana**, which is the inverse of
the rule applied on every other profile page but produces the result the source intended. Leaving
the page nameless, or setting a bare romanized reading with no name above it, would carry a printing
error into the English edition.

- **`体高` is not `身長`.** It is the standing height measured at the shoulder, the term used for
  horses and livestock — pointed, since she is half horse. The same distinction the Spider Witch's
  `全長` made on `s-p006`.
- **`姐御` and `姐さん` are two different words in one sentence.** `姐御` is the yakuza title for a
  boss's woman; `姐さん` is the everyday respectful address. The glossary already locks
  `木和田の姐さん` as `Kiwada-nee-san`, so the English keeps that romanized form for `姐さん` and
  renders `姐御` descriptively, rather than collapsing both into one word.
- **The furigana is dropped and the name set instead** — see the missing-name note above.

¶1 `The mistress of Arataki Kojiro, boss of the Arataki Group. Human from the waist up and horse
from the waist down—a centaur, in other words. Within the Arataki Group people call her the boss's
lady, or Kiwada-nee-san, and they fear her. She has a strong sadistic streak.`

¶2 `If she finds a kitten shivering by the roadside on a rainy day, she throws it into a swollen
river and enjoys seeing how many minutes it takes to sink. She starves her pet dog on purpose by
withholding its food, then sets it on a young underling who has messed up and turns the attack into
a spectacle. There is no shortage of stories about what a piece of trash she is.`

¶3 `When she was sixteen and working as a drug dealer, Arataki Kojiro—the Young Boss at the
time—helped her escape from the police, and that was how she became his mistress. Kojiro frequently
takes mistresses besides Kiwada, but *curiously*, every one of them becomes unreachable before
anyone notices. So when people speak of Kojiro's mistress, Kiwada is the only woman they mean. After
she became a witch her personality changed a little: she no longer kills babies or toddlers.`

### Pass C record

Sent to Codex headless (`gpt-5.6-sol`, medium reasoning, text only, imagegen explicitly forbidden):
the verbatim Japanese by region, the proposed English, and the locked constraints. The brief stated
that the profile's cruelty is the source's characterization of a villain and must not be softened;
the reviewer respected that and proposed no sanitizing, confirming `クズエピソードには事欠かない`
should keep its blunt condemnation. It also endorsed `Height at the withers` for `体高`,
`legs and hindquarters` for `足腰` on a centaur, `Kiwada-nee-san` for `姐さん`, and the italic
treatment of the emphasis-dotted `不思議`.

**Taken — invented content:**

- **A superlative I invented in the quote.** `子供の命乞いは可愛いねぇ！` contains no comparison; my
  `There's nothing cuter than a child begging for its life!` manufactured one. →
  `A child begging for its life is so cute!`
- **`飼い犬` is a pet dog, not a `guard dog`.** I had invented the guard role, which changes the
  picture: the point is that she starves an animal that depends on her.
- **`増水した川` is *a* swollen river, not *the*.** The definite article implies a river already
  introduced.
- **`たびたび` is `frequently`, not `all the time`**, which overstates it.

**Taken — meaning and idiom:**

- `姐御` — my `big sister` read it as ordinary kinship. It is the yakuza title for the boss's woman.
  → `the boss's lady`, which keeps it distinct from the romanized `Kiwada-nee-san` for `姐さん`.
- The dog anecdote was in the present perfect (`has set`), which frames it as one completed incident;
  the Japanese presents it as habitual. Moved to present tense.
- `乳幼児` covers infants *and* toddlers; my `infants` dropped half of it.
- `いつの間にか音信不通になる` — `falls out of contact at some point` is limp; the force is that each
  woman becomes unreachable before anyone notices.
- `虎次郎の愛人といえば木和田しかいない` — `Kojiro's mistress means Kiwada` is calque. It says that
  when people speak of his mistress, she is the only one they mean.
- `性格が変わり` → `personality`, not the calque `character`.
- `見た目通り` → `As her appearance suggests`, replacing the awkward `Exactly what she looks like`.
- `余裕があると` → `breathing room`; `room to spare` is not the English idiom here.
- `中遠距離` is one span, `medium to long range`, not two separate categories.
- `若衆` → `young underling`, which preserves the organizational relationship better than
  `young member`.
- Punctuation: spaced hyphens replaced with em dashes.

**Overridden:**

- Nothing. The reviewer proposed no change that conflicts with a locked decision.
