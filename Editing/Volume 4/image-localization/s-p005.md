## Terminology decisions

- **The furigana is dropped.** `いずみ　なぎさ` is a reading aid for Japanese readers; the Latin
  name already carries the pronunciation. Nothing is lost and a furigana line above English display
  type would look like a design error.
- **Family name first**, per the project convention: `IZUMI NAGISA`, set as display type in caps as
  on `s-p004`. The glossary spelling is unchanged; the caps are typographic.
- **The body calls her `渚` throughout** — given name alone. English uses `Nagisa`, matching the
  filed prose.
- **`一般人基準で` → `By ordinary-person standards`**, deliberately parallel to Fuyo's
  `By witch/mage standards` so a reader comparing the two pages sees the two different yardsticks.

**Body — four paragraphs**, broken at the source's own topic shifts (see the layout decision below).

¶1 `Secretary to the Foresight Mage. She is in love with him. Nagisa had just graduated from junior
college and started an office job at a company belonging to a major corporate group when the
gremlin disaster struck. She had holed herself up in her company apartment, but the moment she
stepped outside to see what was happening, a monster attacked her. She had resigned herself to
death when the Foresight Mage, who lived in the apartment next door, saved her life. From then on
they stayed together, and she settled naturally into the position of his secretary—or so she let
him believe. In reality, she worked very hard to get close to him.`

¶2 `Around the Foresight Mage she presents herself as a demure, composed adult woman, but she is
actually rather strong-willed. Her pursuit of him is gentle but determined, and she never backs
down. The Foresight Mage was reluctant at first because of their fifteen-year age gap, but lately
her persistence has begun to wear him down. Nagisa fully intends to win him over.`

¶3 `Nagisa is an only daughter whose parents had her later in life. They are entering old age now
and seem worried about their later years. They support her relationship with the Foresight Mage,
but clearly only because they expect special treatment from their future son-in-law and hope he
will care for them in old age, and Nagisa finds that deeply irritating. She understands why they
want peace of mind and safety in these unstable times, and she wants to be a good daughter too;
their support is far better than having them oppose the marriage. Still, she wishes they would at
least make some effort to keep up appearances. As it is, they look downright grasping.`

¶4 `The Foresight Mage is far more ordinary than people think—an ordinary man utterly worn down by
an extraordinary amount of self-sacrificing overwork. Her parents do not understand that. Nagisa
recently reached the end of her patience with their overly calculating attitude. They had a huge
shouting match in the middle of the night, loud enough to jolt every neighbor awake, and have been
estranged ever since. She has no intention of speaking to them until they change what they say to
the Foresight Mage and how they behave toward him. In sickness and in health, Nagisa will always be
the Foresight Mage's strongest ally.`

## Layout decision — the body is broken into paragraphs

Same reasoning as `s-p004`: the source runs this as one solid column, which reads as an even grey
texture in Japanese and as an unenterable wall in English. It is broken at the four topic shifts
already present in the Japanese — how she met him and what she lets him believe; what she is
actually like and how the courtship stands; her parents and why their support grates; and the
quarrel, closing on the marriage-vow echo. Nothing is added, reordered or cut.

## Editing and refinement record

**Pass A — source and continuity.** Every string read off the image at 2.5×–3×, since this page has
no text layer. Glossary forms locked: `Foresight Mage`, `Foresight`, `Izumi Nagisa`, and the four
stat labels and seven legend rows shared with `s-p004`. Both grade modifiers were read individually
(`C＋` carries the plus; `魔力` is the numeral `1`, not a letter grade).

**Pass B — publication English.** Read as a finished profile. Past tense for the completed
backstory, present for her current situation and temperament. The furigana was dropped as a reading
aid with no English function.

**Pass C — external review by Codex.** Raw Japanese and Pass B English sent region by region with
the locked constraints attached. Twenty-eight findings returned; the substantive ones are taken:

- `お見知りおきを` is `I hope you'll remember me` — a request to be remembered, not the
  first-meeting greeting `A pleasure to make your acquaintance` that Pass B had used.
- `大手系列企業` is a company **belonging to** a major corporate group, not the group itself.
- `社宅` is company housing — a company apartment, not a dormitory.
- `行動を共にする` is simply that they stayed together; `worked side by side` narrowed it, and the
  present-perfect broke the past-tense backstory.
- `かなり頑張った` is neutral effort; `went to considerable lengths` implied something dubious.
- `おしとやかで余裕のある大人の女性の顔をしている` is presenting herself as demure and composed;
  `wears the face of` was translationese.
- `アプローチ` is a romantic pursuit, and `決して後に引かない` says outright that she never backs
  down. `押され気味` is her persistence wearing him down, not him `losing ground` — the source is
  not adversarial. `押し切る` is winning him over, not the vaguer `seeing it through`.
- `安心と安全` is peace of mind and safety, not comfort and security.
- The parents' motive needed its object made explicit: the care they expect is care **for them**.
- `結婚を反対される` is having the marriage opposed, wider than `told not to marry`.
- `巷で思われている` is what people think; `the world imagines` was too grand. `やつれ果てている`
  is worn down, not the added physical image `worn to the bone`.
- `最近` was dropped from the loss of patience, and `過ぎる` from `打算的過ぎる`.
- `飛び起きる` is being jolted awake, not getting out of bed.
- `未来視への言動` covers both what they say **to** him and how they behave **toward** him; Pass B
  had reduced it to how they talk about him.
- Three spaced hyphens corrected to em dashes.

Confirmed rather than changed: `健やかな時も、病める時も` is the marriage vow, and the English fixed
form `in sickness and in health` is the right rendering even though it reverses the source's order —
the echo is the point of the sentence. `By ordinary-person standards` was confirmed as keeping the
contrast with Fuyo's witch/mage scale legible.

Nothing was overridden on this page; no Codex suggestion conflicted with a locked decision.
