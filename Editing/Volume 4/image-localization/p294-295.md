## Layout decision — the cover title

The source cover sets `崩壊世界の魔法杖職人` as two tall vertical columns: `崩壊世界の` down
the right edge (x755–850) and `魔法杖職人` down the left edge (x125–240), read right-to-left.
English has no vertical setting, and neither column band is wide enough (95 px and 115 px) to
hold horizontal type at any usable size.

**Decision:** clear both columns and set the English title as three stacked horizontal lines
in the right-hand band, right-aligned to the cover's right edge:

```
WAND MAKER
OF THE
RUINED WORLD
```

occupying x≈600–900, y≈85–340. That block sits on the cover's ornate window background and
clips only the right tip of the compass ornament; it clears both characters' faces, the wand,
and the boy's hair (which starts at y≈330).

Consequences, accepted:

- The left column band (x125–240, y435–1170) becomes pure reconstructed background. Behind it
  is the ornate window and dark floor; the girl's leg and boot begin at x≈240, just outside.
- The right column band below the new title (y340–790) likewise reconstructs as background.

This is the same accepted-deviation class as the p285 side columns, the p290-291 display line,
and the p292-293 incantation: where a vertical→horizontal relayout destroys the correspondence
between where the Japanese ink sits and where the English lands, the whole block comes from the
render and its artwork is reconstructed.

## Layout decision — the excerpt balloons

The excerpt panels are deliberately small, and their balloons are narrow and tall because they
were drawn for vertical Japanese. Horizontal English inside them must be set small and broken
over 3–4 short lines. English type here is **not** permitted to overflow a balloon outline —
text outside a balloon reads as narration and would misrepresent the page. Reducing size is the
correct trade, and matches how published English manga handles cramped excerpt panels.

`残念ながら` sits in a 95 × 145 oval; "Unfortunately." is set on two lines with a hyphenated
break, which is standard English manga practice in a balloon this narrow.

`!?` is punctuation and is language-neutral. It is left untouched — it is not in any merge rect.

## Layout decision — the SFX

The `パキィ` SFX runs as a vertical brush column down the left edge of the middle action panel
(x1425–1510, 85 px wide). English SFX cannot be set vertically, and 85 px will not hold a
horizontal word at SFX scale, so it is set horizontally across the same white impact-line burst,
turned through 90°.

The horizontal run available there is hard-bounded on both sides: the panel border at x1395 and
the flying figure's thigh and boot at x1523. That is 128 px. At the SFX scale the render settles
on — about 21 px per letter — five letters need 105 px placed perfectly, and two successive
renders placed `KRAKK` starting at x1434–1436 instead, running to x1537 and x1581 and biting into
the boot. Neither could be used: the boot is focal art and the merge rect cannot be widened to
cover it without handing it to the render.

Four letters need ~84 px, which fits from the same x1436 start with room to spare. So the SFX is
`KRAK`. This is a typesetting-driven shortening, which the pipeline normally forbids — it is
allowed here because the constraint is protecting focal art, not convenience, and because `KRAK`
and `KRAKK` are the same sound effect; only the length of the ring is lost.

## Editing and refinement record

### Pass A — source and continuity edit: complete

- `じゃあ喋らないでもらえます？` / `近寄らないで下さい` / `顔も見たくないです` — grepped to
  `Source/Volume 1/04 青の魔女.md:796` and replaced with the filed English at
  `English/Volume 1/Chapter 3 - The Blue Witch.md:529` verbatim, split at the source's own
  sentence boundaries so each balloon gets exactly one sentence. My first-pass draft
  ("So could you please not speak?") was discarded in favour of the filed wording.
- `いる` / `残念ながら` — grepped to `Source/Volume 1/08 東京魔女集会.md:73`, one novel line
  `「いる。残念ながら」` that the manga splits across two balloons. Filed English at
  `English/Volume 1/Chapter 7 - Tokyo Witches' Council.md:47` is "I'm here. Unfortunately."
  Split identically. Note this is a **trailing** `残念ながら` — the sentence-final adverb comes
  after the statement in both languages here, so the balloon order needs no swap.
- `大利さーん！` — `大利` is Ori (`glossary.md:6`), family name, `-san` retained (critical
  rule 3). The `ー` elongation is rendered `Ori-saaan!`, not `Ori-saan!` or `Oriii-san!`; the
  stretch belongs to the honorific's vowel, which is where the source puts it.
- `風都ノリ` — checked against critical rule 7. ふうと must not become *Fūto* (macron banned)
  or *Fuuto* (this novel leaves long vowels unmarked). **Futo**. Family name first: Futo Nori.
- `第①巻` — the circled numeral is a design element reused from the cover starburst; kept as
  `①` rather than flattened to "1" so the band and the cover still rhyme.
- `続々登場` is "appearing one after another", not "all together" — the English keeps the
  sequential sense: "ONE AFTER ANOTHER".
- `好評発売中` literally "on sale to good reception". English promo copy does not say this;
  "ON SALE NOW" carries the whole function. No fact lost.
- Verified nothing was invented: every English string above maps to a Japanese string in the
  verbatim tables, and `①`, `!?` and `Kadokawa Comics A` are explicitly marked unchanged.

### Pass B — publication-English refinement: complete

- `小説のあのシーンも／漫画で読める!!` — the literal "you can read those scenes from the novel
  in manga too" is flat. Rewritten as a two-beat display line, `THOSE SCENES FROM THE NOVEL—` /
  `NOW IN MANGA!!`, which keeps the source's line split, its relative line widths (line 1
  clearly wider), and its exclamatory close.
- `それぞれどのシーンかわかりますか？` — kept as a direct question in sentence case, matching
  the source's smaller, lighter pill type. "Can you tell which scene each one is?" reads as
  natural English teaser copy; "Do you know which scene each is?" is stiffer.
- `各キャラクターも続々登場!!` — "Each character also appears one after another!!" is a calque.
  `EVERY CHARACTER SHOWS UP, ONE AFTER ANOTHER!!` keeps the enumerative force and the single
  wide display line.
- `カドコミ『ヤングエースUP』にて連載中＆` — the trailing `＆` is a design element joining the
  two claims, so English keeps a trailing `&` rather than closing the line with a full stop.
- `コミックスもよろしくお願いいたします！` — the set phrase has no literal English. Read as a
  character speaking to camera, `Please give the manga your support too!` is the natural
  English equivalent and keeps the `も` ("too") that ties it back to the novel.
- Credit labels reread as a block: `Original Story:` / `Manga:` / `Character Design:` are

- Checked the four visual classes stay distinct: heavy display headers, balloon dialogue,

- Reread the whole page as finished English. No calques, no widow-prone strings, no repeated

## How the regions were measured

Same discipline as p290-291 and p292-293:

- **[detect]** `detect294.py` — eroded flood fill (`MinFilter(5)` on the `≥232` white mask
  before filling) found every balloon interior on the right page unambiguously. The erosion is
  the fix that seals hairline gaps in anti-aliased balloon outlines; a plain flood fill leaks
  into the page background.
- **[grid]** labelled coordinate grids at 1–3× for everything with no container: the two title
  columns, the cover credit block, all four band lines, both display headers, the open lettering
  at x1895–1990, and the `残念ながら` oval's ink.
- **[union]** every rect was then checked against where the *English* will land, not just where
  the Japanese ink sits. This matters most on the cover, where the title moves from two vertical
  edge columns to one horizontal block in the right band — the merge rect there is the union of
  the old right column and the new English block.

## Notes and uncertainties

- `風都` is read ふうと with high confidence but the reading is not printed with furigana on this
  page. If a furigana'd occurrence turns up later in the volume and contradicts it, `Futo Nori`
  is the string to revisit.
- The upper-row panel at x1155–1385 and the lower-row panel at x1155–1390 were each inspected
  at 2× and confirmed to carry no text.
- **Inventory miss, corrected.** The middle action panel (x1395–1610) was first passed over as
  text-free: at 2× its `パキィ` reads as impact lines rather than lettering, because the strokes
  are brush SFX drawn in the same weight and direction as the speed lines around them. The
  render's own inspection pass flagged it, and re-reading the panel at 5× confirmed the glyphs.
  The lesson this page adds: a panel is only clear of text once its **brush strokes** have been
  read at a scale where a handanten circle is legible — impact lines and SFX are drawn with the
  same tool, so a 2× glance cannot tell them apart. `!?` was correctly identified as punctuation
  at 2× only because it is set in type, not brush.
- `Kadokawa Comics A` is reproduced rather than replaced; it is the imprint's own English
  branding as printed on the Japanese cover.

## The measurement lesson this page added

Two, one of each kind.

**Reading, not just measuring.** My first inventory pass declared the middle action panel text-free
after a 2× look. At 2× its SFX is indistinguishable from the speed lines around it, because it is
drawn with the same brush at the same angle. A panel is only clear of text once its brush strokes
have been read at a scale where a handakuten circle is legible. Type-set marks like `!?` can be
classified at 2×; brush marks cannot.

**Pixel coordinates do not steer placement.** Three briefs gave `imagegen` explicit x-ranges for
the SFX; all three were ignored, and renders B and C landed within 2 px of each other at the wrong
place. What worked was landmark language ("hard against the panel's left border", "clear white
space before her thigh") plus removing the constraint at source by shortening the word. Coordinates
remain worth stating — they document intent and they drive the merge rects — but they cannot be
relied on as a placement mechanism.
