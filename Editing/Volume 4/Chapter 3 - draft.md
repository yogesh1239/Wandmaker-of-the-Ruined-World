It was one of those days when the clear blue sky just felt good.

Led by Sekitan, who had bitten my sock and pulled on it to get my attention, I went out for a walk to work off breakfast.

The dandelions blooming in the abandoned fields, which nobody tended anymore, had turned into white puffballs in the short time since I'd last seen them. The spring wind sent them drifting lightly away. Sekitan got distracted by the fluffy seeds floating around and nearly fell into the gutter at the side of the road.

“Mii...”

“You okay?”

“Mi.”

Sekitan carefully moved away from the gutter, which was full of mud and dead leaves, gave a short cry, and started leading me again.

It was unusual for laid-back Sekitan, who was always spacing out, to invite just me along without the other two. Despite that placid face, Sekitan was much more thoughtful and intelligent than it looked. This walk had to have some purpose too.

Sekitan kept wandering off whenever it got distracted by dandelion fluff, grasshoppers, or the sparkle of crystal-rain Gremlins submerged in puddles, but in the end, it led me to the waterwheel hut I'd set up on the Tama River floodplain.

The waterwheel I'd made from floorboards torn out of empty houses broke down often. Sometimes the rising water during the rainy season got to be too much for it, and the whole hut was washed away. Other times, mutant water plants that had reproduced out of control tangled around it and stopped it. Once, a creepy monster like a cross between a fish and a bird had made its nest there.

I couldn't mill the rice I harvested without the waterwheel, and I also used it to grind large amounts of Gremlins into powder all at once, pound mochi, and press oil.

I used it all the time, so its habit of breaking down was a real headache.

The waterwheel had stopped again today. As I scratched my head, wondering if it needed repairs again, Sekitan grabbed my shoelace in its mouth and pulled me over to show me the space between the water buckets and axle.

There was the charred corpse of a four-armed bear. It had to weigh 400 kg and was caught on the waterwheel, half-submerged in the river.

“Whoa...! I-It’s huge. Class C-1?”

“Meemi. Mimi.”

Only weak Class C monsters appeared in Okutama. But this weird bear looked like a pretty strong individual even among weak monsters. The Gremlin in its throat was big too.

As I poked at the burnt-smelling fur to inspect it, Sekitan climbed onto the corpse and kept breathing fire and wagging its tail right in front of me to show off.

“Hm? Did Sekitan take it down?”

“Mi!”

“...All by yourself?”

“Mi.”

“Nice one. You're growing and getting stronger too. Sekitan's strong. Good job!”

“Mimimii!”

I squished the cute pet that had shown me its kill between both hands and petted it all over. Sekitan cried meemi-meemi proudly.

It was small enough to fit in one hand, but it had taken down such a huge monster. Talk about a giant-killing.

Against a weird bear this muscular, with four arms, <ruby>![gaiji-0002.png](images/gaiji-0002.png)-<rt>shooting magic</rt></ruby> wouldn't be enough to kill it. You'd need <ruby>Do Vaa-ra<rt>ice-spear magic</rt></ruby>.

I didn't know if even I could have beaten it before I drastically reduced my magic power in exchange for implanting a fire salamander Gremlin. The fact that Sekitan had killed it without a scratch made it all the more reassuring.

After praising and spoiling Sekitan to my heart's content, I freed the monster corpse caught on the waterwheel and struggled to drag it up onto the riverbank.

When I stomped hard on the gravel by the river several times, Fuyo's roots, which had felt the vibrations, sprang out of the ground. I let the tips of the roots, which were wandering around as if searching for something, touch the burnt corpse. In no time, the corpse was dragged underground.

Having gotten new nutrients, the root bent flexibly one last time to make a heart shape for me, then burrowed back into the ground and disappeared.

In the month since she put down roots, Fuyo had spread them far. Just the other day, her roots had finally reached my house from the mountain behind it, but now she had the riverbank under her control too. It wouldn't be long before Fuyo's roots spread all over Okutama.

The only exception was around the reverberatory furnace, where she had not spread roots so she wouldn't provoke the fire salamanders. That was the truce between the witches' children.

She didn't seem unhappy with my mediation as the boss of my pack. Fuyo showed affection in ways only a monster would, saying things like, “When Uncle dies, I'll suck up every bit of you♡.”

Considering the possibility of resurrection magic, I kind of wanted her to wait and see for a while after I died. Well, she could do what she wanted. I wasn't interested in what happened after I died.

Compared with Fuyo, the fire salamanders' affection was easy to understand. They showed off the prey they'd killed like this and begged me to praise them. It was hard to believe creatures this adorable had been born from the sex-obsessed Flame Witch.

While I was petting Sekitan and thinking about this and that, it went all limp and fell asleep. I gently laid the pet, a snot bubble swelling from its nose, on the ashes in the waterwheel hut's brazier. Since I was here, I decided to do a little experiment.

The waterwheel hut had a homemade Graham condenser that used water pumped up by the waterwheel. Now that there was no running water, the waterwheel was very useful for semi-automating experiments like this too.

According to a paper by Professor Handa Sakunosuke of Tokyo Magic University's Department of Gremlin Engineering, natural Gremlins always contained impurities.

Crystal-rain Gremlins, made by cumulonimbus clouds and falling to the ground with the rain, took in dust and moisture from the air.

The Gremlins inside monsters had blood components mixed into them. That was why they came in so many colors.

The trace impurities in the Gremlin lowered the quality of the melt-recast Gremlin used in the backlash-prevention mechanism. It was neither uniform nor high quality.

So Professor Handa came up with a method for refining pure melt-recast Gremlin with no impurities.

Gremlins melted at the high temperature of 1,200°C. Raising the temperature further to 2,500°C brought them to their boiling point, and they evaporated. If you cooled the evaporated, gaseous Gremlin and returned it to a solid, you got pure Gremlin.

Putting the theory into practice was not as easy as explaining it in words. If you wanted the highest quality, you had to fill a space with noble gases and keep the Gremlin away from nitrogen, oxygen, and the other components of air, so that when it re-solidified after sublimating, it would not take in any material besides Gremlin. There were all sorts of other things too.

This stuff was seriously complicated and confusing. The paper went on and on about the technical barriers to Gremlin refining and how to solve them, as well as ideas for scaling it up from lab experiments to industrial production. I couldn't fully understand it without reading it about three times.

Professor Handa can't come up with a single idea for a new invention, but he can come up with endless ideas for improving existing inventions... Instead of being able to turn 0 into 1, he's way too good at turning 1 into 100.

He had instantly upgraded both the backlash-prevention mechanism I invented and the amulet. Professor Handa dutifully lists Wand Maker 0933 as a collaborator at the end of every paper, so I can't complain, though.

Still, having him outdo me as a Wand Maker, even if it was only in one field, pissed me off...

...No, it didn't piss me off, okay?

Hiyori often praised me, saying Ori was the best Wand Maker. If the strongest witch gave me her seal of approval, there could be no mistake.

I played around by poking Sekitan's snot bubble with a finger and popping it while I continued the Graham condenser's operating test. Eventually, I would use this thing to refine the blood components needed to make personal-color Gremlins, mix them with Professor Handa's refined melt-recast Gremlin, and make the highest-quality custom-made magic-school-customized wands. You could never run too many preliminary function tests.

Fortunately, the homemade Graham condenser was working well. It performed better than the Liebig condenser I'd swiped from the Okutama Middle School science room, and my homemade unfiltered sake was steadily distilling into high-proof alcohol.

I timed it with a wind-up pocket watch, calculated the processing efficiency per unit of time, and wrote it down in my notebook. Hmm. Not bad. I couldn't see any problems either.

“Mi...?”

As I checked that the condenser's thermometer was properly installed, Sekitan woke up and sniffed the air. It wriggled out of its brazier bed, climbed up the leg of the hut's workbench, and stared at the flask full of distilled alcohol with drool running from its mouth.

Crap. The smell of alcohol had caught the fire salamander's attention.

“Wait, Sekitan. That's not food. I'm in the middle of an experiment. Wait.”

“Mii...”

Sekitan looked up at me longingly, but when I shook my head, it lowered its tail and gave up.

So obedient. If it had been Mokutan or Tsubaki, they would've kept begging. Good job holding back!

Using fire really did draw the fire salamanders' attention. When I was doing experiments like this, it might be safer to have Hiyori keep an eye on them so they did not get into mischief. If I was going to ask Hiyori for help and take strict safety measures, I should suspend the experiments for a while.

Hiyori had been on edge lately and had not come to Okutama.

The Foresight Mage had warned her, “Cyanos will be stolen,” so she had holed up at home on high alert.

Apparently, she had left Okutama's security to Fuyo and avoided even seeing Professor Ohinata so that nobody would get dragged in if something happened.

Thanks to the warning and vigilance, the day Cyanos was originally supposed to be stolen passed safely without anything happening.

But the thief apparently had not given up. Instructions had come down to stay alert, and Hiyori could not move carelessly.

She had repeatedly told me never to come to Ome because it was dangerous, so I was taking it easy in safe Okutama, playing around with my pets and neighbors while working hard at experiments and wandmaking.

This really did seem pretty dangerous.

After all, without the Foresight Mage's warning, Cyanos would have been stolen.

Just the fact that they would've succeeded at stealing a magic wand from the renowned Blue Witch told me the culprit was no ordinary person.

I had heard the Flame Heir Witch had also had a magic wand stolen from her, but although she'd inherited the title of witch under special circumstances, she was human at base. She wasn't in the same league as the Blue Witch, who was the best of the best even among witches.

Who could the culprit be? That was ominous.

Personally, it was bad taste, but the story that Cyanos might get stolen had me excited. It was really bad taste, so I didn't say it out loud.

Sometimes, artworks shot up in value by being stolen.

The fact that somebody wanted them badly enough to resort to the crime of theft, plus the interesting history of having been stolen, added value. Picasso's paintings got stolen all the time.

“If it gets stolen once and recovered right away, think that could give it a nice little story? Right, Sekitan?”

“Mii?”

“That'd be outrage marketing, I guess. Faking it isn't right. Hiyori probably wouldn't like that either.”

“Mii.”

As I tickled under Sekitan's chin while it looked completely clueless, the eyeball familiar in my pocket suddenly shook.

Speak of the devil. It was an incoming call from Hiyori. Was she lonely for another human voice again?

When I pulled the eyeball familiar out of my pocket, it floated up to my eye level, snapped its pupil wide open, and blasted me with an ear-splitting yell.

“Ori! Are you okay!? Is anything wrong!?”

“Whoa!? Wh-What's with you all of a sudden? Everything was peaceful until you started yelling your head off.”

I covered my ear, its eardrum nearly blown out, and complained.

Look at Sekitan. Poor thing, it was so shocked that it had frozen in place, its eyes rolled back and its mind gone blank.

Hiyori, who was clearly acting strange, did not pay attention to my complaint and said,

“The Foresight Mage has issued an emergency declaration. He's unconscious. Tokyo is in danger!”

“Huh...!?”

Even though the message was put in clear, concise words, it did not reach my brain.

As the sudden news left me speechless, Hiyori went on in a rush.

“Until I have a grasp of what's happening—no, until I tell you the situation has settled, do not leave Okutama under any circumstances. Have Fuyo protect you! Got it!?”

Hiyori rattled it off in a more desperate voice than I'd ever heard from her.

I was speechless.

The last time the Foresight Mage had lost consciousness, the mushroom pandemic had nearly destroyed Tokyo.

Now, something about as serious as that was about to happen. Or it had already happened.

This is no time to be playing around with my pet in the waterwheel hut.

Tokyo is in serious trouble!!
