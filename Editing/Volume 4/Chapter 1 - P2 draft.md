My hospital stay, which had begun with appendicitis, ended after six days.

After getting my stitches out and being told what to watch out for while recovering at home, I went back to Okutama with Hiyori escorting me. Waiting to greet me were the furious fire salamanders.

“![gaiji-0003.png](images/gaiji-0003.png)ー!”

“Mi![gaiji-0003.png](images/gaiji-0003.png)!”

“Mimi![gaiji-0003.png](images/gaiji-0003.png)!”

Whether they'd heard our footsteps or sensed our magic power, somehow the three fire salamanders knew their boss was back and came all the way to the front door. They looked up at me and angrily meeped away.

Tsubaki was expressive to begin with, but Mokutan too, even laid-back Sekitan, were making rough, accusatory cries. Their displeasure came through as heat waves that prickled and singed my skin.

“Sorry, sorry. I was wrong to leave you guys all alone out of nowhere.”

“![gaiji-0003.png](images/gaiji-0003.png)!”

“But I had it rough too. I had surgery, surgery. Forgive me.”

“Meemi, mimimii!”

When I crouched down to pet them and get back into my pets' good graces, the three bit my fingers a little hard.

“Whoa. Ow...”

“Hey, they just bit you!? Are you okay?”

“I'm fine, I'm fine. If they were seriously pissed, they'd breathe fire. As long as they're biting me, they're holding back.”

Hiyori, overly worried about me just getting over an illness, grabbed my hand, pulled it close, and examined it, but I wasn't bleeding. Still, there were teeth marks, so their anger level was probably about medium. They were about as mad as when I rolled over while we were sunbathing and crushed all three under me at once.

As they body-slammed me and smacked me with their tails, I looked toward the entrance and noticed the bottom part of the door had been burned through.

...I've got a bad feeling about this.

When I unlocked the door and went inside, the house was, just as I'd expected, a complete wreck.

Apparently, they'd run short on food and turned over every shelf and every closet in the house looking for it.

The ash from the workshop furnace and the earthen-floor kitchen stove had all been raked out and scattered everywhere. The frying pans, pots, and kitchen knives had been half melted and tossed together in a corner. The fire extinguishers I'd kept one to a room had been thrown messily into the backyard, scratched all over like they had a personal grudge against them, then covered in dirt.

They'd done whatever the hell they wanted. There were even signs that they'd melted through the storehouse shutter with fire, broken in, and wandered around inside. Even Hiyori sighed at that.

“This is awful. I didn't think about the fire salamanders while Ori was away...”

“I don't think it would've changed even if you had. They don't like you. If you tried to take care of them, it probably would've backfired.”

It was still fresh in my mind how Hiyori, trying to feed them in my place, had somehow broken Tsubaki's favorite toy.

As long as the house hadn't burned down, this was good enough. If the Blue Witch, the fire salamanders' natural enemy, had tried fussing over them, our house might have been burnt ruins by now.

To keep from provoking the pets angry at being abandoned any further, I had Hiyori go into town to get new fire extinguishers and cooking gear while I started cleaning up the wrecked house.

I gathered the broken glass and pottery and the ash scattered all over the floor with a broom and dustpan, then threw it all out in the backyard. I replaced the burnt pillows and sheets too. I replaced the front door they'd burned a hole in with a same-size door borrowed from a nearby house. Then I recovered the hammer and carving knife that had been carried off to the reverberatory furnace nest.

The fire salamanders followed me around for a while, angrily leaking sparks from their mouths, but when I brought out a toothbrush and scrubbed their bodies, completely filthy with ash, dust, and dried mud, they wagged their tails and cheered up.

There, there. You cute little guys. Having to clean up a completely wrecked house right after getting over an illness was a headache, but the fire salamanders were still babies, not even a year old. I couldn't get mad at them. This was a trillion times better than them being sick or wasting away. They're healthy, and that's very good.

The one who wasn't healthy was me. The doctor had told me to avoid hard exercise and not push myself for a month.

Careful not to strain myself, I took breaks and somehow got just the bedroom cleaned up by the time the sun had set.

I told Hiyori, who had brought new fire extinguishers and cooking gear, that I wasn't seeing any visitors for a while. I also asked her to tell Professor Ohinata the same thing.

Playing with friends is fun, but it's tiring. Being alone is the easiest.

The two of them knew me well enough, and we settled it by agreeing that I would always carry an eyeball familiar for emergency contact.

After that, various things kept moving along even as I lazily cleaned up the house every day, sorted seed rice, and raised seedlings.

I killed time by using the letters the two of them sent me regularly as newspapers. I put the magic-wand business on hold for a while and spent the early spring taking it easy and recuperating.

Apparently, the new currency introduced at the start of the year was circulating smoothly.

There were some problems, like long lines forming to exchange ration tickets for new currency, or people continuing to try to use old currency even after it had been advertised to death, but it was mostly going well.

Even in the managed districts of witches who had little contact with the outside, like the Spider Witch, Zombie Witch, and Pebble Witch, the money economy had been accepted. An economic bloc had formed including the Tohoku Hunting Association and Hokkaido Magic Beast Farm.

In the Tohoku Hunting Association, trading caravans with mage guards were traveling back and forth along the overland route opened by Daidarabocchi's defeat. Work had begun on the badly cracked main roads overrun by plants, and apparently there was even talk of running freight cars on the rail lines someday.

As its name suggested, hunting was thriving in the Tohoku Hunting Association. Since many citizens had learned butchering techniques, they were far ahead of Tokyo not just in killing monsters, but in stripping materials from them and using those materials. Through trade, Tokyo was now able to benefit from that too.

Small amounts of local specialties that had barely survived from before the disaster, like beef tongue, zunda mochi, and branded Hitomebore rice, were being imported too. Goods and people were moving between the two regions more often, and currency was being put to great use as the lubricant for that exchange.

After the Kraken disappeared, the Pacific-side coastal route to the Hokkaido Magic Beast Farm opened up, and aquatic magic beasts towing ships brought more traffic to the Hokkaido-Sendai-Tokyo trade route.

There were still very few aquatic magic beasts that could tow ships, and they were valuable, so trade trips weren't that frequent. Ships were often held up by bad weather or aquatic Class A monsters showing up.

Hokkaido's specialty went without saying: domesticated monsters—magic beasts. Not only the magic beasts themselves, but cheese and yogurt made from magic-beast milk (too much gives you diarrhea) were being traded at high prices too.

There were also plenty of foods still being produced from before the disaster, like salmon jerky, dried scallop adductor muscles, and kelp.

I'd heard coal from the Ishikari Coalfield was being sold cheaply as an import from Hokkaido, so I placed an order to have 100 kg sent on the next trade ship. It cost 20,000 yen (the equivalent of 200,000 yen in old currency), but I made plenty of money, so it didn't hurt my wallet much. I've gotta feed the fire salamanders good food.

Meanwhile, the remaining two large survivor communities outside the economic bloc, the Lake Biwa Pact and the Arataki Group, were making unsettling moves. Unfortunately, the Blue Witch's unsettling prediction of the future from whenever it was had not been far off, even if it had not been exactly right.

The Lake Biwa Pact had undergone a political upheaval after the mushroom pandemic and was in internal turmoil.

But around the beginning of the year, the Arataki Group, based in Kyushu, launched a blitz attack on the Lake Biwa Pact and took it over.

The Lake Biwa Pact was swallowed up by the Arataki Group and ceased to exist.

When the Dragon Witch visited Lake Biwa as an envoy from the Tokyo Witches' Council around the end of February, she was welcomed on the surface. But apparently she said, “I feel like they were probing me. It smelled like something bad was going on.”

Unsettling.

But the Dragon Witch was the way she was, so she wasn't a very reliable source of information.

From what I'd heard secondhand, the Arataki Group sounded kind of like an antisocial organization. More specifically, yakuza.[^1]

In this world that had collapsed in the Gremlin Disaster, violence did the talking. Maybe because Japanese people tended to avoid confrontation, the Tokyo Witches' Council had loosely held together despite all kinds of incidents, and it had law and order. Tohoku and Hokkaido were the same.

But the Arataki Group was post-apocalyptic in the bad way: openly violent.

They attacked and took over another survivor community. Scary.

Of course, there was only one source of information, and it was not reliable, so I didn't know how much of the story was true.

They said it was an attack and takeover, but maybe they had actually reluctantly stepped in over the Lake Biwa Pact's outrageous infighting and saved citizens suffering because of the internal conflict! Something like that. Even if they were yakuza, I hoped they were at least chivalrous yakuza.

For the Witches' Council, its own problems mattered more than problems in faraway regions it barely dealt with.

For example, schooling had finally resumed on April 4, just last week, and had already created a mountain of problems.

For now, they had restored six years of elementary education as compulsory education, but for example, children who had been new first graders (six years old) at the time of the Gremlin Disaster had turned 11 without receiving a proper education.

They couldn't exactly say, “You're sixth-grade age, so start from sixth grade!”

But they couldn't mix 11-year-old children in with six-year-old children and treat them together as new first graders either. Their bodies and intellectual development were at completely different stages.

The way they handled this differed by school district. Some districts had too few teachers, so they were forcing children of completely different ages together. Some grouped children only up to a year apart, while others placed children in grades for their age and tried to make it work by giving them extra remedial lessons. There were all kinds of approaches.

But then complaints came pouring out: “Why is so-and-so-chan doing this and that, but I'm different?”

The grade issue alone was troublesome, but then there were problem after problem: children getting attacked by monsters on the way to school, children unfamiliar with walking to and from school in groups getting completely lost, huge crowds of parents worried about their children coming to pick them up from school, and major fights at school where kids fired off magic and injured people. This and that, one problem after another.

Seriously, just reading the softened-up episodes in the letters wore me out.

Endlessly annoying.

It was scary to think they had decided to reopen education only after taking care of the major problems first.

Even after narrowing the subjects down to four—Japanese, arithmetic, science, and moral education (including health)—they barely had enough teachers. You could see the desperate effort in things like mixing social-studies content into Japanese and arts-and-crafts content into science.

It really showed how blessed Japanese education had been before the Gremlin Disaster. There had only been peaceful problems, like Monster Parents[^2] and suspicious people breaking in. Now there were actual monsters, so in rougher districts, instead of Monster Parents, man-eating monsters might show up in the schoolyard. Not funny.

I was really glad I belonged to the generation that had finished all its schooling in a peaceful era.

It sounded hard enough just hearing about it as someone else's problem, so the people actually dealing with it had to be in complete chaos. My condolences~.

The education issue wasn't the only thing giving the Witches' Council trouble. Monster problems had been another headache over the past few months.

The hunt for Daidarabocchi around the end of last year was still fresh in everyone's memory.

Mysterious black Gremlins like the one Daidarabocchi had possessed had begun appearing here and there among Class A monsters and some Class B monsters.

Around the time of the Daidarabocchi hunt, powerful monsters had started becoming even more powerful.

Mainly, Class A monsters had started using time-acceleration magic.

A mysterious black Gremlin that turned to dust and disappeared over time was always recovered from the corpses of monsters that used time-acceleration magic.

Class A monsters were powerful enough already. Only witches or mages could defeat them.

The fact that those Class A monsters had started gaining a troublesome new ability was extremely serious.

At present, Class A monsters with black Gremlins made up about one-fifth of the total, and their time acceleration only lasted a few seconds. The witches could still deal with the monsters.

But the frequency of monsters with black Gremlins appearing was gradually rising.

There had also been several cases where battle-hardened witches were caught off guard by suddenly accelerated monsters and injured.

There was no knowing what would happen if more monsters with black Gremlins appeared from here on and awakened to new abilities. At worst, the witches' and wizards' ability to respond could be overwhelmed, and Tokyo being trampled by a horde of powerful monsters was not something I could say was impossible.

Five years after the Gremlin Disaster.

Why were monsters only now starting to gain new abilities? And the same troublesome ability at that?

Apparently, the research budget for the university's Department of Monster Studies had been increased this academic year to investigate that mystery.

The Department of Combat Studies had increased its recruitment too, and the Department of Gremlin Engineering was apparently researching a Gremlin grenade that used sharp Gremlins against Class A monsters.

Okutama was a peaceful region where even the strongest monsters were only Class C-1, and the Lost Mist was always spread over it. It was one of Tokyo's safest areas, but hearing all this made it hard to let my guard down. Monsters getting stronger had a hundred harms and not one benefit.

Now then.

I had been recuperating while reading all kinds of suspicious news in letters, but May and rice-planting time were approaching. The period when I had to rest had passed, so I took the fire salamanders with me and went for a walk to get my body used to moving again.

Rice planting was hard work. I had to get back in shape before then.

The fire salamanders understood the pack hierarchy well, so when we went for a walk, they never walked in front of me. They toddled along behind me.

Tsubaki, who had the biggest attitude, chased grasshoppers and caught them with its front paws. Curious Mokutan was busy chasing butterflies and trying to breathe fire on dandelion fluff. Laid-back Sekitan tended to fall behind, so Tsubaki often poked Sekitan's tail to hurry it along.

The Lost Mist spread across all of Okutama, so I couldn't enjoy the distant view of the spring mountains turning green. Still, walking familiar roads was a nice change of pace.

I felt good too. I even tried jogging a little, but my stomach did not hurt. Looks like I'm fully recovered.

I'm back! Ori Kenshi is back!

After walking to the edge of Okutama, I turned on my heel and was about to head home feeling good, but I thought I heard a voice from beyond the mist and stopped.

Thinking I might have misheard, I listened carefully, and definitely heard a voice.

“...-cle... ...ncle...”

It was a human voice.

And it belonged to a young girl.

I was confused. People aren't supposed to come near here, are they?

No, wait. Right.

Compulsory education had restarted, and the letters said children walking to and from school for the first time in their lives were often getting lost.

Ah-ha. I got it.

Some kid must have come all the way out here along the railway.

It could happen.

After all, when I was in elementary school, I once wandered all the way into the next city on foot. I felt like if I just walked straight ahead, I'd get somewhere I recognized. Back then, the police who came looking for me after my parents reported me missing helped me out.

Even if it's a lost child, they're still a person. I don't exactly want to go meet them.

But the Lost Mist is magical. Anyone who steps into it could end up wandering around until they starve to death.

“Uncle! Uncle! Unclllllle! ...Where are you? Uncle!”

The girl was anxiously calling for her uncle.

U-Um. I wanted to pretend I hadn't heard her and go home.

But if I ignored her here, she'd probably keep wandering until she died.

Even I can't do that.

I thought about calling Hiyori and having her handle it, but I couldn't call her over something this stupid. This is on a completely different level from a dragon showing up or getting sick.

I took several full minutes to work up the courage to meet a stranger. Just in case, I picked up Mokutan and Sekitan, one in each hand, to use as flamethrowers and headed toward the voice.

All I had to do was guide the lost child to the Ome Line tracks and tell her which way led toward central Tokyo. I didn't care whether she could make it home. I couldn't take responsibility that far.

What emerged from the mist was a young girl, just as the voice had suggested.

She was about as tall as a young elementary schooler. She had a cute, childish face, long green hair, and an elegant red flower ornament in her hair. A soft floral fragrance that somehow smelled both familiar and unfamiliar came from the girl as she looked around anxiously through the mist.

As I moved closer to speak to her, the girl turned around and noticed me.

And I noticed too.

What this girl was wearing on her lower half wasn't a skirt.

It was leaves and petals.

This girl's upper body was human, but her lower body was a plant!

“Ah! It's Uncle! I finally found you!”

The girl's anxious expression completely changed. Her face lit up, and she happily ran over to me.

I was so surprised my legs almost gave out.

Whaaaaaaaaaaaaaat!?

You're the Flower Witch's daughter plant!?

Why are you here!?
