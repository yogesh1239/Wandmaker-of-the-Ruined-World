### Sumida Witch

The witch who governed Sumida Ward was ninety years old, making her the oldest witch in the world. Once a bedridden old woman with advanced dementia, becoming a witch straightened both her back and her mind. She was sensible and cooperated with the Tokyo Witches’ Council, but felt she had already struggled enough and enjoyed a full life. In the end, she hoped to pass away quickly and peacefully.

She used sealing magic that trapped monsters and other things inside Gremlins. She died in the mushroom pandemic.

### Hachioji Witch

The witch who governed Hachioji City had been a casual fangirl of the Blue Witch—Aoyama Hiyori—since Hiyori’s days as a magazine model. She loved fashion in general and made a hobby of dressing stylishly.

Her chain magic boasted powerful restraint and exceptional durability. She herself was sturdy and valued as both a shield and support fighter. She died in the mushroom pandemic.

### Kanoya Tatsumi

A ferret-loving furry and professor in the Department of Mutation Studies at Tokyo Magic University. He had perfect pitch.

He contributed in many ways, passing the Koganei Witch’s death-curse magic from memory to the Department of Magic Linguistics and pioneering research into the mutation mechanism by which humans become Transcendents. After trying to reproduce Ohinata Kei’s beast-person transformation, he concluded that it could not be replicated and was genuinely crushed.

He died in the mushroom pandemic.

### Yomiike Village

A tiny mountain village once called Yomi Pond Village.

The highland pond that supplied its water was believed to connect to the land of the dead. For generations, the spellcaster who managed the water source also served as village head. During the Meiji era, an order requiring commoners to adopt surnames led the village head to take the name Ikegami. The pond’s ominous name was also replaced with the homophonous characters for Yomiike.

### Water Eater

A catfish-like monster that guzzles water and excretes golden droppings. It uses magic that infuses its cry with magic power, awakening aversion and the instinct to flee in anyone who approaches. Because this magic inherently interferes with magic-power control, it has no effect on creatures unable to control magic power.

To the Water Eater, humans are like neon signs in a city. They may catch its attention, but it does not go out of its way to attack them and feels neither affection nor malice.

### Ice from Great Glacier Magic

The magical ice created by the Blue Witch’s signature Great Glacier magic does not melt naturally. As far as anyone has confirmed, only fire magic can melt it.

The ice is extremely hard and difficult to carve out. Once a block is separated from the original glacier, it begins melting naturally, so plans to use it as refrigerator ice were abandoned.

### Giant Kaiju Gremlin

Of the two 80 mm giant Gremlins obtained from the giant kaiju, the one donated to the university is undergoing nondestructive testing.

If technology continues to advance smoothly for several more years, the giant Gremlin can be used for better processing and more valuable research. More advanced investigative techniques could produce deeper, more detailed data. If it is processed now, however, it can never be returned to rough stone. Data obtainable only from the unworked material might be lost forever.
