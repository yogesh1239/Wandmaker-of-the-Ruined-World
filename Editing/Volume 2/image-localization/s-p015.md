### Header

Volume 2 Chapter Commentary

### 01 — Let’s Learn Useful Magic

The author loves building bases. In government-management and survival games, I am always arranging production systems, fortifying the house, and organizing the item boxes. In short, this chapter is about raising the base’s security level.

### 02 — I Saw a UMA Deep in the Okutama Mountains!

A friendly Ori-and-Blue-Witch event. This is roughly what their days as “friends” look like.

The chapter explores the Blue Witch’s love of cute creatures while also digging further into monsters. Her perception of the brave, majestic-looking white wolf as cute is one of the few internal changes caused by her mutation.

### 03 — The Tobacco Witch

This chapter brings up the Tobacco Witch, who lives fairly nearby yet has never appeared in person. She has a great deal of background lore, but because she has no reason to interact directly with Ori, she rarely becomes involved in the main story.

It would be strange for the reclusive Ori to become involved with every witch whose name has appeared. Some witches are simply people he has heard about but never met. That is common in reality, and the author believes it gives a story a comfortable sense of depth.

### 04 — Melt-Recast Gremlin

One of the author’s principles is to explain things with as few foundational concepts as possible. That may sound absurd in a novel packed with this much lore, but I mean it sincerely.

Almost every magic item in this novel ultimately comes from magic stones. Magic stones produce Gremlins; processing and using those Gremlins in many different ways produces a wide variety of magic items. New materials do appear, but trace them back and they come from Gremlins, which in turn come from magic stones. Taken to an extreme, the novel has only one foundational concept: “magic stones.” Everything branches from there.

The melt-recast Gremlin was another idea born as an extension of that original magic-stone concept.

### 05 — Backlash-Prevention Mechanism

It is satisfying when magic items have many different functions. A powerful weapon cannot merely have high attack power. Equipment cannot evolve merely by increasing its attack power.

It should be light, easy to handle, easy to maintain, hard to break, low-risk to use, and capable of sustained operation. It must be judged from many angles and excel in every one of them.

The backlash-prevention mechanism is both useful and interesting because it adds an entirely new field to a wand’s catalog specifications.
