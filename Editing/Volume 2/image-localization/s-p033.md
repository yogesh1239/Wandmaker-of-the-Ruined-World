## Exact English

### Body

stood no chance of winning.

Holding down a headache, she asked what the woman had actually come for. The Tobacco Witch pulled a sheet of paper out of thin air like a piece of sleight of hand (or rather, it probably was sleight of hand—she seemed to have picked up another little party trick), then launched into her explanation with a smug look on her face.

“Ta-daa! And w-w-would you believe it, this spring the very first T-1 Grand Prix has been officially scheduled! The winner’s prize is a voucher for three nights and three days at a luxury hot-spring inn! All-you-can-drink: yes! Yakiniku: yes! Please do enjoy the full yes-and-yes deluxe package. Solo comics and comedy duos alike, no qualifications required, so come one and all—that’s what it says.”

“...In other words, a comedy event.”

Reading the promotional flyer, it seemed the Tobacco Witch had put together yet another event.

One month on from the mushroom pandemic. The mood of mourning for the dead had not begun to lift anywhere. None of that solemn atmosphere touched the Tobacco Witch in the slightest.

“What is this T-1 short for?”

“That’d be ‘Tanoshii Number One.’ Any comic with fun material is welcome!”

“Not Tokyo, or tobacco...?”

“Don’t you fuss over the fine print. So, what do you say? I really do think it’d be an absolute riot if you entered, Ao-chan.”

“I’m not built for that.”

She shook her head and refused, but the Tobacco Witch forced the flyer into the Blue Witch’s pocket anyway.

“The material doesn’t have to be funny. Just the situation of the cool-beauty Blue Witch-sama turning up at a comedy contest is hilarious all by itself.”

“I am absolutely not entering.”

The Blue Witch scowled and raised her voice, and the Tobacco Witch howled with laughter.

Laughed at if she entered, laughed at if she refused—however she struggled, she was in the palm of the woman’s hand.

Humoring her would take all day. The Blue Witch jabbed her in the side with the tip of Cyanos to drive her off, but the Tobacco Witch seemed to notice something and suddenly brought her face in close.

When she leaned away, wondering what now, the witch dropped the cigarette from her mouth at her feet, ground it out underfoot, twitched her nose, and started looking her up and down.

“W-What. Your business here is done, isn’t it. Go home already.”

“Say. Did you change your perfume, by any chance?”

The words did not follow the conversation at all, a complete non sequitur, and yet they were as sharp as a blade.

The Blue Witch stiffened with a jolt, and the follow-up strike landed.

“You’re doing skin care, aren’t you. And you used to skip it constantly, coasting on that invincible youth of yours.”
