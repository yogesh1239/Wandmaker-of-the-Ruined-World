## Exact English

### Running head

SHORT STORY

### Story title

The Big Sis Next Door

### Body

The people of Tokyo were tough.

You could argue that only the tough ones had survived, but either way, even after the mushroom pandemic people got straight back on their feet and started walking, eyes fixed on the future.

The Blue Witch was no different. Tokyo had already come through no end of hardship, and the mushroom pandemic had done even worse damage on top of it, but she refused to buckle and worked at getting back to ordinary life.

Waking up in a quiet house in the morning. Washing her face, combing her hair, tidying the grave in the back garden, a little skin care, then breakfast. The same old routine was what made an ordinary day feel ordinary.

Looking back, maybe the tide had turned the day she met that intruder from Okutama.

The Gremlin Disaster, the Iruma coup—the Blue Witch had beaten her enemies and come through both, and lost every single person who mattered to her.

But ever since she met Ori, she had been beating her enemies and keeping what mattered as well.

She had brought down a giant kaiju with a weapon Ori built, and Ori hadn’t died.

She had beaten the kidnapping dragon into the ground, and Ori was alive and the picture of health.

She had come through the mushroom pandemic, and neither Ori nor Ohinata had died.

The pandemic in particular hadn’t followed her usual pattern of wrestling an enemy down for the sake of her own people. That time one of her own—Ori—had saved her life.

After all that, telling her not to be conscious of that blockhead was asking too much.

He drifted through life so vaguely, so precariously, and he was so hopelessly socially awkward—and yet he was this dependable, and above all, being with him left her warm inside.

The Blue Witch was picking absently at her breakfast, a warmth in her chest greater than the balmy spring sunlight slanting in through the window, when she caught a large magic power approaching the house and her face went straight.

Not a monster. A magic power she knew very well.

She picked up Cyanos just in case and headed for the entrance. The doorbell rang, and a husky woman’s voice came through it.

“Excuuuse me! We were playing and our baseball went into your gaaarden! Mind if I come in and grab it?!”

“Go home.”

She hurled the words through the door, cold, and got nothing back but delighted laughter. It was exhausting.

Last time, this witch had turned up crowing, “I’m here to inspect the fire alarm.” No matter how often she was told to stop barging in over garbage errands, she made up a new excuse and came anyway. There was no dealing with her.
